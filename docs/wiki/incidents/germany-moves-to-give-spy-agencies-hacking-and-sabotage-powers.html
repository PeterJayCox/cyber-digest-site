Germany Moves to Give Spy Agencies Hacking and Sabotage Powers

Germany's cabinet approved legislation that would let its intelligence agencies hack foreign systems, sabotage adversaries' supply chains and feed false information to extremists inside Germany — described as the biggest overhaul of the country's spy laws of the postwar era. The proposal marks a dramatic expansion of German offensive intelligence powers beyond traditional surveillance.

Summary

The cabinet-approved overhaul moves German intelligence beyond surveillance into active offensive action — hacking, supply-chain sabotage and domestic counter-disinformation. It is part of a coordinated expansion of the offensive envelope across Western and allied states, together with the US private-sector offensive-cyber memo.

Impact

  • Expansion of German offensive intelligence powers beyond surveillance
  • Active hacking and supply-chain sabotage authority for spy agencies
  • New authority to influence in-country extremists via disinformation
  • Part of a wider Western/allied trend toward more aggressive, active offensive-posture cyber diplomacy
