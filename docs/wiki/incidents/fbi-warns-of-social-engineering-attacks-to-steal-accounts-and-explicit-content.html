FBI Warns of Social-Engineering Attacks to Steal Accounts and Explicit Content

The FBI issued a public alert on a surge of social-engineering attacks in which hackers breach victims' accounts — often via SIM-swapping and phished recovery flows — to steal explicit photos and videos and extort victims.

Key Facts

Attribute Detail
Authority FBI (public alert)
Method Social engineering, SIM-swapping, phished account-recovery flows
Target Personal accounts; explicit content as extortion leverage
Guidance Account-recovery hardening, MFA, caution with recovery flows

Context

The advisory is a reminder that credential-theft campaigns now target the personal-content layer — not just financial assets — and that account-recovery flows have become a primary attack surface. The same social-engineering tradecraft applies to enterprise accounts, where attackers abuse helpdesk and recovery processes to bypass MFA. For AU/NZ readers it parallels ACSC guidance on account compromise and reminds organisations to treat recovery-process abuse as an MFA bypass vector, not just a consumer problem.
