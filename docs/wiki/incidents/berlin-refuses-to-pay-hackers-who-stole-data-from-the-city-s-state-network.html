Berlin Refuses to Pay Hackers Who Stole Data From the City's State Network

Berlin's state government confirmed it is the target of an extortion attempt following the August 2026 compromise of the city's state administrative network, and said it will not meet the extortionists' demands. Forensic work identified further data outflows in the portfolio of the Senate Department for Mobility, Transport, Climate Protection and the Environment, with exfiltration dated between 7 and 12 August; the department first reported an outflow on 7 August and was cut from the network on 14 August.

Berlin has published no official figure for what left the network — the attackers' leak site, indexed 28 August, claims 5.79 TB of data including personal information on 12,076 individuals. Der Spiegel named Rhysida as the group behind the attack, citing its darknet leak site and security sources, while the official statement identifies no group. Governing Mayor Kai Wegner said "the state of Berlin is being blackmailed". The case is a notable example of a government refusing to pay after a confirmed state-network compromise.

Escalation — 2026-09-14: the dataset was published after the refusal

Berlin's state government has confirmed that Rhysida published the stolen dataset on the dark web after the Senate refused the extortion demand, reported as a minimum bid of 30 bitcoin, roughly €2 million or US$2.3 million. Infosecurity Magazine reports the group released the entire 5.7 TB dataset, described elsewhere as about 1.44 million files, with the material reported to include sensitive state disaster-response planning documents; German reporting puts the record count at roughly 1.4 million. The publication landed less than a month before the city-state's elections on 20 September 2026.

Berlin has still published no figure of its own for how much data left the network, so the volumetric detail — file counts, terabytes, and the claim of personal information on 12,076 individuals — remains the extortion group's assertion rather than the state's assessment. The extortion cycle is now complete: refusal to pay produced full publication, on a date of the attacker's choosing, which is the outcome the earlier entries in this page anticipated.
