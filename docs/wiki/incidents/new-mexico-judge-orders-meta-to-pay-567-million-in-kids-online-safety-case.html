New Mexico Judge Orders Meta to Pay $567 Million in Kids Online Safety Case

A New Mexico judge ordered Meta to pay $567 million — including $420 million for youth mental-health treatment — and overhaul how minors use its platforms, in the first of dozens of state AG suits against Meta.

Key Details

  • Source: The Record
  • Date: 2026-08-07
  • Reliability: Tier 2/4 — Established cyber journalism
  • Nature: Landmark social-media liability / online-safety litigation

Summary

The ruling labelled Meta a 'public nuisance', restricting push notifications between 10pm–7am and limiting youth use to 90 hours/month for New Mexico users. Meta said it would appeal.

Analysis

The case is a bellwether for online-safety and platform-liability litigation globally. It signals to Australian regulators tracking online-safety and data-privacy litigation trends (relevant to Privacy Act reforms and the eSafety Commissioner's mandate) and to New Zealand's Harmful Digital Communications Act and digital-age-harm debates.
