Zoom Screen-Share Hijack Flaws Found by AI Tool in Under 20 Prompts

Researchers disclosed vulnerabilities in Zoom's screen-sharing functionality that could have allowed a malicious participant on a call to take over a target's device. A public AI tool found the dangerous flaw in fewer than 20 prompts — a striking demonstration of AI-accelerated vulnerability discovery in mainstream collaboration software.

Key Facts

Attribute Detail
Product Zoom video conferencing (screen sharing)
Impact Device takeover from a malicious call participant
Discovery Public AI tool, under 20 prompts
Remediation Zoom has addressed the issues
Disclosure date 2026-08-12

Context

The disclosure lands squarely in a week of AI–cyber convergence: AI models are finding bugs faster (this case), while state actors are weaponising them (see First Near Autonomous Ai Attack Documented On Taiwanese Government Target). For AU/NZ organisations, Zoom and similar collaboration platforms are ubiquitous in government, finance, education and healthcare — patching to the latest client is the practical control, and the incident reinforces the argument for treating AI-assisted vulnerability discovery as the new baseline threat to software supply security.
