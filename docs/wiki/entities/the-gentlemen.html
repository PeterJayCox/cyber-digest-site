The Gentlemen

The Gentlemen (also styled "TheGentlemen") is a ransomware group first observed in September 2025. Still relatively new, it has already demonstrated an unusually high operational tempo and the ability to launch advanced, tailored attacks against enterprise systems — in NCC Group's October 2025 assessment, The Gentlemen was singled out as a "newcomer" operating at a tempo "suggestive of experienced operators", distinct from the many recycled-data groups flooding the 2025 ransomware landscape. (Security Reports/2025/NCCGroup-Cyber-Threat-Intelligence-Report-2025.md) (Security Reports/2026/Guidepoint-Ransomware-Annual_Report-2026.md)

Emergence and TTPs

  • First observed: September 2025.
  • By the end of October 2025, the group had made 21 public ransomware attack claims targeting organisations across Asia, South America, Europe and Africa. (Security Reports/2025/NCCGroup-Cyber-Threat-Intelligence-Report-2025.md)
  • Trend Micro highlighted The Gentlemen for its advanced, tailored attacks on enterprise systems, profiling the group after a September attack investigation. (Trend Micro, "Unmasking the Gentlemen Ransomware", 2025)(https://www.trendmicro.com/en_us/research/25/i/unmasking-the-gentlemen-ransomware.html)
  • Operates a Data Leak Site (DLS) listing attack claims with countdown timers and threats to publish exfiltrated data — consistent with double-extortion ransomware activity. (Security Reports/2025/NCCGroup-Cyber-Threat-Intelligence-Report-2025.md)
  • Claimed sectors span healthcare, financial services, IT and consumer discretionary. (Security Reports/2025/NCCGroup-Cyber-Threat-Intelligence-Report-2025.md)

Scale and escalation

  • By the end of October 2025, 21 public victim claims. (Security Reports/2025/NCCGroup-Cyber-Threat-Intelligence-Report-2025.md)
  • The group's claimed victim count had escalated to 332+ by early 2026, per the August 2026 cyber digest's weekly ransomware trend. This figure is self-claimed and not independently verified. (raw/digests/Cyber-Digest-2026-08-12)
  • CYFIRMA reporting lists The Gentlemen as one of the top ransomware groups active in South-East Asia (22 incidents in the collated dataset), behind only the regionally dominant Qilin. (Philippines Threat Assessment/COLLATED_INTELLIGENCE.md)

Confidence in claims: legitimate data, not recycling

NCC Group's review of The Gentlemen's public claims found no overlap with other groups' claims between 2021 and 2025 — an important differentiator from groups such as Funksec and Babuk that make high-volume claims assessed to be recycled breach data. NCC assessed the claim against 2GO (a large Philippines-based logistics company) as likely genuine, noting 2GO had no prior record of being breached. The group therefore appears to be generating fresh breach data rather than relabelling older compromises. (Security Reports/2025/NCCGroup-Cyber-Threat-Intelligence-Report-2025.md)

Notable incidents

AnMed (US healthcare) — August 2026

The Gentlemen claimed responsibility in the August 2026 disruption of AnMed, a nonprofit health system serving Upstate South Carolina and Northeast Georgia. During the fallout the group hijacked AnMed's Facebook page, posting ransom demands that claimed exfiltration (or 6 terabytes) including records relating to sexual assault, mental health, abortion and sexual harassment — claims for which no evidence had been provided. The incident stems from the malware disruption AnMed first disclosed on 26 July 2026. (raw/digests/Cyber-Digest-2026-08-12) See Anmed Closes Almost 80 Facilities Amid Cyberattack.

Philippine Savings Bank (PSBank) — 2 August 2026

A ThreatMon alert (reported by Undercode News) claimed The Gentlemen added Philippine Savings Bank to its victim list. This is a low-confidence, unconfirmed lead — no independent evidence of compromise or public confirmation by the bank. It should not be reported as a confirmed breach. (Philippines Threat Assessment/RESEARCH_UPDATE_2026-08-03.md)

Australian angle

The Gentlemen has a demonstrated regional footprint (South-East Asia and the Philippines, incl. the 2GO logistics breach and the PSBank listing). No confirmed Australian victim was identified at the time this page was created, but the group's active targeting of the healthcare, finance and logistics sectors overlaps closely with APRA/ASIC-regulated industries in AU. Australian organisations should treat The Gentlemen like any credible double-extortion RaaS and apply core ransomware defences (patched, internet-exposed estate; backup isolates; the Acsc "Don't Pay" posture).
