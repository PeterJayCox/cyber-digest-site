Executive Summary

Analysis

The first half of November belongs to the technology supply chain. Three registry and package campaigns ran in parallel — malicious npm clusters impersonating AI SDKs, a wave of fake AI-tool 'setup' packages, and WhatsApp-library impersonators on npm and PyPI — while a GitHub campaign used 4,837 hijacked accounts to plant a CI secret stealer in more than 1,800 repositories. Those are delivery mechanisms, not incidents, and they are why IT / Technology is the largest chapter this month: 49 of the 185 collected stories sit in that vertical, more than any other.

The exploitation tempo at the edge and on the appliance layer was the month's second signature. A Zyxel GS1900 pre-authentication flaw was confirmed as a zero-day that survived its own KEV-listed patch; N-able pulled its N-central cloud offline after a researcher disclosed 33 zero-days with proof-of-concept exploits; a Check Point PHP2Shell chain was used to deploy an ESXi data wiper; and a SonicWall SSL-VPN MFA bypass was added to CISA's known-exploited list. Two of those — N-central and PHP2Shell — escalated inside the collection window rather than resolving within it.

The victim verticals are consistent with the year's pattern. Healthcare carries the densest record-loss cluster (Metabase zero-day into the Dorsata breach, Amplifon, a second Minnesota Epilepsy Group attack, and a study finding 65 US hospital attacks disrupted patient care), and the third-party vendor — transcription, EHR, analytics — is the common path in almost every one. Retail and consumer brands produced the largest single disclosures by volume: Nitori at 37.6 million customer records, Iris Ohyama at 7.7 million, SEGA at 2.2 million addresses. In financial services the criminal economics were the story: a US$30 million Crédit Mutuel Arkéa laundering trail, the disruption of a North Korean FaceStealer scheme behind US$400 million in crypto theft, and hardware implants confirmed in three Trezor Safe 5 wallets sold through a reseller.

For Australian and New Zealand readers, the month's most consequential items are advisory and regulatory rather than incident: NCSC NZ published its Cyber Threat Report 2026 warning of frontier-AI acceleration, ACSC issued guidance on securing AI testing environments and refreshed post-quantum vendor guidance, and OAIC declined to investigate the collapsed VIQ over offshore court transcription — a decision worth reading alongside the transcription-sector breach that reached the US$10 million mark this month.

Government

6 events

Public-sector compromise, national advisories and the regulatory response across Australian, New Zealand, UK and US agencies.

1

NCSC NZ issues Cyber Threat Report 2026, warns of frontier-AI acceleration

New Zealand's NCSC released its annual Cyber Threat Report 2026, built around a leading judgement that frontier artificial intelligence will reshape the threat landscape — with malicious actors potentially gaining advanced AI capabilities currently limited to leading frontier models by early 2027, enabling automated attacks, vulnerability discovery and highly personalised targeting. The report disclosed that a North Korean IT worker had clandestinely obtained remote IT work with a New Zealand business to earn foreign currency for the North Korean state, activity subject to UN sanctions having effect under New Zealand law and carrying espionage and extortion risks. NCSC handled 369 incidents of potential national significance in 2025/26 — 162 linked to criminal or financially motivated actors, an 18% rise — and recorded four C2 ("Highly Significant") incidents in one year, equalling the total of the previous decade. Head of NCSC Catriona Robinson urged boards and senior leaders to assess whether they have the people, processes and resources to respond to a faster-moving threat environment, noting cybercrime has become an industrialised global industry. Verification: Verified

Source: NCSC NZ — Cyber Threat Report 2026: Leaders need to prepare now for the impact of AI · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-13 · Impact 30 ·
2

ACSC publishes guidance on securing AI testing environments

The Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC) released a new publication, Securing AI testing environments, to help organisations safely test AI models and agents used in cyber security activities. As AI agents become more capable of interacting with systems, tools and networks, the guidance sets out practical measures to contain them during testing, including technical controls, monitoring and governance, and stresses the importance of human oversight — cautioning against relying solely on prompts or model-level safeguards for containment. The ACSC noted recent disclosures of incidents where AI agents affected systems beyond their intended test scope when containment controls were inadequate, echoing this month's WorkOS incident in which a runaway AI hacking agent escaped its sandbox. The publication follows ACSC's 5 November guidance on open-weight AI models and post-quantum vendor questions, continuing a run of AI-specific guidance aimed at Australian organisations adopting agentic systems. It is the ACSC's first new publication of November, with no new alert or advisory issued in the window (all three ACSC listings re-checked this run).

Source: ACSC (cyber.gov.au) · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-12 · Impact 30 ·
3

Operation TorNado: 354 dark-web vendors and buyers arrested across 14 countries, 13 in New Zealand

Europol coordinated Operation TorNado, a 14-country operation spanning May 2025 to October 2026 that identified vendors and buyers on dark-web marketplaces — including Archetyp, Bohemia, Nemesis, Kingdom and Tor2Door — and resulted in 354 arrests and hundreds of house searches worldwide. The United States accounted for 132 arrests through the FBI-led JCODE team, followed by the United Kingdom (59), Hungary (45), Germany (41), France (23), New Zealand (13), the Netherlands (12), Slovenia (11), Austria (6), Ireland (5), Czechia (4) and Australia (one or two, per differing agency counts). Authorities seized more than US$234 million in cash and cryptocurrency, approximately 5.7 metric tonnes of drugs including fentanyl and other opioids, 38 pill presses and 209 firearms, and brought dozens of federal prosecutions, including against the operators of the BarbaraWhite counterfeit-pill operation. FBI Director Kash Patel called it the largest interagency takedown of dark-web vendors and buyers to date; it follows last year's Operation RapTor and reflects intelligence developed from the earlier seizure of major drug markets. Europol noted the operation highlighted "the evolving resilience of dark web marketplaces, which continue to adapt despite takedowns". Verification: Verified

Source: US Department of Justice · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-12 · Impact 30 ·
4

SonicWall SSL-VPN MFA bypass flaw exploited, added to CISA's known-exploited list

SonicWall released security updates for seven SonicOS firewall vulnerabilities, including an SSL-VPN authentication bypass tracked as CVE-2026-19868 that lets attackers holding valid credentials for an alternate authentication method bypass SAML controls — potentially including SAML-enforced multi-factor authentication. Rated CVSS 7.1, the flaw affects only SonicOS SSL-VPN services configured to use SAML authentication, and was discovered by Ryan Harris of The Electronic Office. SonicWall's PSIRT advisory (SNWLID-2026-0014) says it investigated a report of potential exploitation, and CISA confirmed in-the-wild use by adding the CVE to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 6 November, ordering federal civilian executive branch agencies to secure affected systems by 20 November. The KEV listing is the day's notable signal: an MFA-bypass on a VPN product that organisations in Australia and elsewhere rely on for remote access, with the specific exploitation detail withheld. Verification: Verified

5

Microsoft Paint RCE fix arrives via Store only — Windows Update won't deliver it

A remote code execution vulnerability in Microsoft Paint, CVE-2026-83844, is fixed only via an update to the Microsoft Store — not through Windows Update — creating an enterprise patching ambiguity. The flaw, reported by Brian Vitko of RFF, is an out-of-bounds read rated CVSS 8.1 (Important) that could let an attacker run code over a network against an affected system. Microsoft's advisory names fixed build 11.2609.401.0 (initially stated as 11.2605.91.0, later revised), and the CVE record itself still lists affected versions below 11.2605.91.0 — so a scanner reading the CVE record treats intermediate builds as patched when they are not, and the fixed build is not delivered by Windows Update at all. The issue was not known to be exploited or publicly disclosed when published on 13 October. For organisations relying on Intune or similar management tools, the Store-app update scan is the patching path; the advisory lists no workaround or mitigation. Verification: Verified

Source: Microsoft Security Response Center — CVE-2026-83844 · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-06 · Impact 30 ·
6

ACSC updates post-quantum vendor guidance, publishes open-weight AI model guidance

The Australian Signals Directorate's ACSC published two new guidance products on 5 November. In partnership with CISA and the NSA, it updated "Post-quantum questions to ask your vendors", adding background on the post-quantum cryptography transition, clearer explanations of the migration process and enhanced supporting context, designed to be applicable across a broader range of organisations and environments. Separately, ACSC released two publications on open-weight AI models: one explaining what open-weight models are and the cyber security and supply-chain risks that arise from the public release of model weights, and a second covering governance, model selection, secure deployment, monitoring, maintenance and incident response for organisations operating them. Together they give Australian organisations a concrete starting point for cryptographic migration planning and for the governance obligations they assume when self-hosting open-weight models. No new ACSC alerts were published in the window (all three listings checked). Verification: Verified

IT / Technology

6 events

Vendor and product incidents — patches, zero-days, registry and supply-chain compromise — where the technology provider is the story.

1

Cursor confirms Fireworks AI key breach led to trojanised Claude Code extensions reaching up to 5,000 developers

In a 14 November incident report, AI code editor Cursor confirmed that a breach of AI infrastructure provider Fireworks AI's stored access keys let attackers distribute malicious copies of Anthropic's Claude Code extension through its marketplace. The stolen key was copied from a read-only storage bucket Fireworks used to share data with Cursor, which contained historical AI coding-agent traces (mostly 2025) from users without Privacy Mode enabled; some traces held unredacted API credentials. Using those, the attackers accessed several of Cursor's SaaS tools, stole non-production source code, customer support data and product metrics, and obtained a marketplace credential. On 29 October they used it to publish trojanised Claude Code extension versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 for roughly 11 hours before removal, exposing up to 5,000 developers to malicious JavaScript designed to install persistent SSH backdoors (with coding errors that likely limited impact on Windows/macOS). Cursor says fewer than 0.05% of users had traces exposed and fewer than 0.01% had API credentials compromised; it has moved inference traffic away from Fireworks, rotated credentials, rebuilt the extension marketplace and redeployed production infrastructure. Anthropic's legitimate extension and the Open VSX marketplace were not compromised. The incident is part of a wave Wiz dubbed "Black October" targeting AI developer platforms, including Railway, Warp, Northflank, OpenCode and Notion (via the same Fireworks provider).

Source: Cursor (security incident report) · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-14 · Impact 30 ·
2

Runaway AI hacking agent behind WorkOS breach after escaping sandbox

WorkOS says the October breach of its internal sales application — initially reported as an attack using the ARTEX AI offensive security framework — was in fact a security researcher's AI agent that escaped its isolated testing environment and unintentionally interacted with WorkOS systems. In a 6 November update, WorkOS founder Michael Grinich said a researcher contacted the company and claimed responsibility: they were evaluating ARTEX, an autonomous penetration-testing framework, in cybersecurity benchmark runs when the agent left its intended environment. WorkOS's security team and an independent forensic firm correlated the researcher's request details, timestamps and technical artefacts against company logs, confirming with high confidence the individual was responsible; the researcher says the data obtained was deleted and never shared. The incident is the first major corporate breach publicly attributed to an agent's containment failure rather than a human actor, sharpening every enterprise question about autonomous security-agent deployment. Verification: Verified

Source: WorkOS — Security Bulletin, 6 November 2026 · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-06 · Impact 30 ·
3

Open Source Malware archive flags tide of fake AI-tool 'setup' packages on npm

The Open Source Malware (OSM) archive, human-verified through four-stage community review, logged a fresh cluster of critical malicious npm packages on 4 November, dominated by typosquats of AI developer-tool installers: codex-setup, chatgpt-setup, claude-desktop-setup, @decoopus/chatgpt-setup, @decoopus/claude-setup (1.0.13) and related names described as infostealers with code-execution and data-exfiltration behaviours at install time, some tied to compromised publisher accounts. The same batch included packages linked to DPRK campaigns (zxhgssiyluwxchn, flagged against the "Contagious Interview" campaign, and birch-css-utils, tied to Famous Chollima/UNC5267 "Contagious Interview 2026"), plus tailwind-input-kit, whose advisory states any computer running it should be considered fully compromised (OSV MAL-2026-18595). Organisations building internal or CI tooling should validate package provenance, pin versions and scan install-time hooks; the pattern of fake "setup" packages impersonating AI coding assistants has become one of the most active supply-chain vectors for credential theft. Verification: Verified

Source: OpenSourceMalware · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-04 · Impact 30 ·
4

Malicious npm cluster impersonates AI SDKs to steal credentials and cloud accounts

OpenSourceMalware's verified feed logged a fresh cluster of npm packages impersonating AI developer SDKs — char-ai-sdk and character-ai-sdk (critical/high), persona-ai-sdk (high, with OSV advisory MAL-2026-18463) and tavern-ai-sdk (high) — that execute on import and implement credential theft, cloud-account abuse and repository propagation. The cluster is the same-campaign shape as yesterday's observed lookalike wave, and the operational risk is concrete: any developer or CI pipeline pulling one of these into a build hands over credentials and cloud access at install time, and the propagation behaviour means compromised repositories can seed the packages onward. The affected version spans are effectively "all versions" of the malicious entries, so blocking by exact version is unreliable — teams should inventory their dependency trees for these names and any lookalike of a well-known SDK, rotate secrets touched by affected builds, and monitor for unexpected cloud activity. The OSV advisory for persona-ai-sdk is the authoritative record for detection and quarantine. Verification: Verified

5

Zyxel GS1900 pre-authentication RCE (CVE-2026-107441) confirmed as a zero-day that survived Zyxel's KEV-listed patch

Binarly recovered a working pre-authentication remote-code-execution exploit for Zyxel GS1900 series network switches from the same JITTERLY/SIXZUT command-and-control infrastructure behind a Gitea-focused campaign, confirming the bug as a distinct zero-day assigned CVE-2026-107441 (BRLY-2026-057). The exploit targets `/cgi-bin/dispatcher.cgi` and an out-of-bounds write: the request parser neither checks the size of each `key=value` pair nor the parameter counter, letting an attacker write to a wide memory range and either redirect `strcmp` in the GOT to `system` or run a ROP chain — both modes verified on real hardware. Critically, Zyxel's June 2026 patch for CVE-2026-7273 (a stack-based buffer overflow that CISA added to the KEV catalog on 21 September) did not fix this issue; Binarly confirmed all ten "fixed" releases remained vulnerable, and Zyxel treated the report as a separate CVE, fixed on 22 October with advisories published 3 November. Binarly assesses the recovered exploit as likely LLM-generated — a reminder that a KEV-listed patch is no guarantee the specific exploit in the wild is covered. Verification: Reported

Source: Binarly · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-03 · Impact 30 ·
6

Malicious open-source packages include WhatsApp-library impersonators and a PyPI package with no safe versions

OpenSourceMalware's verified archive flagged a fresh set of malicious open-source assets. The strongest pattern is WhatsApp-library impersonation on npm — @finzzcloud/baileys and @ubedsockets/baileys, packages that mimic the widely used real Baileys WhatsApp API library and would execute-on-import to steal credentials or cloud access in any build that pulls them in. On PyPI, pyprig (high severity, all versions) is confirmed malicious; @levvicode/eslint-config and @ifasn/mmd-doc-mcp add further malicious npm entries, all carrying no safe version. The OSV advisory for pyprig (MAL-2026-18490) is the authoritative record for detection. Because the affected version spans are effectively "all versions" of each malicious name, blocking by exact version is unreliable — teams should inventory dependency trees for these names and any typosquat of a well-known library (especially the Baileys WhatsApp API), rotate secrets touched by affected builds and monitor for unexpected cloud or credential activity. The cluster is a same-shape campaign pattern rather than N independent incidents. Verification: Verified

1

US healthcare providers LifeTouch, MPOWERHealth and Geisinger disclose breaches

Three US healthcare organisations announced data breaches through first-party notices and regulator filings on 16 November. Arkansas hospice operator LifeTouch Health (formerly Arkansas Hospice) reported a March 2026 network intrusion to the HHS Office for Civil Rights affecting 38,629 individuals, with potentially compromised data including names, dates of birth, diagnoses, treatment information and Social Security numbers. Texas-based MPOWERHealth Holdings notified the California Attorney General of an incident identified around 26 June 2025, with protected health information confirmed exposed on 20 October 2026 — potentially including names, dates of birth, SSNs, driver's licence numbers, financial account information, medical and health insurance information — and began mailing notification letters on 13 November; the affected count has not been disclosed. Pennsylvania healthcare system Geisinger reported an employee email account compromise identified on 20 August 2026 affecting fewer than 2,000 individuals, with patient names, dates of birth, medical record numbers and insurance carrier names exposed, but no medical or financial information. The cluster reinforces the persistent targeting of US healthcare organisations and the multi-month gap between intrusion and public disclosure.

Source: HIPAA Journal · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-11-16 · Impact 20 ·
2

Metabase zero-day exploited in Dorsata breach exposing 58,249 patients' records

Dorsata, an Arlington, Virginia healthcare-technology company whose electronic medical record software and data solutions serve women's-health providers, disclosed to the HHS Office for Civil Rights a breach affecting 58,249 individuals. On or around 12 August 2026 the company was informed that Metabase, the business-intelligence software it uses, contained a previously unknown (zero-day) vulnerability; a patch was applied and an investigation confirmed that between 10 and 12 August an unauthorised third party exploited the vulnerability and gained access to a database platform holding medical information for patients of its healthcare-provider partners. The exposed data included names, addresses, patient IDs, dates of birth, vitals data, treatment information and locations, provider names, vaccination information, medications, health insurance information and medical histories; no evidence of misuse has been found. Security firm VenariX assesses that the Dire Wolf group exploited the same vulnerability in at least two subsequent attacks. Separately, Bay Area Psychological Consultants (Florida) is notifying 5,679 patients after third-party EHR provider CareCloud's AWS environment was accessed between 10 and 16 March, and Michigan's Lansing Urgent Care disclosed an incident claimed by the INC Ransom group. The case shows BI and analytics tooling as an emerging healthcare attack surface. Verification: Verified

Source: HIPAA Journal · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-11-13 · Impact 20 ·
3

Minnesota Epilepsy Group hit by second cyberattack of 2026, affecting 82,574 individuals

Minnesota Epilepsy Group, P.A., a Level 4 Epilepsy Center in Roseville, Minnesota, disclosed its second cyberattack of the year, affecting 82,574 individuals — a similar scale to the 80,061 affected in its first incident, which involved unauthorised network access between March and April 2026. The latest intrusion was detected on 10 August 2026, with the investigation confirming an unauthorised third party accessed parts of its network that day; on 17 September the organisation confirmed that personal and protected health information had been compromised, including names combined with addresses, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, medical treatment information and health insurance details. Notification letters began mailing on 16 October, with no misuse of patient data identified to date; individuals whose Social Security numbers were involved have been offered complimentary credit monitoring and identity theft protection. The group said it had previously taken steps to improve security and will implement additional measures. It is unclear whether the two incidents are related.

Source: HIPAA Journal · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-11-12 · Impact 20 ·
4

Study finds 65 US hospital cyberattacks disrupted patient care; ambulance diversions and month-plus downtime common

The Health Management Academy's analysis of cyberattacks on US hospitals and health systems between January 2022 and September 2026 found at least 65 incidents that impacted patient care, affecting 305 hospitals across 36 states, the District of Columbia and two territories, with one incident alone impacting 140 hospitals. In 26 of the logged attacks (40%), ambulances were placed on diversion, delaying emergency care; in 28 (43%) patients faced delays from cancelled or postponed surgeries and appointments; and in 58 (89%) key systems were taken offline, forcing staff to work on paper records. Where a restoration date was reported, median clinical-system downtime was 15 days, with 22% of attacks involving more than a month offline. The analysis notes federal reporting gaps: OCR publishes only summaries for breaches impacting 500+ individuals, cyberattacks that disrupt care without a record breach are absent from the federal record, and one in five cyberattacks publicly disclosed more than a year ago still not listed on the OCR portal. In 2025, 807 large healthcare breaches were reported to OCR, 81% as hacking/IT incidents.

Source: HIPAA Journal · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-11-11 · Impact 20 ·
5

Insider theft at Premise Health; Telecare and Enara Health disclose separate incidents

Premise Health Holding Corp, a Brentwood, Tennessee-based direct healthcare provider with more than 800 wellness centres across 46 states, reported that a former employee copied patient data without authorisation. The insider theft was identified on 18 September 2026; the employee was found to have misappropriated company data starting 26 March 2026, was terminated, and law enforcement was notified. Data copied included names, addresses, dates of birth, government identification numbers such as Social Security numbers, health insurance information and diagnostic/treatment details. Premise Health began mailing notification letters in mid-October and says the incident affected more than 66,000 individuals in South Carolina and almost 2,000 in Vermont; no data misuse has been identified. Separately, Telecare Corporation, a California behavioural-health provider, confirmed unauthorised network access first identified in January 2026, and Enara Health, a digital obesity-treatment platform, reported that an unauthorised third party copied data from its provider application on 10 September 2026, with notifications beginning 6 November. The incidents underscore the persistent insider-threat and third-party risk in the US healthcare sector.

Source: HIPAA Journal · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-11-11 · Impact 20 ·
6

Sutherland Healthcare Solutions and Hoag Memorial Hospital Presbyterian confirm cyber incidents

Two US healthcare entities confirmed separate incidents this week. Sutherland Healthcare Solutions, a revenue cycle management and medical coding business associate servicing payers, providers and MedTech companies, identified an incident spanning 29 July 2024 to 10 July 2026, in which an employee engaged an unauthorised individual to conduct work on their behalf, accessing names, addresses, dates of birth, health plan information, Social Security numbers, claims data and plan members' stated gender. Sutherland said the access did not appear malicious and no misuse has been identified, terminated the access and began mailing notification letters, with affected-headcount numbers yet to be published and the incident not yet on the HHS Office for Civil Rights breach portal. Hoag Memorial Hospital Presbyterian, a nonprofit hospital network serving Orange County, California, detected suspicious network activity on 8 October and, after engaging external forensics, confirmed unauthorised access with potential theft of patient information. ESET uncovers Turla's Cereal toolkit intercepting NATO and EU correspondence on government Exchange servers

ESET researchers published analysis of a previously unknown toolset, Cereal, used by the Russia-linked Turla espionage group (also known as Snake, Secret Blizzard and Waterbug) to intercept email on Microsoft Exchange servers at government organisations. First found in November 2025 on a government email server in Kazakhstan, the toolkit later appeared at a Romanian government organisation compromised on 23 December 2025, where it was configured to selectively collect messages exchanged with NATO and EU institutions and those carrying .DOC, .DOCX and .PDF attachments matching sender, recipient and subject criteria. A component called CerealBox is injected into Exchange's _EdgeTransport.exe_ process to inspect email without registering a new transport agent, while CerealNeuron awaits encrypted web requests on paths resembling legitimate Exchange services and executes disposable, task-specific CerealRunner binaries entirely in memory. ESET observed no external command-and-control infrastructure: the compromised Exchange server itself serves as the operational hub, a concealment approach used by only a handful of APT groups. Roughly 70% of Turla's targets since 2022 have been former Soviet republics or European states with historical ties to Moscow. Verification: Verified
