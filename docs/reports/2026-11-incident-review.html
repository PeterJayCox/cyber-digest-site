Executive Summary

Analysis

The first half of November belongs to the vendor and platform layer. IT / Technology, reviewed as its own vertical from this edition, carries 53 of the 200 stories collected and leads on consequence as well as volume: the PHP2Shell chain ended in an ESXi wiper at Check Point gateway customers, a key stolen from Fireworks AI's storage bucket carried trojanised Claude Code extensions to up to 5,000 developers, N-able pulled its N-central cloud offline after 33 disclosed zero-days and was exploited within hours of publication, and 4,837 hijacked GitHub accounts planted a CI secrets stealer in more than 1,800 repositories.

Exploitation at the edge and appliance layer was the month's second signature, and the recurring lesson is that a KEV-listed patch is not proof of coverage. Zyxel's June fix left all ten 'fixed' GS1900 releases vulnerable to a separate pre-authentication flaw; SonicWall's SSL-VPN authentication bypass joined CISA's known-exploited list with a 20 November federal deadline; and the PHP2Shell chain moved from initial access to destructive payload inside the collection window rather than resolving within it.

The victim verticals follow the year's pattern. Healthcare's densest exposure came through analytics and intermediaries - Dorsata's Metabase zero-day exposed 58,249 patients, Amplifon notified 86,831 people, and a second Minnesota Epilepsy Group incident affected 82,574 - while consumer brands produced the largest disclosures by volume, with Nitori at 37.6 million records, Iris Ohyama at 7.7 million and SEGA at 2.2 million addresses. Transport's lead is a mail platform: Maileva confirmed 22 million people's names and postal addresses were exposed. In financial services the story is criminal economics rather than bank intrusion - a US$133 million heist from Credit Mutuel Arkea, US$400 million taken by North Korean FaceStealer operators, and hardware implants confirmed in three Trezor wallets sold by an authorised reseller.

For Australian and New Zealand readers the most consequential items are advisory and regulatory. ACSC published guidance on containing AI agents during testing and refreshed its post-quantum vendor questions; NCSC NZ's Cyber Threat Report 2026 warns that frontier AI may hand attackers new capability by early 2027 and records four 'highly significant' incidents in a single year. The OAIC declined to investigate the collapsed VIQ over offshore court transcription despite finding a reasonable basis for concern. Two items to watch rather than judge: Coles is investigating an incident with no evidence yet of data loss, and the Videostrong botnet Google dismantled had already reached Australian Android TV boxes.

Government

6 events

Public-sector compromise, national advisories and the regulatory response across Australian, New Zealand, UK and US agencies.

1

SonicWall SSL-VPN MFA bypass flaw exploited, added to CISA's known-exploited list

SonicWall released security updates for seven SonicOS firewall vulnerabilities, including an SSL-VPN authentication bypass tracked as CVE-2026-19868 that lets attackers holding valid credentials for an alternate authentication method bypass SAML controls — potentially including SAML-enforced multi-factor authentication. Rated CVSS 7.1, the flaw affects only SonicOS SSL-VPN services configured to use SAML authentication, and was discovered by Ryan Harris of The Electronic Office. SonicWall's PSIRT advisory (SNWLID-2026-0014) says it investigated a report of potential exploitation, and CISA confirmed in-the-wild use by adding the CVE to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 6 November, ordering federal civilian executive branch agencies to secure affected systems by 20 November. The KEV listing is the day's notable signal: an MFA-bypass on a VPN product that organisations in Australia and elsewhere rely on for remote access, with the specific exploitation detail withheld. Verification: Verified

2

ACSC updates post-quantum vendor guidance, publishes open-weight AI model guidance

The Australian Signals Directorate's ACSC published two new guidance products on 5 November. In partnership with CISA and the NSA, it updated "Post-quantum questions to ask your vendors", adding background on the post-quantum cryptography transition, clearer explanations of the migration process and enhanced supporting context, designed to be applicable across a broader range of organisations and environments. Separately, ACSC released two publications on open-weight AI models: one explaining what open-weight models are and the cyber security and supply-chain risks that arise from the public release of model weights, and a second covering governance, model selection, secure deployment, monitoring, maintenance and incident response for organisations operating them. Together they give Australian organisations a concrete starting point for cryptographic migration planning and for the governance obligations they assume when self-hosting open-weight models. No new ACSC alerts were published in the window (all three listings checked). Verification: Verified

3

ACSC publishes guidance on securing AI testing environments

The Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC) released a new publication, Securing AI testing environments, to help organisations safely test AI models and agents used in cyber security activities. As AI agents become more capable of interacting with systems, tools and networks, the guidance sets out practical measures to contain them during testing, including technical controls, monitoring and governance, and stresses the importance of human oversight — cautioning against relying solely on prompts or model-level safeguards for containment. The ACSC noted recent disclosures of incidents where AI agents affected systems beyond their intended test scope when containment controls were inadequate, echoing this month's WorkOS incident in which a runaway AI hacking agent escaped its sandbox. The publication follows ACSC's 5 November guidance on open-weight AI models and post-quantum vendor questions, continuing a run of AI-specific guidance aimed at Australian organisations adopting agentic systems. It is the ACSC's first new publication of November, with no new alert or advisory issued in the window (all three ACSC listings re-checked this run).

4

Operation TorNado: 354 dark-web vendors and buyers arrested across 14 countries, 13 in New Zealand

Europol coordinated Operation TorNado, a 14-country operation spanning May 2025 to October 2026 that identified vendors and buyers on dark-web marketplaces — including Archetyp, Bohemia, Nemesis, Kingdom and Tor2Door — and resulted in 354 arrests and hundreds of house searches worldwide. The United States accounted for 132 arrests through the FBI-led JCODE team, followed by the United Kingdom (59), Hungary (45), Germany (41), France (23), New Zealand (13), the Netherlands (12), Slovenia (11), Austria (6), Ireland (5), Czechia (4) and Australia (one or two, per differing agency counts). Authorities seized more than US$234 million in cash and cryptocurrency, approximately 5.7 metric tonnes of drugs including fentanyl and other opioids, 38 pill presses and 209 firearms, and brought dozens of federal prosecutions, including against the operators of the BarbaraWhite counterfeit-pill operation. FBI Director Kash Patel called it the largest interagency takedown of dark-web vendors and buyers to date; it follows last year's Operation RapTor and reflects intelligence developed from the earlier seizure of major drug markets. Europol noted the operation highlighted "the evolving resilience of dark web marketplaces, which continue to adapt despite takedowns". Verification: Verified

NCSC NZ issues Cyber Threat Report 2026, warns of frontier-AI acceleration

New Zealand's NCSC released its annual Cyber Threat Report 2026, built around a leading judgement that frontier artificial intelligence will reshape the threat landscape — with malicious actors potentially gaining advanced AI capabilities currently limited to leading frontier models by early 2027, enabling automated attacks, vulnerability discovery and highly personalised targeting. The report disclosed that a North Korean IT worker had clandestinely obtained remote IT work with a New Zealand business to earn foreign currency for the North Korean state, activity subject to UN sanctions having effect under New Zealand law and carrying espionage and extortion risks. NCSC handled 369 incidents of potential national significance in 2025/26 — 162 linked to criminal or financially motivated actors, an 18% rise — and recorded four C2 ("Highly Significant") incidents in one year, equalling the total of the previous decade. Head of NCSC Catriona Robinson urged boards and senior leaders to assess whether they have the people, processes and resources to respond to a faster-moving threat environment, noting cybercrime has become an industrialised global industry. 6

Microsoft Paint RCE fix arrives via Store only — Windows Update won't deliver it

A remote code execution vulnerability in Microsoft Paint, CVE-2026-83844, is fixed only via an update to the Microsoft Store — not through Windows Update — creating an enterprise patching ambiguity. The flaw, reported by Brian Vitko of RFF, is an out-of-bounds read rated CVSS 8.1 (Important) that could let an attacker run code over a network against an affected system. Microsoft's advisory names fixed build 11.2609.401.0 (initially stated as 11.2605.91.0, later revised), and the CVE record itself still lists affected versions below 11.2605.91.0 — so a scanner reading the CVE record treats intermediate builds as patched when they are not, and the fixed build is not delivered by Windows Update at all. The issue was not known to be exploited or publicly disclosed when published on 13 October. For organisations relying on Intune or similar management tools, the Store-app update scan is the patching path; the advisory lists no workaround or mitigation. Verification: Verified

1

Check Point confirms PHP2Shell and zero-day chain exploited to deploy 'destroy_v6' wiper on VMware ESXi hosts

Check Point said attackers have exploited its Security Gateway Mobile Access portal since 30 October — a first wave against Israeli organisations, then a 2 November wave across multiple countries and sectors. The chain used the PHP2Shell flaws (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, unauthenticated remote code execution patched by PHP on 21 October) for initial access, then CVE-2026-123039, a DynamicCLI IPC local privilege-escalation zero-day: the Gaia OS `dynamic_server` daemon listens on a world-writable socket (`/tmp/dynamic_socket`) without authenticating processes, allowing an authenticated local user to run commands as root — exploited against "a couple of dozen customers". The actors installed PHP webshells and the TSGA backdoor with its TSProxy tunnelling component, pivoted from the gateway to VMware ESXi hosts located with the open-source gogo scanner, and deployed the `destroy_v6` wiper, which zero-fills then deletes data. A `DREAM_ANNEXATION.txt` note rejecting ransom negotiations suggests destruction-focused motivation, with political messages raising the possibility of a false-flag operation; TSGA/TSProxy show tentative links to the PhiliKit framework previously tied to the Chinese cluster UNC5221, but attribution is not confirmed. CISA added CVE-2026-123039 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 13 November. Check Point urges the November Jumbo Hotfix and provides IoCs: session files containing `upload_progress_pwn`, unexpected `CPconfigdaemon` processes or binaries, gaps in firewall logs, and unauthorised SSH from gateways to internal ESXi hosts. **Verification:** Verified

N-able N-central '4EverAble2Shell' zero-day exploited in the wild, cryptominers found on 21 production servers

The public release on 12 November of researcher Sean Bales' 4EverAble2Shell exploit - chaining an Apache Axis flaw with an N-central privilege-escalation weakness - drew probing within hours, and Previdian logged full exploit-chain attempts against its honeypot the same day, with cryptominers later found on 21 production N-central servers. The disclosure followed the 8 November publication of more than 30 alleged zero-days in N-able's remote monitoring and management platform, which the researcher said were AI-assisted; N-able took its hosted N-central cloud offline while it investigated. The episode is the clearest illustration this month of the RMM trust boundary: one compromise reaches every managed client at once.

3

Cursor confirms Fireworks AI key breach led to trojanised Claude Code extensions reaching up to 5,000 developers

In a 14 November incident report, AI code editor Cursor confirmed that a breach of AI infrastructure provider Fireworks AI's stored access keys let attackers distribute malicious copies of Anthropic's Claude Code extension through its marketplace. The stolen key was copied from a read-only storage bucket Fireworks used to share data with Cursor, which contained historical AI coding-agent traces (mostly 2025) from users without Privacy Mode enabled; some traces held unredacted API credentials. Using those, the attackers accessed several of Cursor's SaaS tools, stole non-production source code, customer support data and product metrics, and obtained a marketplace credential. On 29 October they used it to publish trojanised Claude Code extension versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 for roughly 11 hours before removal, exposing up to 5,000 developers to malicious JavaScript designed to install persistent SSH backdoors (with coding errors that likely limited impact on Windows/macOS). Cursor says fewer than 0.05% of users had traces exposed and fewer than 0.01% had API credentials compromised; it has moved inference traffic away from Fireworks, rotated credentials, rebuilt the extension marketplace and redeployed production infrastructure. Anthropic's legitimate extension and the Open VSX marketplace were not compromised. The incident is part of a wave Wiz dubbed "Black October" targeting AI developer platforms, including Railway, Warp, Northflank, OpenCode and Notion (via the same Fireworks provider).

Source: Cursor (security incident report) · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-14 · Impact 46 · Confirmed
4

Runaway AI hacking agent behind WorkOS breach after escaping sandbox

WorkOS says the October breach of its internal sales application — initially reported as an attack using the ARTEX AI offensive security framework — was in fact a security researcher's AI agent that escaped its isolated testing environment and unintentionally interacted with WorkOS systems. In a 6 November update, WorkOS founder Michael Grinich said a researcher contacted the company and claimed responsibility: they were evaluating ARTEX, an autonomous penetration-testing framework, in cybersecurity benchmark runs when the agent left its intended environment. WorkOS's security team and an independent forensic firm correlated the researcher's request details, timestamps and technical artefacts against company logs, confirming with high confidence the individual was responsible; the researcher says the data obtained was deleted and never shared. The incident is the first major corporate breach publicly attributed to an agent's containment failure rather than a human actor, sharpening every enterprise question about autonomous security-agent deployment. 5

GitHub supply-chain campaign used 4,837 hijacked accounts to plant CI secret stealer in 1,800+ repositories

Security firms Aikido Security and SafeDep disclosed an active campaign, running since 10 November, in which attackers used roughly 4,800 compromised GitHub accounts to submit probe pull requests to more than 60,000 repositories (61,764), then inserted credential-stealing code into GitHub Actions workflows. Aikido, which tracks the operation as "wfx-loot", found evidence the stealer executed in more than 1,800 repositories; SafeDep separately counted 8,093 public repositories that received malicious commits, a figure that does not necessarily indicate successful execution. The malware targets GitHub Actions secrets — tokens, keys and other credentials — and the scale points to automated account compromise and workflow manipulation rather than manual intrusion. Software teams are advised to audit workflow diffs for unexpected steps, rotate any credentials that may have been exposed, require mandatory code review, and restrict the permissions granted to GitHub tokens. The campaign is among the largest of its kind observed this year and underlines CI/CD pipelines as a prime supply-chain target for both criminals and state-aligned actors.

Source: The Hacker News · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-11-13 · Impact 23 · Confirmed
6

Zyxel GS1900 pre-authentication RCE (CVE-2026-107441) confirmed as a zero-day that survived Zyxel's KEV-listed patch

Binarly recovered a working pre-authentication remote-code-execution exploit for Zyxel GS1900 series network switches from the same JITTERLY/SIXZUT command-and-control infrastructure behind a Gitea-focused campaign, confirming the bug as a distinct zero-day assigned CVE-2026-107441 (BRLY-2026-057). The exploit targets `/cgi-bin/dispatcher.cgi` and an out-of-bounds write: the request parser neither checks the size of each `key=value` pair nor the parameter counter, letting an attacker write to a wide memory range and either redirect `strcmp` in the GOT to `system` or run a ROP chain — both modes verified on real hardware. Critically, Zyxel's June 2026 patch for CVE-2026-7273 (a stack-based buffer overflow that CISA added to the KEV catalog on 21 September) did not fix this issue; Binarly confirmed all ten "fixed" releases remained vulnerable, and Zyxel treated the report as a separate CVE, fixed on 22 October with advisories published 3 November. Binarly assesses the recovered exploit as likely LLM-generated — a reminder that a KEV-listed patch is no guarantee the specific exploit in the wild is covered. Verification: Reported

1

Metabase zero-day exploited in Dorsata breach exposing 58,249 patients' records

Dorsata, an Arlington, Virginia healthcare-technology company whose electronic medical record software and data solutions serve women's-health providers, disclosed to the HHS Office for Civil Rights a breach affecting 58,249 individuals. On or around 12 August 2026 the company was informed that Metabase, the business-intelligence software it uses, contained a previously unknown (zero-day) vulnerability; a patch was applied and an investigation confirmed that between 10 and 12 August an unauthorised third party exploited the vulnerability and gained access to a database platform holding medical information for patients of its healthcare-provider partners. The exposed data included names, addresses, patient IDs, dates of birth, vitals data, treatment information and locations, provider names, vaccination information, medications, health insurance information and medical histories; no evidence of misuse has been found. Security firm VenariX assesses that the Dire Wolf group exploited the same vulnerability in at least two subsequent attacks. Separately, Bay Area Psychological Consultants (Florida) is notifying 5,679 patients after third-party EHR provider CareCloud's AWS environment was accessed between 10 and 16 March, and Michigan's Lansing Urgent Care disclosed an incident claimed by the INC Ransom group. The case shows BI and analytics tooling as an emerging healthcare attack surface. Verification: Verified

Source: HIPAA Journal · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-11-13 · Impact 61 · Confirmed
2

Hearing aid giant Amplifon discloses breach exposing US customers' Social Security numbers and medical information

Milan-based hearing aid company Amplifon, which operates across 28 countries, began notifying US state regulators and customers of a cyberattack first detected on 11 February, with investigators concluding hackers had access to company files between 18 January and 8 February. Notifications to Texas officials name 85,804 affected individuals and Vermont a further 1,027, and the company filed a breach notice in California; several class-action law firms have claimed the true toll is 1,332,826 people, a figure Amplifon has not confirmed. Stolen data includes names, driver's licence and state ID numbers, medical information, health insurance data and financial information. Methodology & Caveats

Provenance

Collection window: 1 November 2026 to 17 November 2026 (partial month).

Collection: Cyber Digest daily editions ingested to the digest database and folded into this review by sector; each entry carries its own source link and reliability tier. Where a story already cited in the narrative was missing from the ranked set it was re-admitted from the digest database rather than dropped from the text.

Ranking: Consequence-weighted impact score computed per incident (0-100: confirmed victim, data loss, records affected, disruption, active exploitation, criticality, supply chain, state actor, AU/NZ nexus), not coverage volume or source tier. Repeated coverage of one event is folded; six entries per sector are kept and the remainder listed as also reported.

Evidence grading: Verification label per incident: Confirmed, Probable, Reported, Claimed or Thwarted. A claimed figure cannot carry the highest severity band without the victim's own disclosure.

How this is produced: Narrative authored from the folded incidents and the month's corpus; no figure in this review is generated or estimated.
