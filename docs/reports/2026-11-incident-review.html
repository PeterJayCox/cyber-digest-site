Executive Summary

Analysis

The first half of November belongs to the vendor and platform layer. IT / Technology, reviewed as its own vertical from this edition, carries 53 of the 200 stories collected and leads on consequence as well as volume: the PHP2Shell chain ended in an ESXi wiper at Check Point gateway customers, a key stolen from Fireworks AI's storage bucket carried trojanised Claude Code extensions to up to 5,000 developers, N-able pulled its N-central cloud offline after 33 disclosed zero-days and was exploited within hours of publication, and 4,837 hijacked GitHub accounts planted a CI secrets stealer in more than 1,800 repositories.

Exploitation at the edge and appliance layer was the month's second signature, and the recurring lesson is that a KEV-listed patch is not proof of coverage. Zyxel's June fix left all ten 'fixed' GS1900 releases vulnerable to a separate pre-authentication flaw; SonicWall's SSL-VPN authentication bypass joined CISA's known-exploited list with a 20 November federal deadline; and the PHP2Shell chain moved from initial access to destructive payload inside the collection window rather than resolving within it.

The victim verticals follow the year's pattern. Healthcare's densest exposure came through analytics and intermediaries - Dorsata's Metabase zero-day exposed 58,249 patients, Amplifon notified 86,831 people, and a second Minnesota Epilepsy Group incident affected 82,574 - while consumer brands produced the largest disclosures by volume, with Nitori at 37.6 million records, Iris Ohyama at 7.7 million and SEGA at 2.2 million addresses. Transport's lead is a mail platform: Maileva confirmed 22 million people's names and postal addresses were exposed. In financial services the story is criminal economics rather than bank intrusion - a US$133 million heist from Credit Mutuel Arkea, US$400 million taken by North Korean FaceStealer operators, and hardware implants confirmed in three Trezor wallets sold by an authorised reseller.

For Australian and New Zealand readers the most consequential items are advisory and regulatory. ACSC published guidance on containing AI agents during testing and refreshed its post-quantum vendor questions; NCSC NZ's Cyber Threat Report 2026 warns that frontier AI may hand attackers new capability by early 2027 and records four 'highly significant' incidents in a single year. The OAIC declined to investigate the collapsed VIQ over offshore court transcription despite finding a reasonable basis for concern. Two items to watch rather than judge: Coles is investigating an incident with no evidence yet of data loss, and the Videostrong botnet Google dismantled had already reached Australian Android TV boxes.

Government

6 events

Public-sector compromise, national advisories and the regulatory response across Australian, New Zealand, UK and US agencies.

1

CISA adds actively exploited N-able MailAssure SSRF and MONAI Label path traversal to KEV Catalog

CISA added two vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog on 23 November based on evidence of active exploitation: CVE-2026-133968, a path traversal in Project MONAI's MONAI Label medical-imaging annotation platform, and CVE-2026-134624, a server-side request forgery in N-able Mail Assure (SpamExperts). The additions reinforce Binding Operational Directive 26-04, which requires federal civilian agencies to prioritise rapid remediation of KEV-listed vulnerabilities on publicly exposed assets that grant total control post-exploitation and to check for pre-patch compromise. Both products sit in widely deployed operational niches — MONAI Label in medical AI workflows and Mail Assure in managed email security — making MSP and healthcare mail environments the likely hunting ground. The additions follow the 20 November batch that included Cisco Expressway and VMware vCenter. Federal agencies must remediate on the KEV clock; Australian and New Zealand organisations have no equivalent mandate but should treat both CVEs as patch-now items. Verification: Verified

Source: CISA Alert · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-23 · Impact 30 ·
2

CISA adds four exploited flaws to KEV — LiteLLM, Synology DSM, REDCap and Apache Axis

CISA added four known-exploited vulnerabilities to its KEV catalogue on 18 November, flagging their use in ransomware, cryptomining and LLMjacking attacks: CVE-2026-40217 in BerriAI LiteLLM, an unprotected alternate channel that could allow unauthorised access (a frequent AI-gateway target); CVE-2024-10441 in Synology DiskStation Manager, an improper encoding or escaping of output; CVE-2026-90817 in Vanderbilt University's REDCap research platform, a code-injection flaw; and CVE-2026-90436 in Apache Axis, an unsafe reflection issue allowing unauthenticated remote code execution. Federal civilian agencies face binding remediation deadlines per CISA's directive, and CISA urged all organisations to patch given evidence of active exploitation. The additions follow the 17 November inclusion of Perforce Puppet Enterprise (CVE-2026-115731), which The Hacker News reported is already being exploited against enterprise management servers. The LiteLLM entry is notable for organisations exposing AI gateways, and the Axis entry for Java-based web services still in production. Verification: Verified

Source: CISA · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-18 · Impact 30 ·
3

ACSC warns vulnerabilities are moving quickly: patch, protect and prevent

Australia's Cyber Security Centre issued an alert on 17 November warning that software vulnerabilities are being disclosed faster and exploited sooner, with the ASD's Annual Cyber Threat Report 2025–26 finding publicly reported vulnerabilities increased by 30 per cent last financial year and some flaws were successfully exploited within 48 hours of patches or mitigation advice becoming available. The alert, aimed at a technical audience across all organisations that manage and operate networks, says automated and AI-enabled tools are helping malicious actors identify and exploit vulnerabilities faster and across more systems, so organisations have less time to act once a vulnerability becomes known. Its guidance is structured as three actions: patch (accelerate patching, enable automatic updates, confirm suppliers can patch managed systems promptly); protect (reduce exposure of internet-facing systems, strengthen identity and access controls including MFA, address legacy and unsupported systems); and prevent (monitor for suspicious activity, identify and remediate known flaws before exploitation). Where updates cannot be applied immediately, the ACSC advises assessing risk, restricting exposure and applying temporary controls until remediation is complete, with assistance available via 1300 CYBER1.

Source: ACSC (cyber.gov.au) · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-17 · Impact 30 ·
4

RIKEN's Fugaku supercomputer breached; user SSH keys exposed, up to 4,597 affected

RIKEN confirmed unauthorised access to a system involved in operating Fugaku, Japan's national flagship supercomputer, on 13 November, and suspended Fugaku and blocked external access at 04:30 on 14 November. The intruder entered through a publicly accessible web page displaying Fugaku's operational status, with access occurring between 10 and 13 November, and tampered with multiple internal servers, including by installing malicious programs. RIKEN says files in users' home directories and records from the user authentication system were "either taken or may have been taken" — names, email addresses and IDs for up to 4,224 Fugaku users and up to 375 RIKYU users, 4,597 people in total — plus credentials users stored for reaching external systems, such as SSH private keys and access keys. RIKEN states there is "no evidence that research data or files were exfiltrated", but confirmed the intruder attempted to connect to other systems using obtained credentials. It invalidated all users' login public keys and is rebuilding compromised servers. Verification: Verified

5

Bulgaria uncovers five-year cyberespionage operation that drained 130 GB of government files

Bulgaria's ministry for innovation and digital transformation has revealed a hacking operation that ran undetected inside government systems from 2021 until August, when a newly deployed national cybersecurity monitoring system flagged suspicious activity. Minister Ivan Vassilev said more than 130 GB — around 62 million pages — of documents were downloaded, including cabinet papers and inter-ministerial communications, though no evidence has emerged that citizens' personal data was affected. Investigators say several international hacking groups were involved but have attributed the campaign to no state; the affected systems have been isolated, malware removed and prosecutors asked to examine both the intrusion and the agencies' five-year failure to detect it. The announcement was first made around 18 November and the corpus's first coverage appeared 23 November, so it stands as a first-coverage entry outside the strict 48-hour window. The case dwarfs Bulgaria's 2019 National Revenue Agency breach in volume and raises the same monitoring question for every allied government. Verification: Reported Breach: Probable breach

FBI investigates apparent breach of National Crime Information Center (NCIC)

The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of its National Crime Information Center, the nationwide database federal, state and local law enforcement use to share warrant, criminal history and background-check records. The intrusion was discovered in recent days; officials briefed on the probe told CNN and AP the bureau has yet to make a public statement. The scope is unclear, but the NCIC serves more than 100,000 law-enforcement partners and held over 18 million active records as of 2023. A former FBI official told CNN the concern is not only whether hackers accessed sensitive information but whether they could compromise the accuracy of records officers rely on. President Trump called the reported hack "not good" in a Fox News interview. The incident follows the FBI's September disclosure that agents' own personal information was stolen in a separate cyberattack. Verification: Reported Breach: Probable breach

1

Check Point confirms PHP2Shell and zero-day chain exploited to deploy 'destroy_v6' wiper on VMware ESXi hosts

Check Point said attackers have exploited its Security Gateway Mobile Access portal since 30 October — a first wave against Israeli organisations, then a 2 November wave across multiple countries and sectors. The chain used the PHP2Shell flaws (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, unauthenticated remote code execution patched by PHP on 21 October) for initial access, then CVE-2026-123039, a DynamicCLI IPC local privilege-escalation zero-day: the Gaia OS `dynamic_server` daemon listens on a world-writable socket (`/tmp/dynamic_socket`) without authenticating processes, allowing an authenticated local user to run commands as root — exploited against "a couple of dozen customers". The actors installed PHP webshells and the TSGA backdoor with its TSProxy tunnelling component, pivoted from the gateway to VMware ESXi hosts located with the open-source gogo scanner, and deployed the `destroy_v6` wiper, which zero-fills then deletes data. A `DREAM_ANNEXATION.txt` note rejecting ransom negotiations suggests destruction-focused motivation, with political messages raising the possibility of a false-flag operation; TSGA/TSProxy show tentative links to the PhiliKit framework previously tied to the Chinese cluster UNC5221, but attribution is not confirmed. CISA added CVE-2026-123039 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 13 November. Check Point urges the November Jumbo Hotfix and provides IoCs: session files containing `upload_progress_pwn`, unexpected `CPconfigdaemon` processes or binaries, gaps in firewall logs, and unauthorised SSH from gateways to internal ESXi hosts. **Verification:** Verified

N-able N-central '4EverAble2Shell' zero-day exploited in the wild, cryptominers found on 21 production servers

The public release on 12 November of researcher Sean Bales' 4EverAble2Shell exploit - chaining an Apache Axis flaw with an N-central privilege-escalation weakness - drew probing within hours, and Previdian logged full exploit-chain attempts against its honeypot the same day, with cryptominers later found on 21 production N-central servers. The disclosure followed the 8 November publication of more than 30 alleged zero-days in N-able's remote monitoring and management platform, which the researcher said were AI-assisted; N-able took its hosted N-central cloud offline while it investigated. The episode is the clearest illustration this month of the RMM trust boundary: one compromise reaches every managed client at once.

3

Cursor confirms Fireworks AI key breach led to trojanised Claude Code extensions reaching up to 5,000 developers

In a 14 November incident report, AI code editor Cursor confirmed that a breach of AI infrastructure provider Fireworks AI's stored access keys let attackers distribute malicious copies of Anthropic's Claude Code extension through its marketplace. The stolen key was copied from a read-only storage bucket Fireworks used to share data with Cursor, which contained historical AI coding-agent traces (mostly 2025) from users without Privacy Mode enabled; some traces held unredacted API credentials. Using those, the attackers accessed several of Cursor's SaaS tools, stole non-production source code, customer support data and product metrics, and obtained a marketplace credential. On 29 October they used it to publish trojanised Claude Code extension versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 for roughly 11 hours before removal, exposing up to 5,000 developers to malicious JavaScript designed to install persistent SSH backdoors (with coding errors that likely limited impact on Windows/macOS). Cursor says fewer than 0.05% of users had traces exposed and fewer than 0.01% had API credentials compromised; it has moved inference traffic away from Fireworks, rotated credentials, rebuilt the extension marketplace and redeployed production infrastructure. Anthropic's legitimate extension and the Open VSX marketplace were not compromised. The incident is part of a wave Wiz dubbed "Black October" targeting AI developer platforms, including Railway, Warp, Northflank, OpenCode and Notion (via the same Fireworks provider).

Source: Cursor (security incident report) · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-14 · Impact 46 · Confirmed
4

Siemens extends Java4Shell (CVE-2026-114938) to Desigo CC and Siveillance with no fix yet

Siemens updated advisory SSA-810379 on 23 November to add Desigo CC Advanced Reporting V8/V9 and Siveillance Control/Control Pro — building management, PSIM and critical-site command-and-control products — to the list of products affected by Java4Shell (CVE-2026-114938), the heap-based out-of-bounds write in OpenJDK's hardware-accelerated Base64 decoder disclosed on 17 November. The flaw requires an affected JVM on an AVX-512-capable Intel or AMD x86-64 CPU, untrusted Base64 reaching the JIT-compiled decoder, and carries a CVSS 3.1 base score of 8.1 (6.6 product-specific for Desigo's loopback-isolated architecture). No fixes are available for the affected Siemens products yet; until patched Java ships, Siemens directs customers to add `-XX:-UseBASE64Intrinsics` to JVM startup arguments. The update is the clearest sign yet that the Java4Shell family, already a known-exploited concern in the week's patch storm, reaches into operational technology — ports, airports, oil and gas, and pharma among Siveillance's target environments. Verification: Verified

Source: Siemens ProductCERT · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-23 · Impact 30 ·
5

Upload2Shell: Ghostscript CVE-2026-125396 turns image uploads into remote code execution

Researchers at pwn.ai have detailed Upload2Shell (CVE-2026-125396), a use-after-free in Ghostscript's Type 1 font loader that breaks the interpreter's `-dSAFER` sandbox: a crafted Type 1 font triggers a type error in the `.putgstringcopy` helper, letting an error handler reach the privileged `.forceput` operator and, combined with a save/restore lifetime bug, build a write-what-where primitive that disables sandbox state and runs commands as the rendering process. Because applications hand uploads to image libraries that silently route PostScript to Ghostscript, confirmed command execution paths include Debian 13's CUPS print stack via anonymous print jobs, Roundcube webmail (attachment execution when the recipient merely viewed the email), Plesk Obsidian, unauthenticated WordPress plugin routes and MediaWiki/DokuWiki previews; at Google, a signed-out request reached Ghostscript in the document renderer, mitigated 10 November. CVSS 4.0 scores the flaw 8.5 High, though the unauthenticated upload routes make it more serious in practice. The fix shipped in Ghostscript 10.08.0 on 8 September, but no exploitation in the wild has been reported and no PoC is public. Verification: Verified

Source: pwn.ai · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-11-22 · Impact 30 ·
6

Broadcom ships emergency patch for VMware vCenter zero-day exploited since September; Mandiant attributes it to China-linked Armory Castle

Broadcom has released emergency fixes under VMSA-2026-0009 for CVE-2026-123890, a critical (CVSS 9.8) authentication-bypass in the VMware Directory Service of vCenter Server that lets an unauthenticated, network-reachable attacker gain administrator access; the advisory also fixes four flaws in SDDC Manager and VMware Tools for Windows, with no workarounds available. Mandiant and Google Threat Intelligence Group report that suspected Chinese state-sponsored group Armory Castle (formerly UNC5221) has exploited the flaw in zero-day attacks since early September, chaining it with a BeyondTrust Remote Support zero-day (CVE-2026-102764). The researchers observed a custom Golang tool, BONESAW, creating a vCenter SSO admin account and stealing the directory database (`data.mdb`), then SAWSNAKE enabling Golden SAML attacks against MFA, with CAGEMATCH, BEARBONES and LAZYBONES webshells for persistence; victims' ESXi hosts and Windows Active Directory files were ultimately accessed. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 20 November. Verification: Verified

1

Metabase zero-day exploited in Dorsata breach exposing 58,249 patients' records

Dorsata, an Arlington, Virginia healthcare-technology company whose electronic medical record software and data solutions serve women's-health providers, disclosed to the HHS Office for Civil Rights a breach affecting 58,249 individuals. On or around 12 August 2026 the company was informed that Metabase, the business-intelligence software it uses, contained a previously unknown (zero-day) vulnerability; a patch was applied and an investigation confirmed that between 10 and 12 August an unauthorised third party exploited the vulnerability and gained access to a database platform holding medical information for patients of its healthcare-provider partners. The exposed data included names, addresses, patient IDs, dates of birth, vitals data, treatment information and locations, provider names, vaccination information, medications, health insurance information and medical histories; no evidence of misuse has been found. Security firm VenariX assesses that the Dire Wolf group exploited the same vulnerability in at least two subsequent attacks. Separately, Bay Area Psychological Consultants (Florida) is notifying 5,679 patients after third-party EHR provider CareCloud's AWS environment was accessed between 10 and 16 March, and Michigan's Lansing Urgent Care disclosed an incident claimed by the INC Ransom group. The case shows BI and analytics tooling as an emerging healthcare attack surface. Verification: Verified

Source: HIPAA Journal · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-11-13 · Impact 61 · Confirmed
2

Hearing aid giant Amplifon discloses breach exposing US customers' Social Security numbers and medical information

Milan-based hearing aid company Amplifon, which operates across 28 countries, began notifying US state regulators and customers of a cyberattack first detected on 11 February, with investigators concluding hackers had access to company files between 18 January and 8 February. Notifications to Texas officials name 85,804 affected individuals and Vermont a further 1,027, and the company filed a breach notice in California; several class-action law firms have claimed the true toll is 1,332,826 people, a figure Amplifon has not confirmed. The Tor Project released an emergency update on 17 November in response to an ongoing distributed denial-of-service attack targeting the anonymity network, urging relay operators and users to upgrade. Methodology & Caveats

Provenance

Collection window: 1 November 2026 to 17 November 2026 (partial month).

Collection: Cyber Digest daily editions ingested to the digest database and folded into this review by sector; each entry carries its own source link and reliability tier. Where a story already cited in the narrative was missing from the ranked set it was re-admitted from the digest database rather than dropped from the text.

Ranking: Consequence-weighted impact score computed per incident (0-100: confirmed victim, data loss, records affected, disruption, active exploitation, criticality, supply chain, state actor, AU/NZ nexus), not coverage volume or source tier. Repeated coverage of one event is folded; six entries per sector are kept and the remainder listed as also reported.

Evidence grading: Verification label per incident: Confirmed, Probable, Reported, Claimed or Thwarted. A claimed figure cannot carry the highest severity band without the victim's own disclosure.

How this is produced: Narrative authored from the folded incidents and the month's corpus; no figure in this review is generated or estimated.
