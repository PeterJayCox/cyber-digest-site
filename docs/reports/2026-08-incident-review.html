Healthcare

Health-ISAC Warns of Increasing ShinyHunters Healthcare Data Theft Attacks

Health sector organisations warned of increased successful attacks by the ShinyHunters group, which has been increasingly targeting healthcare data. The ISAC alert underscores persistent targeting of health sector data by criminal groups.

Source: HIPAA Journal · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-07-31 · Score 80
2

Data Breaches Announced by Loma Linda University Health & UCLA Health

Two California academic providers disclosed incidents. Loma Linda University Health reported that a dataset containing medical record numbers, dates of birth and limited clinical information from an IRB-approved research study was inadvertently uploaded to an external AI platform (no SSNs or financial data involved; affected count not yet disclosed). UCLA Health notified patients of an improper disclosure to an outside healthcare provider occurring between December 2024 and April 2026, involving names, DOBs, health insurance and clinical referral data, with last-four SSNs for a limited number of individuals; UCLA reported no evidence of misuse. Breach Triage: Confirmed — both are first-party disclosures to state regulators.

Source: HIPAA Journal · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-10 · Score 60
3

CareCloud Notifies More Than 345,000 Patients About Cyberattack Data Theft

CareCloud, a cloud-based EHR/RCM vendor, began notifying over 345,000 patients that their data was compromised. The incident highlights concentration risk in healthcare IT vendors — a single compromise cascades across multiple provider organisations.

Source: HIPAA Journal · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-03 · Score 60
ThematicTop thematic change

Health-data exposure keeps widening — from vendor breaches to AI-platform and cross-provider disclosure

The August incidents cluster around vendor concentration risk (CareCloud 345k+ patients) and a growing set of non-breach exposure paths: an IRB research dataset inadvertently uploaded to an external AI platform (Loma Linda University Health) and an improper cross-provider disclosure dating back to 2024 (UCLA Health). Regulators are increasingly treating these as safety issues, not just privacy events, and the AI-upload vector is a new class of accidental exposure health organisations need to govern — mirroring the vendor risk-management expectations APRA CPS 234 places on financial services.

Source: HIPAA Journal, Cyber Digest database, July–August 2026

Energy & Utilities

3 incidents
1

Poland Uncovers Second Heat Plant Cyberattack That Went Hidden for Months

CERT Polska revealed at DEF CON that an attack on a Polish combined heat and power plant supplying ~50,000 residents went unrecognised as malicious during last winter, initially blamed on a contractor error. The investigation traced the first known use of a private cellular data network as an ICS pathway: attackers moved from already-compromised wind farm firewalls to a cellular router, then across the private network to a heat plant controller still running factory-default credentials, dwelled 11 days, and disabled Siemens controllers on 29 December before wiping network equipment to destroy forensic evidence — only one legacy router's logs enabled reconstruction. CERT Polska warns the "trusted" private cellular network misconfiguration is believed widespread internationally.

Source: The Record · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-11 · Score 60
2

Over 4,400 Rockwell PLCs Exposed Online, 22 Found in Water Attack Cities

Forescout's scan counted 4,407 internet-exposed Rockwell Automation PLCs worldwide, including 22 in cities hit by recent US water-sector attacks. Exposing EtherNet/IP on port 44818 creates an unauthenticated path letting attackers change IP settings or set passwords, matching the no-exploit effect seen in the water campaign.

Source: The Hacker News · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-06 · Score 60
3

Cyberattacks on Water Systems Expand to 12 States as South Dakota and Georgia Announce Incidents

Cyberattacks targeting water systems expanded to 12 US states, with South Dakota and Georgia the latest to announce incidents as CISA urged the water and wastewater sector to protect OT against PLC-targeting activity. The spread marks an escalation from single-state incidents to a coordinated, multi-state campaign against critical water infrastructure.

Source: The Record · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-05 · Score 60
TechnologyTop technology change

Private cellular networks are a new OT attack pathway — the 'trusted' enclave assumption is breaking down

CERT Polska's DEF CON disclosure showed the first documented use of a private cellular data network as an ICS pathway: attackers pivoted from compromised wind-farm firewalls through a cellular router into a heat plant controller still on factory-default credentials, dwelling 11 days before disabling Siemens controllers and wiping evidence. Combined with the August water-sector campaign (now 12 US states) and 4,400+ exposed Rockwell PLCs, the period shows OT segregation must extend to private LTE/cellular backhaul — a direct priority for Australian and NZ energy operators under the SOCI Act and NZISM, whose distributed renewable sites rely on the same private-cellular pattern.

Source: The Record (CERT Polska/DEF CON), The Hacker News, CyberScoop, August 2026

Global (Macro)

3 incidents
1

FBI, CISA and International Partners Warn of Gunra Ransomware Targeting Critical Infrastructure

A joint advisory (AA26-222A) from the FBI, CISA, DoD DC3, NSA, US Secret Service and the Republic of Korea's National Police Agency details Gunra, a ransomware-as-a-service variant derived from leaked Conti source code. Gunra first emerged April 2025, launched a structured RaaS affiliate program in early 2026, and operates a double-extortion model with a Tor-based negotiation portal and dedicated leak site. Victim sectors span healthcare, financial services, critical manufacturing, transport, government, utilities, academia, media and retail across the Americas, Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Asia-Pacific. The advisory includes patching priorities for internet-facing VPN/RDP, offline immutable backups, and network segmentation. Confidence: Confirmed (joint official advisory with IOCs).

Source: CISA · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-08-10 · Score 100
2

China-Linked Hackers Deploy New StormEncryptor Ransomware, Likely via N-central Flaw

Microsoft's Threat Intelligence team disclosed that Storm-1175, a financially motivated China-linked threat actor, has deployed a previously undocumented ransomware strain called StormEncryptor — a C++ locker that appends `.encrypted` to encrypted files and drops `!!!README_FIRST!!!.txt` ransom notes, marking a shift from the actor's prior use of Medusa. Microsoft assesses the initial-access vector is likely exploitation of CVE-2026-18577 in N-able N-central, tying the new ransomware to the same RMM vulnerability that drove the week's hotfix cycle. Confidence: Probable (vendor analysis; exploitation path assessed, not directly observed).

Source: The Hacker News · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-10 · Score 60
3

Kimsuky Builds Offline AI Stack to Boost Phishing and Automate Malware Development

South Korea's Genians reports that North Korean espionage group Kimsuky (under the Reconnaissance General Bureau) is running AI offline on its own servers, connecting document-search tools to stolen files and assembling the software components needed to fold AI into its malware. Genians found no evidence of proprietary model training but describes the group in a "research and knowledge acquisition" stage, using AI to automate phishing and malware development. Confidence: Probable (vendor telemetry-based; no intrusion confirmed).

Source: The Hacker News · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-10 · Score 60
ThematicTop thematic change

Ransomware scale and RMM exploitation are the defining macro threats of August — not AI agents

The period's lead story is the joint Five Eyes–ROK #StopRansomware advisory on Gunra, a Conti-derived RaaS whose affiliates target government, healthcare, transport and utilities worldwide, alongside Microsoft's disclosure that China-linked Storm-1175 is now deploying a new StormEncryptor locker likely via the N-able N-central flaw (CVE-2026-18577). A joint CISA/FBI/DoD advisory naming critical infrastructure replaces the earlier AI-containment theme as the top macro signal: privilege-management platforms and internet-exposed RMM surfaces are now the highest-value initial-access vector, a direct concern for Australian MSPs and critical-infrastructure operators running N-central under ACSC Essential Eight guidance.

Source: CISA (AA26-222A), The Hacker News/Microsoft, The Record, August 2026

Transport

3 incidents
1

CISA Issues ICS Advisory on CPDLC over ATN-B1 Vulnerabilities (Five CVEs)

CISA released ICS advisory ICSA-26-219-01 covering five CVEs affecting Controller-Pilot Data Link Communications (CPDLC) over ATN-B1. The system relies on legacy clear-text, unauthenticated radio-frequency links that allow unauthorised message injection, denial-of-service and forced session resets in the air-traffic-control data link — can degrade operational safety margins, though not an unsafe aircraft condition.

Source: CISA · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-08-07 · Score 100
2

Cyberattack on North Carolina Ports 'Contained' as Coast Guard, State Officials Investigate

North Carolina Ports handling more than 4 million tons of cargo annually was hacked, forcing a shift to manual operations. The breach was later 'contained' with facilities in recovery but still processing manually with gate delays, as an outside forensics team investigates and other US ports conduct checks.

Source: The Record · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-06 · Score 60
3

Cyberattack on Ceva Logistics Warehouses in Europe Impacts Retailers

Operations at eight Ceva Logistics warehouses in Europe were disrupted by a cyberattack in early August 2026, causing shipping delays for retail customers with inventory held there. Affected customers were notified on 1 August that the intrusion had captured personal and business data; Dutch retail group De Bijenkorf and e-commerce platform bol confirmed a logistics vendor's systems were compromised and temporarily suspended data exchanges. The breach is being investigated by the Dutch Data Protection Authority.

Source: FreightWaves / American Shipper · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-01 · Score 60
TechnologyTop technology change

Aviation safety-data links, maritime ports and logistics networks are all emerging as high-value OT and supply-chain targets

The CPDLC/ATN-B1 advisory highlights legacy clear-text, unauthenticated radio links in air-traffic control, the North Carolina Ports outage forced manual operations, and the Ceva Logistics warehouse attack disrupted European retail supply chains. Together they point to operational technology in transport — from aircraft data links to port and warehouse systems — becoming a rising target with direct downstream economic impact.

Source: CISA, The Record, August 2026

ThematicTop thematic change

Wallet security and social-engineering-by-phone are the leading financial-sector threat vectors

Two hardware-wallet incidents (Coincard $88M, Coldcard $70M) expose trust in the crypto custody layer, while UNC6671's vishing wave shows attackers pivoting to employees' personal phones with AI-assisted lures, and Nigeria's Zenith Bank suffered a database breach of customer records. For banks and fintechs, the takeaway is that credential and custody controls — not just perimeter defence — are now the front line.

Source: The Record, The Hacker News, August 2026
