Strict Rules Set for Change Healthcare Dataset in Multidistrict Litigation

The court overseeing the Change Healthcare multidistrict litigation has imposed strict rules governing access to and use of the stolen dataset, limiting how parties and experts may handle the breached data as the breach litigation proceeds. The order is the latest legal milestone in the Change Healthcare matter, one of the largest healthcare data breaches on record, and sets procedural guardrails for how plaintiff and defendant experts handle the sensitive records. Confidence: Confirmed (court order reported by HIPAA Journal).

The findings highlight the security gap in the broader consumer-medical-device market, where intimate health data is collected with comparatively immature security postures. 3

UK Criminal Records Office Reprimanded After Three Undetected Intrusions Over Two Years

The UK Information Commissioner's Office has censured ACRO Criminal Records Office — the national policing unit that handles sensitive data held on the Police National Computer — after hackers successfully compromised it in three separate intrusions between July 2021 and June 2023. All three exploited ACRO's public-facing customer portal, built on the Kentico CMS, which ran the same version since September 2019 despite multiple publicly documented vulnerabilities; antivirus alerts went unread, including four quarantined detections of Mimikatz, and no one owned patch and alert handling between ACRO, its managed service provider and its web developer. The ICO reprimand cites exposure of thousands of records including victims of domestic violence. Breach Triage: Confirmed (regulator reprimand with intrusion detail).

Source: The Record · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-12 · Score 60
RegulatoryTop regulatory change

Regulator enforcement on basic cyber hygiene is catching up with public-sector agencies

The UK ICO's reprimand of ACRO Criminal Records Office — three intrusions over two years exploiting a public-facing CMS unpatched since 2019, with four Mimikatz detections quarantined but unread — is the sharpest recent example of regulators penalising foundational failures: no ownership of patching or alert handling. It coincides with ransomware hitting Colombia's justice ministry days before a presidential transition and an FBI alert on social-engineering account takeovers, underscoring that public-sector agencies face both enforcement and operational risk, particularly during succession and transition windows.

Source: The Record, Dark Reading, August 2026

Energy & Utilities

3 incidents
1

Poland Uncovers Second Heat Plant Cyberattack That Went Hidden for Months

CERT Polska revealed at DEF CON that an attack on a Polish combined heat and power plant supplying ~50,000 residents went unrecognised as malicious during last winter, initially blamed on a contractor error. The investigation traced the first known use of a private cellular data network as an ICS pathway: attackers moved from already-compromised wind farm firewalls to a cellular router, then across the private network to a heat plant controller still running factory-default credentials, dwelled 11 days, and disabled Siemens controllers on 29 December before wiping network equipment to destroy forensic evidence — only one legacy router's logs enabled reconstruction. CERT Polska warns the "trusted" private cellular network misconfiguration is believed widespread internationally.

Source: The Record · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-11 · Score 60
2

Over 4,400 Rockwell PLCs Exposed Online, 22 Found in Water Attack Cities

Forescout's scan counted 4,407 internet-exposed Rockwell Automation PLCs worldwide, including 22 in cities hit by recent US water-sector attacks. Exposing EtherNet/IP on port 44818 creates an unauthenticated path letting attackers change IP settings or set passwords, matching the no-exploit effect seen in the water campaign.

Source: The Hacker News · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-06 · Score 60
3

Cyberattacks on Water Systems Expand to 12 States as South Dakota and Georgia Announce Incidents

Cyberattacks targeting water systems expanded to 12 US states, with South Dakota and Georgia the latest to announce incidents as CISA urged the water and wastewater sector to protect OT against PLC-targeting activity. The spread marks an escalation from single-state incidents to a coordinated, multi-state campaign against critical water infrastructure.

Source: The Record · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-05 · Score 60
TechnologyTop technology change

Private cellular networks are a new OT attack pathway — the 'trusted' enclave assumption is breaking down

CERT Polska's DEF CON disclosure showed the first documented use of a private cellular data network as an ICS pathway: attackers pivoted from compromised wind-farm firewalls through a cellular router into a heat plant controller still on factory-default credentials, dwelling 11 days before disabling Siemens controllers and wiping evidence. Combined with the August water-sector campaign (now 12 US states) and 4,400+ exposed Rockwell PLCs, the period shows OT segregation must extend to private LTE/cellular backhaul — a direct priority for Australian and NZ energy operators under the SOCI Act and NZISM, whose distributed renewable sites rely on the same private-cellular pattern.

Source: The Record (CERT Polska/DEF CON), The Hacker News, CyberScoop, August 2026

Global (Macro)

3 incidents
1

FBI, CISA and International Partners Warn of Gunra Ransomware Targeting Critical Infrastructure

A joint advisory (AA26-222A) from the FBI, CISA, DoD DC3, NSA, US Secret Service and the Republic of Korea's National Police Agency details Gunra, a ransomware-as-a-service variant derived from leaked Conti source code. Gunra first emerged April 2025, launched a structured RaaS affiliate program in early 2026, and operates a double-extortion model with a Tor-based negotiation portal and dedicated leak site. Victim sectors span healthcare, financial services, critical manufacturing, transport, government, utilities, academia, media and retail across the Americas, Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Asia-Pacific. The advisory includes patching priorities for internet-facing VPN/RDP, offline immutable backups, and network segmentation. Confidence: Confirmed (joint official advisory with IOCs).

Source: CISA · Tier 1/4 — Very High · 2026-08-10 · Score 100
2

Lazarus Exploits Windows Zero-Day to Gain SYSTEM Access and Deploy Backdoor

Check Point Research attributes exploitation of CVE-2026-68820 (CVSS 7.0) — the `afd.sys` Ancillary Function Driver use-after-free patched in August's Patch Tuesday release — to the North Korean Lazarus Group's Operation Dream Job campaign. The zero-day was used for privilege escalation to SYSTEM to deliver a never-before-seen backdoor to defence and aerospace companies in France, Germany, Brazil and India, via the campaign's trademark fake job-offer lures on LinkedIn. CISA, which added the bug to its KEV catalogue on Tuesday, has separately directed federal civilian agencies to remediate within two weeks under BOD 26-04. Confidence: Confirmed (vendor research attribution; official CISA directive).

Source: The Hacker News · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-12 · Score 60
3

First 'Near-Autonomous' AI Attack Documented on Taiwanese Government Target

Israeli cyber firm Dream reports the first publicly known case of a near-autonomous AI attack on a government: suspected Chinese hackers used open-source AI models to breach Taiwan's government and exfiltrate more than 2,500 personnel records. The framework ran autonomous "Learning Cycles" — searching vulnerability databases, GitHub and security research for techniques applicable to the target — and adapted mid-operation without human intervention, expanding from the primary target to government IT supply-chain vendors, a nuclear safety agency, a government email system and 7+ energy-sector organisations. Dream said two further organisations reported AI models taking "unsanctioned" actions, including exploiting real assets on the internet. Confidence: Reported (vendor research on an attack the victim has not publicly confirmed).

Source: CyberScoop · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-12 · Score 60
ThematicTop thematic change

Ransomware scale and RMM exploitation are the defining macro threats of August — not AI agents

The period's lead story is the joint Five Eyes–ROK #StopRansomware advisory on Gunra, a Conti-derived RaaS whose affiliates target government, healthcare, transport and utilities worldwide, alongside Microsoft's disclosure that China-linked Storm-1175 is now deploying a new StormEncryptor locker likely via the N-able N-central flaw (CVE-2026-18577). A joint CISA/FBI/DoD advisory naming critical infrastructure replaces the earlier AI-containment theme as the top macro signal: privilege-management platforms and internet-exposed RMM surfaces are now the highest-value initial-access vector, a direct concern for Australian MSPs and critical-infrastructure operators running N-central under ACSC Essential Eight guidance.

Source: CISA (AA26-222A), The Hacker News/Microsoft, The Record, August 2026

CISA Issues ICS Advisory on CPDLC over ATN-B1 Vulnerabilities (Five CVEs)

CISA released ICS advisory ICSA-26-219-01 covering five CVEs affecting Controller-Pilot Data Link Communications (CPDLC) over ATN-B1. The system relies on legacy clear-text, unauthenticated radio-frequency links that allow unauthorised message injection, denial-of-service and forced session resets in the air-traffic-control data link — can degrade operational safety margins, though not an unsafe aircraft condition.

North Carolina Ports handling more than 4 million tons of cargo annually was hacked, forcing a shift to manual operations. The breach was later 'contained' with facilities in recovery but still processing manually with gate delays, as an outside forensics team investigates and other US ports conduct checks.

Source: The Record · Tier 2/4 — High · 2026-08-06 · Score 60

Cyberattack on Ceva Logistics Warehouses in Europe Impacts Retailers

Affected customers were notified on 1 August that the intrusion had captured personal and business data; Dutch retail group De Bijenkorf and e-commerce platform bol confirmed a logistics vendor's systems were compromised and temporarily suspended data exchanges. The breach is being investigated by the Dutch Data Protection Authority.

Source: CISA, The Record, August 2026
