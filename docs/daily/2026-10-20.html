Executive Summary

A compromise of certificate-authority intermediates is the day's most consequential story: the private keys behind eight eMudhra intermediate CAs are in unknown hands, forcing Chrome and Mozilla to push emergency blocks within hours of a researcher's 17 October report and Go to ship blocking releases on 20 October — a trust-infrastructure failure that could let a holder mint a browser-trusted certificate for any domain. Running second is evidence that agentic tooling has crossed into criminal production use: a researcher reading the attackers' own agent boards watched roughly two dozen exposed instances of the open-source AI penetration-testing tool Cairn run more than 1,100 projects from instructions of fewer than 100 Chinese characters, attempting attacks on more than 1,200 sites and reaching named industrial and retail victims in the United States, Japan, Australia and Canada. Alongside them, an FBI/CISA-led coalition published an authoritative advisory on SafePay, now linked to at least 600 victims since August 2024 including Australian organisations, while two software-supply incidents landed in one day — a hijacked contributor account backdooring five OVHcloud CLI releases and a self-restoring backdoor in Rank Math PRO updates.

There is no direct Australian incident in this window, but two of the day's stories land squarely on local interests. The ConnectWise Cloud Backup (formerly SkyKick) supply-chain compromise — iTnews reported on 18 October that Huntress had confirmed unauthorised Microsoft 365 sign-ins in 162 tenants, later finding almost 1,000 tenants with sign-in activity from the attacker's address and rogue app passwords planted to last until 2028 — puts application-identity governance and APRA's CPS 234 third-party expectations back at the centre of local assurance, and the separate wave of attempts against 16 tenants in the policy, national-security, trade and critical-minerals sectors maps onto allied and Australian targeting. The joint FBI/CISA-led advisory on SafePay names at least 600 victims across critical infrastructure since August 2024, including Australian organisations, with edge devices and backup infrastructure the main initial-access routes. All three ACSC listings were re-checked on 21 October with no new alert or advisory in the 18–20 October window: the newest alert remains the 14 October *Protecting operational technology and edge devices*, the newest advisory the 9 October item on Chinese state-linked actors, and the news listing's newest post the 15 October ASD Essentials release already covered in the 17 October edition. The OAIC Funnelback search endpoint, queried 21 October, shows no new notifiable-data-breach or enforcement item — its newest entry is *International Access to Information Day 2026* (20 October).

In the week to 19 October (14–19 October), the digest corpus carried 45 stories: Zero-day/Vulnerability led with 13, followed by Malware (11), Breach/Data Leak (9) and Ransomware (4), with OT/ICS at 3. The geography was US and Australia level at 18 each, then Global (4) and Europe (3). Three threads dominate the run into this edition. The first is trust infrastructure rather than any single victim: the eMudhra intermediate-CA key compromise sits alongside the Rank Math PRO and OVHcloud CLI backdoors as a week of attacks on the channels through which software and certificates reach their consumers, where a single credential or key grants attacker-scale reach. The second is the maturing criminal use of autonomous agents — the Cairn campaign's leaked agent boards give the first readable evidence that AI tooling is being orchestrated at volume against named industrial and retail targets across the United States, Japan, Australia and Canada, which is why the agent layer rather than any individual intrusion is the trend to watch. The third is the ransomware enforcement turn: the multi-agency SafePay advisory, six same-day KEV additions and continuing PAN-OS CVE-2026-0257 exploitation against Qilin and INC describe the same edge-device and backup-infrastructure weakness from four different angles. Expect the certificate and software-release supply chain, and the agent layer, to keep echoing through the week ahead.

Global (Macro) 8 stories

1

eMudhra intermediate CA keys compromised, forcing emergency browser and Go blocks

The private keys of eight eMudhra intermediate certificate authority certificates are compromised, the Mozilla and Chrome root programs confirmed on 20 October. An intermediate CA signs the certificates that websites present, so anyone holding one of these keys could issue a certificate for any domain that a browser would accept. A researcher reported the compromise at 18:05 UTC on 17 October; Chrome had a CRLSet blocking the six TLS intermediates live by 19:45 UTC and Mozilla finished pushing a OneCRL block to Firefox at 22:59 UTC — neither required a browser update or restart. The eight certificates include the DV, OV and EV TLS intermediates G3B and G3C plus two OV code-signing intermediates; the six TLS ones chain to emSign ECC Root CA G3, which was pulled from Mozilla's root store. Chrome's block matches the public key, so it also catches Sectigo cross-signed copies. No TLS certificate chaining to the six intermediates had been logged to Certificate Transparency, so none could have been trusted by default. It is not known how, when or to whom the keys left eMudhra; Go blocked the same eight in its 20 October releases.

2

Chinese-speaking operator uses autonomous AI agents to attack more than 1,200 sites

A researcher has documented a Chinese-speaking criminal outsourcing a mass intrusion campaign almost entirely to autonomous AI agents. Snorre Fagerland of NorForge found roughly two dozen exposed instances of Cairn — an open-source AI penetration-testing tool — being used maliciously from about 2 October, letting him read the attackers' shared agent boards in real time. The busiest cluster, AIA0001, linked 14 panels to more than 1,100 attack projects run from instructions of fewer than 100 Chinese characters; typical tasks told the agents to find code execution on a shop and plant a web-shell uploader. By late last week the agents had attempted more than 1,200 sites and reached websites belonging to more than 100 companies, including Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins, which Reuters named and which each said the damage was limited. NorForge independently confirmed a live web shell at a large hardware-parts vendor and a compromise at a very large US retailer; targets sat in the United States, Japan, Australia and Canada, with successful intrusions taking about 23 hours of autonomous probing.

3

Unit 42 says Qilin and INC ransomware crews keep exploiting PAN-OS CVE-2026-0257 to bypass MFA

Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 says ransomware operators are continuing to exploit CVE-2026-0257, an authentication bypass in the portal and gateway components of PAN-OS and Prisma Access (CVSS 7.8) that lets an attacker set up a VPN connection without valid credentials. In an update published 19 October, Unit 42 attributed the activity to Spikey Scorpius (Qilin) and Tarnished Scorpius (INC), with public reporting also linking operators using Settra ransomware. After establishing a VPN session the attackers bypass MFA, run internal reconnaissance with dual-use RMM tools, move laterally over RDP and SMB, and stage tooling for credential harvesting, exfiltration and eventually ransomware. Unit 42 says the most significant change in tactics is a direct pivot to the virtualization layer: in one case attackers moved from a compromised gateway to the vCenter management appliance, disabled ESXi's execInstalledOnly control and dumped domain credentials offline from a domain controller's VMDK, evading in-guest EDR, and chained the access with Veeam backup flaws (CVE-2023-27532). Palo Alto patched the flaw in May 2026 and CISA KEV-listed it on 13 May.

4

Attacker backdoors five OVHcloud CLI releases via a hijacked contributor account

An attacker used a hijacked contributor's GitHub account to replace the download archives on five existing releases of OVHcloud's command-line tool on 19 October, swapping in a build designed to steal secrets stored on disk. The malicious archives were live on GitHub for up to 94 minutes, from 12:47 to 14:21 UTC. On each release the attacker replaced six files — the macOS x86_64 and arm64 archives, the Linux x86_64 and arm64 archives, the Windows x86_64 archive and checksums.txt, so the published hashes matched the new binaries; OVHcloud lists v0.11.0 through v0.15.0 as compromised and v0.10.0 as clean. OVHcloud deleted the swapped archives and published a clean, immutable v0.16.0 that evening; the source code, release tag and build pipeline were untouched, the malicious build being a plain `go build` of a later commit. Anyone who ran the official install script, downloaded a release archive, ran a hash-unpinned CI job or used the CLI's own upgrade command in that window should rotate credentials. Homebrew installs, `go install`, source builds and the Docker image were unaffected. OpenSourceMalware attributes the activity to its Glassworm tracking.

5

Unpatched Broadcom Wi-Fi flaw lets an attacker in radio range run code as root on routers

A researcher has disclosed an unpatched flaw in Broadcom's Wi-Fi Protected Setup implementation that lets an attacker within radio range run code as root on Broadcom-based routers without the Wi-Fi password or any pairing step. Quentin Kaiser, who published on 16 October, exploited Broadcom's mishandling of fragmented WPS setup-message reassembly to corrupt router memory, then built primitives to read memory back off the device and to execute his own code. Against an Askey TCG3000 gateway supplied by Orange Belgium he read the stored Wi-Fi PSK off the device over the air and joined the network with it; against a Proximus Sagemcom BBOX3V+ he opened a reverse shell carried inside Wi-Fi frames, needing no outside server. No CVE has been assigned and no fix has been deployed. Older eCos chips (BCM3380, BCM3383, BCM33834) have no usable fix and Kaiser calls them a permanent "forever day"; newer Linux-based chips (BCM3390, BCM65xx, BCM68xx) could be fixed by switching Wi-Fi software. He estimates millions of devices across dozens of vendors are affected and reports no real-world exploitation.

6

Rank Math PRO update window delivered a self-restoring backdoor (CVE-2026-111862)

Copies of Rank Math PRO, the paid tier of the Rank Math SEO plugin for WordPress, downloaded from rankmath.com or delivered as automatic updates between 20:02 UTC on 15 October and 07:12 UTC on 16 October contained malware, Rank Math said in a security notice. Any site that installed and activated one of those copies should be treated as compromised, and the malware can recreate its hidden backdoor whenever someone visits the site. Rank Math removed the affected files and released a clean 3.0.124, but installing it does not remove files, administrator accounts or scheduled tasks the malware may already have placed. Wordfence warns attackers may have left PHP backdoors in other plugin, theme and must-use plugin directories, altered .htaccess so .cache files execute as PHP, and added SSH keys, so owners should assume any password, database credential or API key the site could reach was exposed. Version numbers do not indicate exposure. Wordfence assigned CVE-2026-111862 and rated it 9.8; the free WordPress.org plugin, with more than four million active installs, is not affected.

7

OpenCode Console breach may have exposed customers' AI provider API keys

OpenCode says an intruder reached a legacy system on the night of 16 October and may have exposed the third-party AI provider keys users had stored in OpenCode Console, its optional hosted workspace. Console holds a customer's OpenAI, Anthropic or Google key and uses it to call the provider so team members never see it; the affected group is 1.4% of Console users, all of whom were emailed on 17 October and told to revoke and replace their keys. A provider key is a bearer credential, so deleting it from Console does not revoke it and a Console budget cannot cap its use, because Console only sees requests through its own gateway. Some users report billing they did not run — one posted USD 281 on an OpenAI key and USD 168 on an Anthropic key, another put losses at USD 1,700. OpenCode described the incident as ongoing and "related to a large-scale distillation attack", and separately published distillation-defence guidance; it has not said that any key was copied or used, who was behind it, or how the legacy system was reached. Keys stored locally by the CLI and desktop apps were not involved.

8

Zeabur to shut down after a second breach exposes 321,810 accounts and 3.2 million secrets

Zeabur, a developer hosting and deployment platform, is shutting down after a second security breach in two months. On 14–15 October an attacker used leaked internal credentials — stored in plaintext — and cloud access keys to break into its shared cluster, some dedicated servers and internal systems, and ultimately read the production database, exposing more than 3.2 million environment-variable records and information tied to 321,810 accounts across nine regions. The company also confirmed root sign-ins on 101 Google Cloud servers by 92 users, SSH keys added to 50 servers and 16 customer disk copies attached to attacker virtual machines. Administrative tokens had no expiry, had not been rotated since they were created in January 2024 and were excluded from the post-August rotation, and administrator portals remained reachable from the public internet; Zeabur believes the attacker likely obtained the credentials in the August breach, when a customer's NextChat flaw led to stolen AWS credentials. Services end on 1 December 2026 and close on 31 December; users are urged to rotate exposed secrets, revoke sessions and rebuild compromised servers.

APT28 breached a NATO naval shipbuilder, a military engine maker and a law enforcement agency

Symantec has attributed an espionage campaign against two defence firms and a law enforcement agency in NATO countries to Swallowtail, also known as APT28, Fancy Bear and Forest Blizzard — the GRU-linked group behind the 2016 Democratic National Committee hacks. The targets chosen were a naval shipbuilder and a manufacturer of military aircraft engines; in the shipbuilder intrusion Symantec observed hands-on-keyboard activity as the operators repeatedly returned to a compromised host over about a week to steal credentialed material, which Dick O'Brien of the Symantec Threat Hunter Team said confirms a genuine, deliberate targeting rather than an opportunistic infection. The attackers used the Hookedge backdoor first documented in August by Recorded Future's Insikt Group, which had already seen the same group using it since September 2025 against defence and diplomatic targets in Romania, Spain and Turkey. Symantec also observed a previously undocumented loader, ld.exe, which decrypts its payload in memory and needs a key supplied only at runtime, so researchers could not recover or determine the payload's function. O'Brien tied the operation to broader intelligence collection against European government and defence targets.

Central Ohio Primary Care Physicians breach affects 1.4 million patients

Central Ohio Primary Care Physicians (COPC), one of the largest independent physician-owned primary care groups in the United States with more than 80 locations across Central Ohio, has disclosed a breach affecting approximately 1.4 million current and former patients after a voice-phishing (vishing) attack in August 2026 yielded third-party access to internal systems. The unauthorised access was detected and blocked on 25 August 2026, but the investigation confirmed that files were exfiltrated containing patient names, addresses, Social Security numbers, financial information, health information and other identifiers, covering patients past and present. Notification letters are being mailed with complimentary credit monitoring, identity-theft protection and dark-web monitoring, and the group says it has found no misuse of the data to date. No threat group was named in the breach notices, but the HIPAA Journal reports the incident appears to be the work of the Chaos ransomware group, which listed the practice on its dark-web leak site and threatened to publish the stolen data before the listing was withdrawn. It is one of the largest US patient-data breaches disclosed this year and shows vishing remains an effective first-stage route into healthcare networks.

$80 million settlement proposed to resolve 62.2 million-record Conduent breach litigation

A proposed US$80 million settlement would resolve the consolidated class action against Conduent Business Services LLC, Conduent State & Local Solutions Inc and Conduent Incorporated over a 2024 breach in which an unauthorised party first entered the network on 21 October 2024 and maintained access until 13 January 2025, with the SafePay ransomware group claiming 8.5 terabytes of data exfiltrated. Conduent's forensic investigation determined the personal and protected health information of approximately 62.2 million individuals was stolen — names, addresses, dates of birth, medical and health-insurance information and Social Security numbers — the largest healthcare data breach reported in 2025 and the third-largest of all time. Filed in the US District Court for the District of New Jersey after mediation, the deal splits the class into Subclass A (~40 million, data held in complex backup files) and Subclass B (~22 million, readily accessible data); all class members may claim a year of single-bureau credit monitoring (valued at $179.40) and up to $3,500 in documented losses, while Subclass B members and California residents are also eligible for pro rata cash payments. Attorneys are seeking 34 per cent of the fund.

California warns all public school students after ETS contractor breach

California's Department of Education (CDE) has begun notifying families that assessment contractor Educational Testing Service (ETS) suffered a months-long breach in which hackers copied sensitive student records through a secure file transfer protocol site between 22 May and 18 September 2026. The data covers essentially every child enrolled in California public schools over the past three years — names, dates of birth, residential addresses, schools, districts and grade levels, statewide and local student identifiers, gender, race or ethnicity, primary language and country of origin, English-learner status, disability and Section 504 status, testing accommodations, Title I and McKinney-Vento eligibility, and assessment registration, participation and results — plus students who left a California school since 2006 without a confirmed transfer. ETS, which runs the CAASPP and ELPAC exams for a system that enrolled more than 5.7 million students in the 2025 school year, discovered the breach on 25 September, disabled the abused account, took the site offline and rebuilt the platform, and has paused normal enrolment data processing and some testing while systems are assessed. No hacking group had claimed responsibility as of 20 October. It is the broadest exposure of K-12 pupil data disclosed this year.

Anti-Putin messages disrupt nationwide school assessments in Russia

Russia's education watchdog Rosobrnadzor says it has restored its online systems after a cyberattack forced it to suspend nationwide school assessments and replaced questionnaires on its Edutest platform with anti-government messages urging students to join an underground movement against President Vladimir Putin. 1

Freight fraud shifts to email takeover and AI-forged credentials heading into peak season

Freight fraud is moving from physical cargo theft to identity-based deception, according to a FreightWaves interview with E3 Group co-founder and CEO Aqil Naeem. Reported Q2 2026 cargo theft incidents fell roughly a quarter year on year, but losses over the same period more than doubled to US$304.6 million, with the average theft now about US$564,000 (Verisk), and industry estimates suggest only about 10% of incidents are ever reported at all. Naeem attributes the shift to cheap impersonation: magnetic carrier decals and fake plates ordered overnight, carrier email logins bought on the dark web, and AI-generated photographs of trucks and CDLs. The pattern E3 sees repeating is phishing campaigns and remote-access malware used to take over carrier business email and compliance accounts, so fraudulent pickup instructions arrive from the real inbox and a fictitious pickup can be run remotely without anyone entering the yard. E3's verification service cross-checks the CDL barcode against the card front, VINs across cab card, door-jamb sticker and broker records, and cell-tower, GPS and IP location data, flagging a verification link opened in the wrong state; Naeem calls November and December the highest-risk months. Why it matters: the weak point in freight has moved from the trailer lock to the mailbox and the credential, so brokers and carriers must verify the person behind the wheel, not the paperwork.

Rapid7 discloses critical unauthenticated RCE chain in Eptura Archibus workplace platform

Rapid7 has published three chained vulnerabilities in Eptura Archibus, the workplace and real-estate management platform that tracks assets, maintenance work orders, leases and desk or room reservations, and which Eptura says was used by more than 16,000 companies and 40% of the Fortune 500 as of 2023. CVE-2026-107186 (CVSS 8.6) abuses Archibus Web Central's Java Direct Web Remoting endpoint: the pre-authentication isPasswordUppercase method loads a core user session onto the thread, and a second batched call to runWorkflowRule then runs workflow rules under that session with no credentials. Chained with it, CVE-2026-107187 (CVSS 8.5) reads arbitrary database tables through the AbCommonResources-getDataRecords workflow — Rapid7 extracted user names and passwords from the afm_users table — while CVE-2026-107188 (CVSS 9.6, critical) allows JSP/JSPX uploads via export reports, yielding command execution as NT AUTHORITY\Local Service. Rapid7 found the flaws through continuous red teaming and says all three are fixed, and CISA's SSVC record lists no known exploitation. Why it matters: facility and property teams run Archibus inside corporate networks, so an internet-facing instance is a direct path to stored credentials and code execution on a building-management platform.

Connecticut asks regulator to probe utility AI use as FERC warns of new grid access risks

Connecticut Attorney General William Tong and Consumer Counsel Claire Coleman petitioned the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority to investigate how the state's electric, gas and water utilities deploy artificial intelligence and what safeguards protect their systems. Why it matters: AI tooling is arriving on grid networks inside vendor products faster than utility compliance and oversight programmes have adapted.

Fraud ring clones bank support pages to push remote-access tools

British threat-intelligence firm Lab539 has detailed a phone-led fraud operation, live since at least March 2026, that builds convincing copies of banks' customer-support sites and talks victims into installing legitimate remote-support software. The campaign spans more than 380 registered domains, including cloned help pages for Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, Revolut and Santander, and adds fake workflow buttons — "Verify Call", "Live Chat", "Generate Case ID", "Security Check" and "Fraud Report" — that hand the attacker one-time remote access. The Live Chat path downloads a genuine AnyDesk installer while other chains deliver custom-signed payloads such as verify_call.exe, and researchers classify the technique as telephone-oriented attack delivery (TOAD), using lures that contain no links or attachments and so slip past email gateways. Why it matters: the operation weaponises bank-brand trust rather than a software flaw, so allow-listing and signature-based defences will not stop it — only customer-side call-verification discipline and takedown of the cloned estate will.

Ahold Delhaize confirms cyberattack forced systems offline across US and European brands

Ahold Delhaize said in a 19 October update that "malicious actions of others" caused the IT incident it first disclosed on 12 October, which forced the Dutch grocery group to take systems offline across most of its US and European brands. Albert Heijn, Etos and Gall & Gall websites in the Netherlands are back online, but Albert Heijn home deliveries were not expected to resume until Friday 23 October, while e-commerce for Ahold Delhaize USA brands including Food Lion and Stop & Shop remained down and was expected back "in the coming days". Belgian e-commerce and Central and South-Eastern Europe services are restored. The company filed a formal police report with Dutch police and notified the Dutch National Cyber Security Centre within 24 hours of detecting the activity, and says its investigation has so far found no evidence that sensitive customer, associate or partner data was stolen. CEO Frans Muller apologised for the disruption. With 9,551 stores, 384,000 employees and 77 million weekly customers, this is the group's second major cyber incident since the November 2024 attack claimed by INC Ransom, in which 2.2 million people's data was stolen.

23andMe successor to pay California US$6 million over genetic-data breach

California has settled its claims against 23andMe — now owned by the non-profit TTAM Research Institute — for US$6 million, resolving alleged violations of state law over the 2023 credential-stuffing breach that exposed the personal and genetic data of about 6.9 million people, including 855,000 Californians. Attorney General Rob Bonta's office opted out of the US$18 million multistate settlement agreed in July with 42 other attorneys general; under the new deal the state drops its appeals of a bankruptcy-court ruling that barred it from suing TTAM over the breach after the company's US$305 million sale. Customers retain the right to demand deletion of their personal and genetic data. 23andMe separately paid US$46.75 million to settle consumer class actions, approved in July 2026. Why it matters: the settlement closes the costliest privacy chapter of the breach while establishing that a post-bankruptcy asset sale does not extinguish state genetic-privacy enforcement — and it preserves consumer deletion rights over data now held by a different legal entity.

US$80 million settlement proposed in Conduent data breach litigation

A US$80 million settlement has been proposed to resolve the consolidated class action against Conduent Business Services over its 2024 breach, in which an unauthorised third party held access from 21 October 2024 to 13 January 2025 and the SafePay ransomware group claimed to have exfiltrated 8.5 terabytes. Conduent's forensics put the toll at roughly 62.2 million people — names, addresses, dates of birth, medical and health-insurance information and Social Security numbers — the largest healthcare data breach reported in 2025 and the third-largest of all time. Filed in the US District Court for the District of New Jersey after mediation, the deal splits claimants into Subclass A (about 40 million, whose data sat in complex backups) and Subclass B (about 22 million, in readily accessible form). Members get one year of single-bureau credit monitoring valued at US$179.40 and up to US$3,500 in documented losses, with Subclass B also eligible for a pro-rata payment; counsel seek 34% of the fund. Court approval is still pending.

FBI and CISA publish joint #StopRansomware advisory on SafePay's 600-plus victims

The FBI, CISA, the US Secret Service, Germany's Landeskriminalamt Rheinland-Pfalz, the UK National Crime Agency and the UK North West Regional Organised Crime Unit released joint advisory AA26-293A on 20 October detailing SafePay, which the authoring agencies call one of the most active ransomware groups of 2026 and link to at least 600 separate victims across critical infrastructure in North America, South America, Europe and Australia since August 2024. Initial access came from credential exposure, brute-force password attacks (T1110) and VPN accounts reached through Mullvad IP addresses, exploiting CVE-2020-3259, CVE-2023-20269, CVE-2023-27532, CVE-2024-40711 and CVE-2024-40766 across edge devices and backup infrastructure. SafePay actors escalate to administrative accounts, run PsExec, WinRM, ShareFinder.ps1 and SharpShares.exe, disable security software, exfiltrate via Rclone, WinSCP and OneDrive, halt the Volume Shadow Copy Service, factory-reset NAS backups, then encrypt with a ChaCha20/RSA hybrid that appends the .SafePay extension. Aimed explicitly at federal, state, local, tribal and critical-infrastructure operators, the advisory is now the authoritative TTP and indicator set for a group with confirmed Australian victims.

CISA adds six exploited vulnerabilities to KEV, including four Plex CVEs and two federal-mandate additions

CISA added six vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 20 October on evidence of active exploitation: Plex Media Server CVE-2026-96652, CVE-2026-96654, CVE-2026-96655 and CVE-2026-96656 (server-side request forgery, improper neutralisation of encoded URI schemes, and external control of file name or path); EG Secure Solutions SiteGuard Server Edition OS command injection CVE-2026-106931; and Siemens Software Center uncontrolled search path element CVE-2026-110936. Under Binding Operational Directive 26-04, federal civilian executive branch agencies must prioritise rapid remediation of KEV-listed flaws on publicly exposed assets that grant total control post-exploitation, and must check whether systems were compromised before the patch was applied. The SiteGuard flaw — already exploited in the wild with root/SYSTEM privileges — was covered in the 19 October edition; what is new is its formal KEV listing a day later, alongside four Plex and one Siemens entry. Six additions in one day resets the remediation clock for every agency running Plex, SiteGuard or Siemens Software Center.

DHS bans DeepSeek from US federal civilian agencies under acquisition supply-chain rules

A Department of Homeland Security notice published in the Federal Register on 20 October bars civilian federal agencies from buying or using any information technology products and services from Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence and its affiliates, citing the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Act and a Federal Acquisition Security Council review. The notice reproduces a 26 June exclusion order from DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin, indicating the ban has been in force for months. Mullin said DeepSeek models present "multiple threat vectors": data stored on China-based servers where national security laws compel disclosure, collection of personally identifiable information, and prompt-and-response handling that "may result in the transfer of sensitive data" to the Chinese government. Agencies must exclude DeepSeek from future procurements and remove pre-existing versions; exceptions require a 60-day advance request with compelling justification. DHS encourages agencies to monitor traffic, strengthen logging and data-exfiltration controls, and flag anomalous activity in the interim. DeepSeek is the only Chinese model named, with Moonshot AI (Kimi) and Alibaba (Qwen) also flagged by Washington.

Key to this page

Two pill families appear in the text and they answer different questions. A CVE pill colours severity — a measured CVSS band from the National Vulnerability Database. A threat-actor pill colours attribution confidence — how well-corroborated the naming is, which is a claim rather than a measurement. Both are links: a CVE opens the ATT&CK matrix or its wiki page, an actor opens its wiki page.

CVE identifiers

  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNCritical · CVSS 9.0+
  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNHigh · CVSS 7.0–8.9
  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNMedium · CVSS 4.0–6.9
  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNLow · below 4.0
  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNNo severity resolved — not the same as low
