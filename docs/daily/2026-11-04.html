Executive Summary

Binance published a post-mortem of the record US$6.3 billion theft, describing a months-long North Korean campaign that began with a fake job interview and a tampered Homebrew installation — a day after Pyongyang publicly denied any role; Marathon Health notified state attorneys general that a vishing attack on an employee led to exposure of data on more than 5.7 million people, which would make it the fourth-largest healthcare breach of 2026 in the US; and Bitdefender documented a suspected agentic-AI ransomware operation that encrypted 37 machines across a British school estate, the first time an AI-automated kill chain has been described inside a real intrusion. CISA assigned CVE-2026-119977 to the WordPress Core race condition that drew nearly 63,000 exploit attempts last week and added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, and researchers confirmed pre-authentication exploitation attempts against a CVSS 10.0 Siemens industrial-IoT flaw — a day in which artificial intelligence moved from assisting attackers to operating the intrusion itself.

The China-nexus XCPE botnet that Google dismantled last week infected Australian households at scale: Plume Security Labs mapped infection clusters around Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide, with the Videostrong-built backdoor found in MECOOL and Skyworth Android TV boxes sold locally, and Google Threat Intelligence Group also detected the malware in telehealth devices, karaoke machines and digital signage — a reminder that consumer-entertainment hardware carries the same firmware-supply risk as enterprise gear, and that such devices sit inside home networks alongside work-from-home endpoints. ACSC published nothing new across its three listings between 27 October and this run — the newest items remain the ASD *Cyber Threat Report 2025-26* news release (27 October) and the Fortinet alert (23 October) — so the week's most locally actionable guidance is unchanged: the WordPress Core race now assigned CVE-2026-119977 underpins a large share of Australian small-business and government web estates, and the Siemens SIMATIC IoT2050 exploitation observed by VulnCheck maps directly onto ACSC's "protecting operational technology and edge devices" alert (14 October). No Australian organisation appeared as a named victim in the window.

The week to 4 November (28 October–3 November) logged 100 stories across the pipeline: 31 zero-day/vulnerability items, 25 breach/leak disclosures, 18 malware analyses and 9 ransomware events, with reporting led by the US (55), global coverage (26) and Australia (14). Three threads define the period. First, AI has shifted from assisting attackers to operating the intrusion: ARTEX — the Chinese-developed offensive framework — produced its first named corporate victim in WorkOS, with blockchain provider Chainstack separately assessing its breach as overlapping the same campaign; Bitdefender documented a 40–100-second command loop in a UK school ransom attack that it believes was agentic; Google put a figure of up to US$1 million on LLMjacking losses; and Binarly assessed the Zyxel GS1900 exploit it recovered as LLM-generated — four independent lines, on top of last week's VOLTA, Langflow and AI-SDK-npm disclosures, all converging on AI-driven initial access. Second, the exploitation wave has moved onto OT and legacy industrial control gear: a CVSS 10.0 flaw in Siemens' Node-RED-based IoT gateway drew working exploit attempts within two months of its advisory, a pre-authentication Zyxel switch zero-day survived a KEV-listed patch that fixed a different bug (all ten "fixed" releases remained vulnerable), and the European cold-chain campaign reached British meat suppliers through Copeland XWEB controllers whose vendor confirmed the exposure — while UK NCSC's annual report called the state of basic cyber hygiene "the foothills" of an expected rise in incidents. Third, attribution is being contested in public in ways that matter for trust in disclosures: North Korea's Kim Yo Jong rejected the Binance attribution through state media, Russia publicly dismantled its own VasyGrek cybercrime group with Kaspersky's help, and German lawmakers surfaced among Apple's spyware-warning recipients days before the Pall Mall Process meeting in Paris (11–12 November) where voluntary industry guidelines on spyware are due — the disclosure battles now run alongside the operations they describe.

Binance details US$6.3 billion theft: months-long campaign, fake job interviews and a tampered Homebrew install, as North Korea denies role

Binance's post-mortem of last week's record theft describes a "sophisticated, multi-stage campaign" running since June — not an isolated breach. The chain started when an engineer downloaded a tampered tool while installing a popular AI coding product via Homebrew, and a separate engineer was recruited through a fake job interview in which the "technical assignment" was a malicious code repository downloaded during the second interview. Attackers then compromised internal AI tooling, moved laterally to two more devices by 20 October, reached critical wallet tooling by 29 October, and used credentials from the breached employees to trigger the first outflows — almost US$400 million through an internal wallet-signing service, then US$1.4 billion in bitcoin, then 50 further transactions totalling US$6.34 billion. About US$2.85 billion was frozen with industry coordination. Binance says it detected and stopped 57 separate North Korean attempts in 2026. Kim Yo Jong denied the country's role as "absurd"; the FBI had already attributed the theft to TraderTraitor, while Elliptic tracked some 12,000 wallets across 11 blockchains with funds moved roughly every 25 seconds.

2

Chainstack discloses breach exposing customer data and password hashes, links it to ARTEX campaign

Blockchain infrastructure provider Chainstack disclosed that an external attacker breached a development environment on 26 October through a third-party component and escalated into "some parts of our production environment" before being detected and cut off on 31 October. The intruder accessed a database holding organisation names, account holder names, member email addresses and password hashes, and also reached systems storing node credentials — Chainstack found no evidence the node credentials were read but is treating them as exposed and has told customers to rotate node credentials, regenerate platform API keys and change reused passwords. The company declined specifics about the attack, but the incident is assessed as overlapping the campaign in which a China-linked cluster has used the ARTEX AI framework against high-value targets — the same infrastructure WorkOS blamed on Tuesday. Chainstack told customers to watch for phishing that abuses the exposed details.

3

BTCPay Server urges upgrade to 2.4.7 after Bitcoin and Litecoin drains from hot wallets

BTCPay Server, the self-hosted open-source cryptocurrency payment processor, urged operators to upgrade after Bitcoin and Litecoin were stolen from hot wallets on instances running version 2.4.1 (released 23 July 2026) or earlier. The project says the attackers used "a different method" from the vulnerability fixed in 2.4.2 (7 August), and that operators on affected versions should move to a fresh server with a new wallet even if no theft has been noticed — treating the whole machine and its secrets as compromised. Reports of drains began surfacing around 1 November; BTCPay Server said there were no "credible reports" of hot-wallet losses on the current 2.4.7 release, which fixes one critical and several medium-severity vulnerabilities identified via Anthropic's OSS scanner. Hosting providers have been shutting down older instances automatically, and the project recommends keeping only operational funds on server-held hot wallets.

Marathon Health data breach reportedly affects 5.7 million individuals

Indianapolis-based employer-sponsored healthcare provider Marathon Health told state attorneys general that a breach of 24 August 2026 affected almost 5.7 million people — potentially the fourth-largest healthcare data breach of 2026 reported by a HIPAA-regulated entity. An attacker posed as a Marathon IT worker and tricked an employee into disclosing credentials, which provided access to a cloud-hosted application from which data was exported within a few hours. Impacted data includes names, contact information, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, and — per Texas and Vermont notifications — medical and health insurance information. Marathon's review completed 9 September and notification letters began mailing 20 October, with 12 or 24 months of credit monitoring offered. State AG figures vary — Texas 5,684,735 per law-firm reporting (234,534 per the state's own site), Massachusetts 29,075, Vermont 8,858 — and the breach is not yet listed on the HHS OCR portal. ShinyHunters and Scattered Spider use the same IT-impersonation tactic but neither has claimed the attack.

Harvard data breach exposed information of 851,000 accounts, HIBP confirms

Have I Been Pwned confirmed on Tuesday that data tied to Harvard University's breach of its Alumni Affairs and Development systems in November 2025 exposes 851,000 unique email addresses and extensive profile data for donors, alumni, parents and some faculty, staff and students — names, emails, dates of birth, phone numbers, physical addresses and social-media profile data. The extortion gang ShinyHunters claimed the breach and leaked a 1.1 GB archive on its dark-web leak site in early February after Harvard declined to pay a ransom. Harvard's CIO said the breached systems did not store Social Security numbers, passwords, payment card or financial information. Two other Ivy League universities — Princeton and Pennsylvania — disclosed parallel donor-data breaches in November 2025, and Harvard separately told BleepingComputer in October 2025 it was investigating a second breach after Clop claimed to have used an Oracle E-Business Suite zero-day. The scale confirmation ends a year of uncertainty about how many records were caught in the initial fundraising-systems intrusion.

2

UK school estate encrypted by 3AM ransomware in suspected agentic-AI attack

A 3AM ransomware affiliate encrypted around 37 of nearly 50 machines across a British school estate, and Bitdefender believes the operator may have used AI-generated commands and an automated agentic loop: a sequence of repeated but slightly modified commands issued at roughly regular 40–100-second intervals, which the researchers say "looked similar to an agentic loop" (they could not inspect the attacker's machine to prove which model, if any, was involved). The activity ran across 10–19 September with the initial access method never established. The chain included a modified public PowerShell tool to extract and decrypt credentials stored on three Veeam backup servers, a Chrome credential-database grab, Mimikatz and LSASS memory dumping, mapping of domain relationships, and creation of two backdoor domain-admin accounts named "library" and "svc_bkpmon". The attacker stole finance data, configured two-way trusts across three previously separate Active Directory domains to expand reach, then attempted to disable Sophos and Bitdefender protection, delete system-state backups and deploy 3AM's Rust encryptor. 3AM emerged in 2023 and has been linked to Conti and Royal operators.

1

CISA assigns CVE-2026-119977 to WordPress Core race and adds it to the KEV catalog

CISA added CVE-2026-119977 — a time-of-check/time-of-use race condition in WordPress Core — to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 3 November based on evidence of active exploitation, closing the loop on the WordPress Core flaw that Wordfence reported last week had drawn nearly 63,000 probe attempts within 24 hours and that carried no CVE identifier at the time. BOD 26-04 requires US federal civilian agencies to prioritise rapid remediation of KEV-listed vulnerabilities on exposed assets, and CISA encouraged all organisations to apply risk-based patching. The underlying issue is in WordPress's full-package core upgrade path, where an unauthenticated attacker who hits a predictable staging directory at the right moment can interact with a freshly unpacked installer and reach code execution. Because WordPress powers a large share of the public web — including much Australian small-business and government estate — the CVE assignment and KEV listing effectively confirm an officially recognised, in-the-wild exploit target and should move administrators from monitoring to urgent patching.

German lawmakers and foreign-policy staff among Apple mercenary-spyware warning recipients; BSI tells them to assume infection

Multiple German lawmakers, federal ministry staffers and journalists who travelled with Chancellor Friedrich Merz's delegation through Asia received Apple mercenary-spyware threat notifications in last week's round affecting people in 99 countries, according to German information-security agency BSI and Der Spiegel. BSI sent a letter to recipients telling them to assume their devices are infected and immediately replace them, within 24 hours of Apple's alerts. Green Party co-leader Franziska Brantner, a Bundestag European Affairs Committee member, confirmed publicly she was among the recipients; a Lebanese filmmaker and a Turkey-based journalist were the first to publicly confirm earlier. European Parliament member Hannah Neumann called it "very different this time", describing "something big" against the German political elite. Citizen Lab's John Scott-Railton said the timing — ahead of the French–British Pall Mall Process meeting in Paris on 11–12 November, where voluntary spyware-industry guidelines are due — makes Germany the latest proof of "an untreated mercenary spyware epidemic" in Europe.

1

WorkOS says attacker used ARTEX AI framework to breach internal sales application

Developer-platform company WorkOS disclosed an incident in which an attacker used an AI offensive-security framework to automate reconnaissance and exploitation, accessed an internal application used by its sales team on 30 October. Compromised data covers business contacts (names, emails, job titles, company affiliations from CRM systems), customer account histories, summaries and excerpts of sales calls, support discussions and customer Slack conversations, and business relationship information — but WorkOS says no production services or app data were accessed. WorkOS underpins enterprise authentication, SSO and identity for at least 3,000 companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, Cursor, Perplexity, Snowflake, Plaid and Netlify; founder Michael Grinich attributed the operation to ARTEX, the China-developed open-source AI penetration-testing suite that went private after earlier attacks on South Korean banks. The breach is the first named corporate victim of the ARTEX framework that CrowdStrike last week linked to 200+ targets, and sits alongside Chainstack's assessment that its own breach overlapped the same campaign.

2

Russia-aligned Gorbag group exploits WinRAR flaw to deploy data-stealing malware against Ukraine

ESET documented Gorbag, a Russia-aligned threat actor active since at least February 2025 that has focused exclusively on Ukrainian targets, with a clear preference for law enforcement and justice entities, local administrations near the front line and the defence sector. A central tool is GIFTEDCROOK, a C/C++ information stealer that harvests browser cookies, browsing history and authentication data from Chrome, Edge and Firefox; Trend Micro earlier attributed the group to attacks weaponising the now-patched WinRAR vulnerability CVE-2025-8088 to trigger a DLL side-loading chain that executes GIFTEDCROOK. The stealer also collects VPN configurations and documents of interest, leading ESET to assess Gorbag may be acting as an initial access broker feeding stolen access to other actors — "Gorbag may represent only the first stage of a longer intrusion chain". Delivery uses spear-phishing with government- and tax-themed lures, including macro-enabled spreadsheets. Gorbag overlaps clusters tracked by CERT-UA as UAC-0226 and by Trend Micro as SHADOW-EARTH-066, and its targeting "closely align[s] with Russia's wartime intelligence priorities".

Defence contractors ILC Dover and ILC Astrospace pay US$13 million over cybersecurity contract failures

ILC — the combined name for ILC Dover and ILC Astrospace — agreed to pay US$13 million to resolve False Claims Act allegations over its contracts with the US Army, Missile Defense Agency and NASA, after the government alleged the Delaware-based defence and aerospace firm knowingly submitted false payment claims while failing to meet contracted cybersecurity obligations for a period spanning 2018–2024. The government cited use of a third-party cloud-storage provider without proper safeguards and a failure to develop and update mandated security plans for covered information systems. Former director of cybersecurity Philip Root, the whistleblower, receives US$2.35 million. Parent Ingersoll Rand said it discovered the issues after acquiring ILC Dover in June 2024 and was compliant by October 2024. The settlement is among the larger since the Justice Department began using the False Claims Act against contractors over cyber shortfalls in 2022, and it lands amid a pending regulatory overhaul of CUI handling that could widen exposure for government suppliers.

Attackers exploit CVSS 10.0 flaw in Siemens SIMATIC IoT2050 to plant remote-command backdoors

VulnCheck reported pre-authentication exploitation attempts against CVE-2026-58115 (CVSS 10.0), a missing-authentication vulnerability in the Node-RED HTTP interface of Siemens SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced industrial gateways running Industrial OS with Node-RED installed. Siemens' August advisory describes it as allowing an unauthenticated remote attacker to create malicious flows and execute arbitrary code on the underlying server with maximum privileges, remediated in version 4.3.4.1 or later. From 30 October, VulnCheck observed attackers attempting to weaponise the flaw against its honeypots in a complete, three-layer-encoded exploit that deployed a Node-RED flow, triggered it automatically and pulled a Linux botnet binary (AMD64) from an attacker-controlled server via wget with a curl fallback — evidence of active exploitation rather than mere scanning. Siemens recommends uninstalling Node-RED or hardening the installation with HTTPS, credential-based or OAuth/OpenID authentication on the editor and admin API, and basic authentication on HTTP nodes. The case follows ACSC's repeated guidance on securing operational technology and edge devices exposed to the internet.

1

Chinese Videostrong botnet hit Australian Android TV boxes, researchers map city clusters

The XCPE botnet dismantled by Google and industry partners last week reached Australian households at scale: Plume Security Labs mapped infection clusters around Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide from the malware's command traffic. Shenzhen-based Videostrong Technology built the "x.cpe" backdoor into its Android TV streaming-box firmware and sold access to criminal affiliates, Google Threat Intelligence Group assessed with high confidence; researchers at Synthient found the same malware in boxes sold under the MECOOL brand by listed Chinese company Skyworth Technology. The backdoor ran with system-level privileges, reinstalled itself if removed and disabled Play Protect verification, and operators captured live screenshots, injected keystrokes, stole account information and opened reverse shells — beyond silent app installs for ad fraud and residential-proxy exit traffic. GTIG also found the malware in telehealth devices, karaoke machines and digital signage, and its partners took down command servers for more than a dozen malware families, cutting off at least 10 million more compromised devices. Google barred Videostrong from building certified Android devices and advised buyers to stick to reputable manufacturers.

2

Europe's refrigeration attacks hit British meat suppliers via vulnerable Copeland XWEB controllers

Three British meat suppliers and a fruit-and-vegetable wholesaler were caught in Monday's Europe-wide wave of attacks on supermarket refrigeration, the Association of Independent Meat Suppliers said, with members attacked through vulnerable Copeland XWEB supervisory controllers. Copeland confirmed certain XWEB monitoring solutions are impacted and said it had re-communicated patching and security protocols. A security note shared among AIMS members says XWEB firmware 1.12.1 and earlier carried "severe authentication bypass vulnerabilities that allow hackers to take control without a password"; researchers at Claroty disclosed almost two dozen XWEB vulnerabilities in August. Langridge Organic's managing director said he interrupted a live hacking event when he physically disconnected a fridge after watching it be placed in defrost mode. No stock was lost among the affected members, and the attackers remain unidentified — Belgium's cybersecurity agency said identification "may never be possible". UK NCSC, whose annual report launched the same day, called the attacks "the foothills" of an expected rise in incidents, while AIMS urged bringing food supply chains under critical-infrastructure designation and the UK Cyber Security and Resilience Bill.

Europol dismantles investment-fraud scheme in 17 countries, seizes 100+ servers

Europol announced seven arrests and the seizure of more than 100 servers across 17 countries in a coordinated takedown of an investment-fraud operation linked to a company run from Cyprus and Israel with call centres in Albania, Kosovo and North Macedonia. Officials said the scheme claimed potential victims in more than 50 countries with estimated losses of at least US$4.5 million. Operators used affiliate-marketing advertisements to drive traffic to fraudulent cryptocurrency, forex and stock-trading platforms, displayed fictitious profits and used sustained psychological pressure to convince victims to invest more, then blocked withdrawal requests until contact ceased. The company is alleged to have supplied cybercrime groups with the trading websites and call-centre infrastructure used to deceive victims. Authorities were tipped off in 2022 when a German couple reported being unable to withdraw funds; two arrests that year led to the searches and further seizures in 2025. The seven arrested on Tuesday face extradition to Germany, with support from agencies in Albania, Cyprus, Finland, Germany, Israel, Kosovo, North Macedonia and Poland.

Russia dismantles VasyGrek cybercrime group infrastructure, detains three suspects

Russian authorities arrested three suspected members of VasyGrek (also known as Fluffy Wolf), a Russian-speaking hacktivist-turned-cybercriminal group active since at least 2016, in a rare public Russian crackdown on a domestic hacking operation. The Russian Interior Ministry said police dismantled the group's infrastructure with assistance from Kaspersky; the alleged leader is under house arrest and two accomplices were released under travel restrictions. VasyGrek targeted Russian companies with phishing emails impersonating financial and accounting services, using remote-administration tools bought on dark-web marketplaces to access victims' computers and online banking — in one documented case transferring more than 660,000 rubles (about US$8,000) from a company account. Kaspersky said it detected more than 100 attributed attack attempts between January and September 2026 against organisations in Russia, Belarus and Kazakhstan, most frequently metallurgical companies, raw-material and industrial-goods suppliers and government agencies, with healthcare, oil and gas, energy, finance and agriculture also targeted. The suspects face up to 10 years' imprisonment on theft and malware charges; officials noted authorities in Russia, the US and Europe had all taken interest in the group.

Global (Macro) 3 stories

1

Zyxel GS1900 pre-authentication RCE (CVE-2026-107441) confirmed as a zero-day that survived Zyxel's KEV-listed patch

Binarly recovered a working pre-authentication remote-code-execution exploit for Zyxel GS1900 series network switches from the same JITTERLY/SIXZUT command-and-control infrastructure behind a Gitea-focused campaign, confirming the bug as a distinct zero-day assigned CVE-2026-107441 (BRLY-2026-057). The exploit targets `/cgi-bin/dispatcher.cgi` and an out-of-bounds write: the request parser neither checks the size of each `key=value` pair nor the parameter counter, letting an attacker write to a wide memory range and either redirect `strcmp` in the GOT to `system` or run a ROP chain — both modes verified on real hardware. Critically, Zyxel's June 2026 patch for CVE-2026-7273 (a stack-based buffer overflow that CISA added to the KEV catalog on 21 September) did not fix this issue; Binarly confirmed all ten "fixed" releases remained vulnerable, and Zyxel treated the report as a separate CVE, fixed on 22 October with advisories published 3 November. Binarly assesses the recovered exploit as likely LLM-generated — a reminder that a KEV-listed patch is no guarantee the specific exploit in the wild is covered.

2

cPGuard update channel hijacked to backdoor customers' Linux servers and collect credentials

An attacker who gained access through compromised credentials broke into the update system of cPGuard — a security product that audits TLS/email server hardening on Linux — on 2 November at 02:06 UTC and used it to push a malicious script to customers' servers running versions 5.88.12 through 5.88.19. The script installed a backdoor and collected `/etc/shadow` and `/etc/passwd`, root's MariaDB/MySQL configuration, root shell history and the SSH `authorized_keys` and `known_hosts` files; maker OPSSHIELD says the attacker has since taken some of that data. The activity was detected within 12 hours, and cPGuard 5.88.25 and later remove the backdoor and the files it left — but credentials the script copied may still be valid, so any affected server also needs new passwords and keys. OPSSHIELD has cleared its infrastructure credentials and is still investigating how the attacker obtained access; it has not said how many servers installed an affected version. The incident is the latest supply-chain compromise of a software-update channel, and administrators should audit install logs for affected versions.

3

Malicious open-source packages include WhatsApp-library impersonators and a PyPI package with no safe versions

OpenSourceMalware's verified archive flagged a fresh set of malicious open-source assets. The strongest pattern is WhatsApp-library impersonation on npm — @finzzcloud/baileys and @ubedsockets/baileys, packages that mimic the widely used real Baileys WhatsApp API library and would execute-on-import to steal credentials or cloud access in any build that pulls them in. On PyPI, pyprig (high severity, all versions) is confirmed malicious; @levvicode/eslint-config and @ifasn/mmd-doc-mcp add further malicious npm entries, all carrying no safe version. The OSV advisory for pyprig (MAL-2026-18490) is the authoritative record for detection. Because the affected version spans are effectively "all versions" of each malicious name, blocking by exact version is unreliable — teams should inventory dependency trees for these names and any typosquat of a well-known library (especially the Baileys WhatsApp API), rotate secrets touched by affected builds and monitor for unexpected cloud or credential activity. The cluster is a same-shape campaign pattern rather than N independent incidents.
