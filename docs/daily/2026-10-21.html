Executive Summary

The week's most widely felt flaw is also its least detailed: the PHP development team has told administrators to prepare for emergency releases at 14:00 UTC on 21 October for two remote code execution vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, each rated 9.2 under CVSS v4.0, with the researchers at watchTowr telling BleepingComputer they affect PHP versions 5 through 8 and were meant to ship on 22 October before technical detail leaked — leaving an unpatched window open in the language that runs WordPress and much of the public web. Second, a supply-chain investigation named the actor behind a months-long update-channel hijack: AOMEI's promotional-popup channel was tampered with between April and July 2026, Google's Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant attribute the campaign to UNC7017 and say it evaded point-in-time triage by restoring the files it altered, the payloads were a new downloader and backdoor, and AOMEI told The Record it has still not identified or notified the customers who were infected — across nearly a dozen countries including Australia. Third, a public proof-of-concept landed for the FortiSIEM flaw CVE-2026-106173 (CVSS 9.4), and GhostAction returned with a second stage that survives workflow removal: 94 compromised GitHub repositories carrying a fake font file that is in fact a credential stealer. Two things follow. First, the pre-patch window is now the story as much as the flaw: a leaked coordinated-disclosure timeline gave attackers a day's head start on PHP ahead of a fleet-wide fix, and Horizon3's FortiSIEM proof-of-concept converts a patched-but-unexploited 9.4 into a short remediation clock for every exposed instance. Second, the realised supply-chain losses this week are in restoration and notification, not in exploitation: AOMEI's customers still do not know whether they were hit because the attacker erased its tracks, and GhostAction's payload is designed to keep working after the workflow that delivered it is gone. The through-line for defenders is remediation hygiene — hash-pinned installs, absence of evidence treated as absence only where triage is continuous rather than point-in-time, and credential rotation as the default response to any channel compromise.

There is no single direct Australian incident in the 19–21 October window, so the local read is patching pressure plus named Australian victims in two supply-chain campaigns. The most AU-relevant story is FortiSIEM CVE-2026-106173 (CVSS 9.4): a path-traversal file-write escalating to unauthenticated remote code execution over the gRPC service on TCP port 27918, patched last week — FortiSIEM 7.5.0 requires upgrading to 7.5.1 or above and 7.6 is unaffected — and given a public proof-of-concept by Horizon3.ai on 20 October. Fortinet's deep Australian government, defence and critical-infrastructure footprint makes that a short-window fix, and it sits squarely under the ACSC's newest alert, the 14 October guidance on protecting operational technology and edge devices. Australia is also a named victim jurisdiction in UNC7017's compromise of AOMEI's update channel, alongside the United States, India and Brazil, with Google notifying victims in IT services, telecommunications and higher education. Continuity remains live too: ConnectWise reset credentials and kept Cloud Backup offline on 20 October, with roughly 1,000 Microsoft 365 tenants showing attacker sign-in activity and rogue app passwords planted to run until 2028, putting MSP and third-party assurance under APRA CPS 234 and the SOCI Act back in view. All three ACSC listings were re-checked on 21 October with no new item in the window — the newest alert remains 14 October, the newest advisory 9 October and the newest news post 15 October (ASD Essentials, covered in the 17 October edition) — and the OAIC Funnelback endpoint shows no new notifiable-data-breach or enforcement item, its newest entry remaining *International Access to Information Day 2026* (20 October).

In the week to 20 October (15–20 October), the digest corpus carried 64 stories: Breach/Data Leak led with 16, followed by Zero-day/Vulnerability (14), Malware (13) and Ransomware (10), with OT/ICS at 4. The geography was US 29, Australia 20, Global 10 and Europe 3. Three threads converge in this edition. The first is channel compromise as the attack of choice — AOMEI's pop-up delivery channel, GhostAction's CI workflows and fake-font stealer, OVHcloud's release archives and Rank Math's update window, all in one week: none required a zero-day, each required only a legitimate distribution path and the discipline not to look beyond it. The second is the cost of unpatched infrastructure moving from the network to the physical and the legal layer: the EU's Cyber Resilience Act can now compel disclosure of attacks on end-of-life refrigeration controllers but not the patches, and a drone strike degraded a Russian hyperscaler's data centre while Russian missiles destroyed equipment at a Ukrainian operator's — physical resilience and regulatory reach are both part of the cyber bill now. The third is disclosure asymmetry: the actor behind the AOMEI campaign erased its tracks and the vendor still cannot say who was hit, while the PHP team lost control of its own coordinated-release timeline — the defenders' advantage of early warning is only as good as the handling of the information. Expect continued pressure on coordinated disclosure practice, a short FortiSIEM exploitation window, and the `.woff2` trick to reappear under other names.

1

PHP to ship emergency patches for two critical remote code execution flaws

The PHP development team has told administrators to prepare for emergency security updates on Wednesday 21 October at 14:00 UTC (22:00 AEST) addressing two remote code execution vulnerabilities, each rated 9.2 under CVSS v4.0. The pair are tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619; watchTowr told BleepingComputer they affect PHP versions 5, 6, 7 and 8, that the flaws were due for coordinated release on 22 October before technical information leaked, and that it assesses both as CVSS 10 and has reproduced CVE-2026-109618 without releasing detail until patches ship. The researchers say they have seen no in-the-wild exploitation. The PHP team has disclosed no affected versions, exploit mechanism or fixed releases, and reserved the right to publish early if details or exploitation emerge. PHP powers WordPress and much of the public web, so the leaked advisory gives attackers a head start on a pre-patch window that, at the time of writing, had not closed — a fleet-wide remote code execution flaw in the language that runs a majority of websites.

2

Public exploit released for a FortiSIEM path-traversal flaw rated 9.4 (CVE-2026-106173)

Horizon3.ai has published a proof-of-concept exploit for CVE-2026-106173, a critical path-traversal flaw in Fortinet's FortiSIEM platform that Fortinet patched last week. Fortinet's advisory FG-IR-26-180 describes an improper limitation of a pathname to a restricted directory (CWE-22) that lets an unauthenticated attacker execute unauthorised code or commands via specially crafted packets; FortiSIEM 7.6 is not affected and 7.5.0 users must move to 7.5.1 or above. Horizon3 says an attacker connects to the gRPC service listening on TCP port 27918 and abuses unsafe path construction in the SendQueryResult() handler, which passes a user-supplied query-id into a file path without validation. Traversal alone cannot choose a file name because the server appends result-<source IP>-0-0-<id>.dat, so Horizon3 embeds a NUL byte in query-id to truncate that suffix, overwriting an existing file in place — its example rewrites a Redis health-check shell script that cron executes every 60 seconds, turning an arbitrary file write into remote code execution. Fortinet records no known exploitation; the PoC is the exposure.

3

AOMEI's update channel hijacked for months and victims still not notified

AOMEI says it has not yet identified or notified the customers infected after attackers tampered with its update servers between April and July 2026. ManageEngine's Threat Analysis Lab and Google's Threat Intelligence Group, working with Mandiant, attribute the campaign to UNC7017, which abused the channel AOMEI Backupper uses to deliver promotional pop-ups: two configuration files at aomeisoftware.com and aomeitech.com were briefly rewritten to point the software at malware, then restored so later inspection found nothing — Google called the short-burst tampering the first supply-chain compromise it had seen that evades point-in-time triage. The payloads were a previously unknown downloader, CROSSROAD, and a backdoor, SHINYROAD; during a 20 July window an invisible pop-up also coerced machines into sending Windows password hashes to an attacker server. ManageEngine first warned AOMEI on 28 May after Backupper silently installed Radmin at two customers; AOMEI blamed interception in transit and could not find the root cause. Google notified victims in IT services, telecommunications and higher education across nearly a dozen countries including the United States, Australia, India and Brazil, and says the true number is probably higher. AOMEI's server-side fixes do not clean machines already infected.

4

Citizen Lab ties mercenary spyware delivery to targeted ads in the 'AdFection' campaign

Citizen Lab has published what it calls the first technical evidence of mercenary spyware delivered through targeted online advertising with no user interaction, naming an ad-tech company it calls Discover-Tech. The technique, dubbed AdFection, exploits real-time ad bidding: a tag inside a rendered ad contacts a Discover-Tech server with the device's IP address, advertising ID and cookies, and the server returns JavaScript only when the device matches targeting criteria. The strongest evidence concerns Candiru — now Integrity Labs, which the US Commerce Department placed on its Entity List — and at least 45 devices belonging to Al Jazeera personnel; network logs the broadcaster supplied show infected devices contacting Discover-Tech infrastructure, then Candiru validator servers and finally a Candiru command-and-control server. Discover-Tech's Controlium platform served targeted ads to almost 200,000 unique websites and Android and iOS apps over four months, including on devices associated with government, military, critical-infrastructure and media organisations across dozens of countries. Google terminated its ad-exchange access, and IAB Europe, Beeswax, Eskimi and Vungle moved to cut it off. Ad impressions became an unauthenticated infection vector.

5

GhostAction escalates with a credential stealer hidden in a fake font across 94 GitHub repositories

The actor behind the GhostAction supply-chain attack has moved beyond injecting malicious GitHub Actions workflows, adding a second stage that persists on developer machines after the workflow is removed. OpenSourceMalware identified 94 compromised repositories belonging to 66 owners, 15 of which have since been deleted, renamed or made private, carrying a Visual Studio Code tasks.json file with a runOn: folderOpen trigger, a "public/fonts/fa-solid-400.woff2" file that is in fact a stealer, and a fallback CI workflow. Opening the folder runs the stealer, which harvests AWS keys, PyPI and npm tokens, Git logins, Kubernetes cluster credentials, SSH private keys and Anthropic Claude and Google Gemini API credentials, installs a cron job executing attacker code every five minutes, and exfiltrates over plain HTTP on port 8080. The fake-font filename copies North Korea's Fake Font/PolinRider campaign, and most repositories are abandoned: 65 of 79 had gone more than a year without a commit. The operator also registered my-github[.]com on 9 October, using it in a fake GitHub security-alert email whose reverse-proxied login page captured passwords and 2FA codes and silently authorised a device-flow OAuth app with repo, workflow and read:org scopes. Stage 2 arrived six days after GitHub began blocking the malicious workflow.

1

Sophos names BRONZE SANDCASTLE, a China-nexus espionage group wielding modified Meterpreter

Sophos's Counter Threat Unit has designated a new espionage cluster, BRONZE SANDCASTLE, after investigating incidents between August 2025 and August 2026 with victims in the Philippines, Taiwan, Afghanistan, Namibia and likely South Korea. The group's consistent marker is DLL sideloading of a malicious sbiedll.dll alongside legitimate Sandboxie binaries, with sideload pairs also abusing javaw.exe/jli.dll, Krita.exe/Krita.dll and NvdaHelper.exe/NvdaHelperRemote.dll; the DLLs read encrypted config.ini payloads, minimising static artefacts. The decrypted payload is a reflective loader Sophos describes as structurally similar to Metasploit's stageless Meterpreter shellcode but not a standard MSF artefact — it uses non-ROR13 API hashing — and it carries COFFLoader elements suggesting custom Beacon Object Files; versions 1.1.5 and 1.1.7 beacon a JSON block of host, process and network detail to command and control, and 1.1.7 pulls encrypted configs from a GitHub repository. Operators enumerate the network with Windows net commands and create Volume Shadow copies to copy ntds.dit, the Active Directory credential database. Activity concentrated in UTC+8 working hours is what Sophos cites in attributing the group to China. Why it matters: fresh tradecraft against Asia-Pacific government and critical-sector targets, where only memory-based detection is reliable.

2

Drone strike on a VK data centre degrades Russia's largest social network

A Ukrainian drone strike on the night of 18 October damaged a data centre in Russia's Leningrad region belonging to VK, the operator of VKontakte, and users reported problems loading feeds, streaming music, uploading photos and signing in through the weekend. VK confirmed the facility was targeted but denied any effect on services, and has not disclosed the damage or how many servers were hit. Downdetector logged about 6,600 complaints on Sunday, including roughly 600 within a single hour; the Russian monitoring service Sboy recorded around 2,100; NetBlocks measured connectivity associated with VK at 56% of normal levels during the incident. Ukrainian drone manufacturer Fire Point said its FP-1 drones struck several targets in the region including the VK site, describing it as a key VKontakte data centre that held more than 20,000 servers in 640 racks; VK co-founder Pavel Durov said he built it about 15 years ago and that "they failed to protect it". The strike continues a reciprocal campaign: Ukrainian forces hit Yandex data centres three times earlier in October and reportedly targeted a Sberbank facility, while Russian missile strikes destroyed equipment at Ukrainian operator Datagroup's Kyiv data centre. Data centres are now battlefield targets, so physical resilience is part of cyber resilience.

Kentucky Medicaid vendor breach exposed 3,816 members' billing data months after three intrusions

Kentucky's Cabinet for Health and Family Services disclosed on 19 October that Gainwell Technologies, a Medicaid programme vendor, suffered three separate intrusions — on 26 March, 16 April and 21 April 2026 — and did not discover them until August. On each occasion an unauthorised third party reached provider payment accounts on the KY Health-Net Provider Portal and downloaded files containing Kentucky Medicaid member information, with notifications to the estimated 3,816 affected people beginning on 16 October. The Cabinet says the exposed data did not include electronic health records, Social Security numbers or financial account information, but may have included full names, provider or claims identification numbers, dates of medical services, details of services received and how they were billed, payment amounts and non-Medicaid health-insurance policy and group numbers; it believes the intruder was seeking provider payment information rather than patient data. Gainwell holds a nearly US$34 million two-year contract for Medicaid programme reviews and has since added call-centre training on validating portal access requests plus further security enhancements. Why it matters: a state health programme inherits its payment vendor's detection gaps, here measured in months of unreported member billing data.

Cyberattack on a Baden-Württemberg university service centre leaves payroll, admissions and exam records offline

Central administrative services at universities across Baden-Württemberg remain crippled after an attack on the Hochschulservicezentrum Baden-Württemberg (HSZ-BW) in Reutlingen, detected on the evening of 7 October 2026 and detailed on 19 October. The centre, which runs central IT for the state's applied-sciences and education universities, art and music academies, the Cooperative State University and cultural institutions, shut down its internet connection, systems and telephone network. Finance, HR and building management are worst affected: with the link to the state office for salaries and pensions severed, universities cannot pay student assistants or scholarship recipients, lecturers at the Karlsruhe University of Music are receiving no advance payments, and Pforzheim University cannot settle digital invoices, while students in Karlsruhe and Pforzheim have no access to exam results. At the Schwäbisch Gmünd University of Design the application portal is offline, applications must be emailed, and transcripts, enrolment certificates, BAföG and health-insurance documents are unavailable. Reutlingen police and the Karlsruhe public prosecutor's cybercrime division are investigating; the cause is officially described only as malware, with ransomware unconfirmed and ransom demands anticipated. Why it matters: one shared platform has stalled payroll, admissions and student records across a state higher-education system at once.

1

Kaspersky flags unauthenticated wireless barcode scanners as a way into warehouse networks

Kaspersky Security Services researchers say the wireless handheld barcode scanners used across warehousing and e-commerce fulfilment are an unguarded entry point into warehouse networks, presenting the finding at the Security Analyst Summit 2026 in Bali on 20 October. In the standard layout several scanners talk to a base station wired to a workstation, and there is no authentication between the base station and the scanners. An attacker in Bluetooth range learns the base station's address through passive eavesdropping, then sends a payload the base station forwards to the connected PC as keystrokes — input the host treats as genuine user activity, allowing unauthorised commands and potentially remote code execution with no user interaction. Kaspersky observed the pattern across devices from multiple vendors, and its accompanying Securelist write-up notes Honeywell units affected by CVE-2026-4272. The scanner is only the doorway: the value lies in the workstation's trusted position and its connections to warehouse management systems, and Kaspersky says portable data terminals and thermal printers may offer the same route. Why it matters: the cheapest device in a distribution centre can now be an attacker's first foothold, so device verification, Bluetooth hygiene and network segmentation belong in logistics assurance.

2

n0n extortion batch names 38 port, terminal and shipping firms in one sweep

The n0n extortion crew listed 38 maritime, port and logistics organisations across 18 countries in a single batch on 12 October and gave the whole set one shared publication deadline of 13:27 UTC on 19 October. Demands reviewed by SOCRadar ran from US$2.4 million to US$14 million per company; Cydome put the aggregate at about US$1.22 billion. Named targets include Ocean Network Express, CULines, T.S. Lines, Santos Brasil, Portonave, BNCT and Pusan Newport Company in Busan, TTI Algeciras, Eurogate Tanger, Nam Dinh Vu Port, NEOM Ports and crane manufacturer ZPMC. The claimed material is dominated by EDI archives — container manifests, vessel schedules, gate movements and bookings — alongside terminal operating system records and invoices. Cydome assessed the data most likely came from a single support and project archive linked to Korean maritime software vendor CyberLogitec: at least 21 of the 38 are documented CyberLogitec customers and seven sample filenames reference the vendor or its products, with file dates running from 2009 to August 2026. No listed organisation had publicly confirmed a breach, and Korea's KISA has been notifying the Korean companies named. Why it matters: when carriers and terminals share a vendor or an EDI hub, one exposure concentrates risk across dozens of operators at once.

Building-automation maker Loxone loses CRM and customer data as a group called Samurai defaces its site

Austrian building-automation manufacturer Loxone reported on 19 October that intruders reached its internal servers over the weekend, copying data and corrupting its internal CRM and the website's database. Loxone said the data taken centres on CRM records — contact details for partner businesses, chiefly electrical installation firms — plus support requests and applicant data, and described some of it, including corporate mailboxes and payslips, as outdated. Sample data circulating from the breach reportedly includes Microsoft 365 account details, SSO login information and a register of 300,000 Miniserver serial numbers with project names. A group calling itself Samurai defaced Loxone's website and claims roughly 200 GB was copied; Loxone declined to comment on a ransom demand or on the attack path, though it said its IT teams identified and closed the exploited route, and chief executive Florian Wöss told ORF the entry came through one of its web servers. Loxone says customer installations are unaffected because credentials sit locally on the Miniserver and because Miniserver, Remote Connect and Exosphere were untouched, and that it has notified authorities and engaged external forensics. Why it matters: compromising a controller vendor can hand an attacker a map of a widely deployed installed base even while the buildings themselves keep running.

1

EU cyber law will not force fixes for the end-of-life refrigeration controllers hit in France

The European Union's Cyber Resilience Act will not compel manufacturers to fix flaws in the refrigeration controllers attacked across French supermarkets, the EU agency ENISA said on Monday 20 October. ENISA confirmed that the Act's reporting duties, which took effect last month, do cover products no longer sold in the bloc or no longer supported — makers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents — but that the separate duty to handle vulnerabilities and issue free security updates applies only during a manufacturer-defined support period. Where a product was placed on the market before 11 December 2027, or its support has ended, "the manufacturer is not required to comply with the vulnerability handling obligations". The intrusion on the night of 11–12 October hijacked internet-exposed controllers and drove continuous defrost cycles reported at up to 50°C at chains including Intermarché, Carrefour, Monoprix, Leclerc and Coopérative U, spoiling stock at a cost estimated in the tens of millions of euros. ANSSI named Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC series, Copeland xweb and Dixell xweb units — all end-of-life; Copeland and Danfoss each say the affected generation is discontinued, and CERT-FR urged operators to disconnect or filter any controller exposed to the internet. The Paris prosecutor's office told Recorded Future News it has received about ten complaints and estimates roughly 700 victims. Why it matters: the installed base of unsupported industrial kit keeps creating incidents that the new EU regime has explicitly decided not to reach.

Extortion group n0n claims bank-payments vendor Finzly's access secrets

An extortion group calling itself n0n added bank-payments vendor Finzly to its leak site over the weekend and is threatening to publish "network configuration and access secrets for payment infrastructure" and "cloud environment topology and access keys" at 8:20 p.m. Eastern time on Friday unless a "settlement" is paid, American Banker reported on 20 October. Charlotte, North Carolina-based Finzly sells a platform called BankOS that links banks to ACH, Fedwire, RTP, FedNow and Swift through a single connection; its website lists 11 bank and credit-union clients, among them Synovus, First Citizens Bank, First Horizon and EverBank. Finzly said it "recently learned" it had been named, is investigating with external cybersecurity experts and remains "fully operational", but did not say whether credentials were taken, whether it received a demand, whether it has notified clients, or whether it has rotated keys. n0n had posted no sample of the data as of Tuesday afternoon. Threat-intelligence firm Cyfirma rates "moderate" confidence that n0n has credible access to at least some listed victims, and says the group most likely breaks in with passwords stolen by infostealer malware. n0n launched its leak site on 18 September and has posted 131 listings across 32 countries. Why it matters: one payments vendor's credential exposure can reach many banks at once — ShinyHunters' theft from bank-technology vendor Jack Henry exposed client institutions' account holders this year.

2

Group-IB: fraudsters used a fintech's open-banking link flow as a free stolen-credential checker

Group-IB's Fraud Protection team says Classiscam-linked criminals turned a legitimate spend-management platform into fraud infrastructure rather than a victim. In research published 20 October and titled "The Checker You Pay For", analysts Yuan Huang and Alexander Grabko describe two linked behaviours. From late April 2026 the actors created accounts and used the platform's payment-notification and email rails to send Poshmark-impersonation messages — Poshmark itself was not compromised — whose fake verification pages harvested names, card details, phone numbers, addresses, one-time passcodes, card balances and payment-wallet access. The harvested bank and card credentials were then fed into the platform's open-banking account-linking flow, where a successful link confirmed the credentials were valid and the account funded; because the third-party aggregator billed the platform an estimated US$0.30–1.00 per link, the checking cost the criminals nothing. Shared device identifiers tied the phishing accounts to the card-testing accounts. After Group-IB's engagement, the client's fraudulent-account blocking time fell from several hours to under a minute and more than 230 fraudulent accounts were stopped in May 2026. Why it matters: open-banking linking APIs have become a ready-made, billable credential oracle for fraud crews.

1

Court records show how a crime ring used Staples' help desk to mint US$5m in gift cards

A Homeland Security Investigations search warrant application unsealed last week, reported by 404 Media on 19 October, sets out how a Chinese organised-crime ring turned Staples' own customer-service system into a gift-card factory. The document does not name the company but 404 Media matched it to Staples on three identifying features: a Massachusetts headquarters, gift-card orders filled by Blackhawk Network, and an internal support tool called Nexus. Staples believed Nexus was reachable only from its internal computers, but hackers reached it from outside and ran repeated heap-dump requests, which returned snapshots of the app's memory — including the live session data of logged-in customer-service representatives. Hijacking those sessions, they issued more than 15,000 gift cards worth over US$5 million and emailed them to accounts they controlled. The gang redeemed 4,630 of them, about US$425,000: 3,850 cards worth roughly US$141,000 at 85 Target stores in 18 states in two days, about 330 cards worth US$58,600 at Sephora online across 1,500 transactions, and 450 cards worth US$226,000 at more than 22 Lowe's locations, with electronics shipped to China. The Justice Department says the wider ring acquired more than US$100 million in gift cards and sentenced three Chinese nationals in April 2026. Why it matters: three safeguards failed together — an internal-only tool was internet-reachable, it leaked live sessions on demand, and a session could issue cards with no payment behind them.

1

Nigerian hacker sentenced to eight years over spearphishing campaign against US tax preparers

Chukwuemeka Amachukwu, a Nigerian national extradited from France last year on fraud, hacking and identity-theft charges, was sentenced to eight years in federal prison, one year of supervised release and more than US$3.6 million in restitution. US District Judge Paul Gardephe imposed the sentence in the Southern District of New York; US Attorney Jamie McDonald said the schemes "imposed significant harm on the victims and the public". Prosecutors said Amachukwu led a group that used spearphishing emails to breach US tax-preparation businesses from 2019, stealing the information of about 800 business customers and filing fraudulent state and federal returns that sought roughly US$8.4 million, of which about US$2.5 million was paid out. The group also used the stolen data for about US$819,000 in fraudulent Small Business Administration Economic Injury Disaster Loan claims; separately, Amachukwu made more than US$7 million impersonating wealthy overseas businessmen and offering fake standby letters of credit. Co-conspirator Kingsley Uchelue Utulu was sentenced to five years in June after his arrest in the UK. Why it matters: it shows the long enforcement tail of the phishing-and-refund-fraud economy and that extradition, custodial terms and restitution are now routine.

Key to this page

Two pill families appear in the text and they answer different questions. A CVE pill colours severity — a measured CVSS band from the National Vulnerability Database. A threat-actor pill colours attribution confidence — how well-corroborated the naming is, which is a claim rather than a measurement. Both are links: a CVE opens the ATT&CK matrix or its wiki page, an actor opens its wiki page.

CVE identifiers

  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNCritical · CVSS 9.0+
  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNHigh · CVSS 7.0–8.9
  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNMedium · CVSS 4.0–6.9
  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNLow · below 4.0
  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNNo severity resolved — not the same as low

Threat actors · MITRE ATT&CK

  • APT29State attribution stated by MITRE ATT&CK
  • ShinyHuntersSelf-declared, or criminal-reporting attribution
  • Transparent TribeContested — ATT&CK hedges, or two plausible sponsors
  • ZIRCONIUMNo attribution in MITRE ATT&CK

Story signals

  • ● Tier 1/4Source reliability — 1 official, 4 leads only
  • VerifiedCorroborated by a second source or the principal
  • ReportedSingle outlet, or a claim still in progress
  • UnverifiedA claim we could not corroborate
  • ConfirmedBreach acknowledged by the victim or a regulator
  • ProbableBreach indicated but not yet acknowledged
  • IOCs · FamilyLive abuse.ch indicators exist for that malware family

A collapsed Indicators of compromise block under a story lists defanged abuse.ch indicator values. The defanging is deliberate — never click, resolve or fetch them. An indicator corroborates a report; it never proves one.
