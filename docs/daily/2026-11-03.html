Executive Summary

Ivanti patched the final two flaws in a Connect Secure zero-day chain that criminals have been chaining in attacks since at least 1 October, completing a fix cycle that its vendor, Volexity and CISA have all been tracking; Google dismantled a botnet of more than 1.3 million Android TV boxes and said it is "highly confident" a Shenzhen manufacturer deliberately backdoored them and sold access to criminals; and a second wave of suspected attacks remotely melted supermarket freezers across at least five European countries three weeks after the first. On the financial side, Polymarket published a post-mortem promising US$3.9 million in restitution for last week's DebugBear script hijack — the same day ASIC warned Australians that prediction-market contracts carry "all the features of a bet". Three separate disclosures also showed AI being operationalised by attackers: a new infostealer that ranks victims by financial worth with an LLM, unauthourised remote code execution in the popular Langflow AI framework, and an impersonation cluster of malicious AI-SDK packages on npm.

ASIC delivered the only Australian regulator action in the window, telling licensees it is "not currently convinced" that prediction-market products for retail clients are in the public interest and that contracts have "all the features of a bet" — the regulator compares them to binary options, which have been banned for retail since 2021, and warns that Australians using offshore platforms have no protection under Australian law. The story carries direct relevance for licensed financial services providers facilitating access to offshore platforms, whose obligations continue to apply. ACSC published nothing new across its three listings — the newest item remains the ASD *Cyber Threat Report 2025-26* news release (27 October), with the Fortinet (23 October) and PHP (21 October) alerts the most recent security alerts. The Ivanti Connect Secure chain matters locally: the appliances are a standard remote-access gateway across Australian government and enterprise estates, and Shadowserver tracks more than 5,000 internet-exposed instances globally, so the completed patch cycle should prompt immediate upgrade checks. Separately, Melbourne researcher Ben Wilson of Tanto Security disclosed that Microsoft quietly fixed a Windows 11 diagnostics privilege-escalation flaw without issuing CVE identifiers — a patch-visibility gap every Australian Windows fleet inherits. No Australian organisation appeared as a named victim in the window.

The week to 2 November (28 October–2 November) logged 86 stories across the pipeline: 27 zero-day/vulnerability items, 22 breach/leak disclosures, 14 malware analyses, 8 ransomware events and 6 phishing items, with reporting led by the US (47), global coverage (22) and Australia (12). Three threads define the period. First, civilian alert infrastructure has become a target in the Russia–Ukraine war: Ukraine's Air Alert app (6.6 million users) was hijacked late Sunday to push fake radiation and nuclear-strike warnings, and within hours a Kyiv-linked group claimed a parallel compromise of Russia's EMERCOM alert app — the two incidents echo 2023 precedents (hijacked Russian radio, AnonGhost's fake Red Alert) and mark the first time both sides' mobile alert apps were hit in the same cycle; Russia's statistics agency separately confirmed the hacktivist attack on its systems claimed by Belarusian and Ukrainian groups. Second, AI is being operationalised on the offensive side with striking speed: the VOLTA infostealer feeds stolen evidence bundles to Claude, Kimi and Gemini to rank victims by financial worth; Langflow shipped fixes for two critical unauthourised flaws (one reachable without an account, with a Metasploit module in the wild); and Unit 42's CL-UNK-1211 disclosure (15 October) showed suspected AI-generated scripts in a campaign against government Linux infrastructure — three independent lines converging in one week. Third, the edge and appliance exploitation wave has not let up since the ASD threat report set the baseline: the Ivanti chain is now fully patched but was live for a month, more than 1,000 GitLab servers remain exposed to a KEV-listed maximum-severity RCE, and Synology warned of a C3RB3R ransomware surge exploiting flaws patched in December 2024 — while the supermarket cold-chain attacks have recurred across the EU with no attribution and no legal duty on manufacturers to patch legacy equipment until the Cyber Resilience Act's support-period obligations arrive in December 2027.

1

Ivanti patches final two flaws in Connect Secure zero-day chain exploited since October

Ivanti released updates closing the last two vulnerabilities in a Connect Secure remote-access gateway chain that has been chained in zero-day attacks since at least 1 October: CVE-2026-111357, a high-severity privilege escalation, and CVE-2026-118527, which lets an authenticated attacker upload files to an unpatched device. Both were used alongside the authentication-bypass flaw CVE-2026-104935 that Ivanti patched last month, and that CISA added to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 13 October. Volexity, which tracks the actor as UTA0576, reported root-level code execution on fully patched devices without malware or web shells — the intruder re-exploited the appliance for every command and tampered with logs to evade Ivanti's Integrity Checker Tool — and Defused saw the first exploitation signs on 1 October. Ivanti said it held the October fix in QA while prioritising the primary exploitation path. Shadowserver tracks more than 5,000 internet-exposed Connect Secure instances, with no word on how many are patched; the appliances remain a favourite target because they sit at the enterprise perimeter.

1

Over 1,000 GitLab servers still exposed to actively exploited maximum-severity RCE

Shadowserver tracks more than 1,000 internet-exposed GitLab servers still unpatched against CVE-2026-97229, a maximum-severity integer overflow in the CI/CD Regular Expression Compiler that lets an unauthenticated user execute arbitrary code via a crafted regular expression in a pipeline configuration. GitLab patched the flaw on 12 October in Community and Enterprise editions 19.4.2, 19.3.5 and 19.2.8, and CISA confirmed in-the-wild exploitation when it added the CVE to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 29 October. Shadowserver's Sunday data also counts around 11,300 instances vulnerable to a separate last-month regex-compiler flaw, CVE-2026-110264, out of roughly 39,000 GitLab instances it sees daily. The exposure matters because GitLab is the DevSecOps platform for more than half of the Fortune 100: an unauthenticated RCE on the CI server is effectively domain-admin-equivalent access to an organisation's software supply chain. Patches have been available for three weeks, so the residual 1,000-plus is a patch-management failure rather than a vendor gap.

2

Attackers pose as IT support on Teams, then abuse AD CS to steal company data

Microsoft Threat Intelligence investigated two human-operated intrusions in which attackers messaged and called employees on Teams while posing as the company's own IT or help desk, talked them into reading back a Quick Assist connection code, and took direct remote control of the workstation. From there they staged portable Python, Bun or Java runtimes, performed host and network discovery, and pivoted to Active Directory Certificate Services: in one case they abused an ESC1-vulnerable certificate template to request authentication certificates for highly privileged accounts and ran DCSync, and relayed the domain controller's NTLM authentication to AD CS Web Enrollment (ESC8) for a domain-controller certificate used with PKINIT. Microsoft notes such certificates survive password resets and device wipes, and that one intrusion ended with company data copied to an external server — Defender's automatic attack disruption cut the other short. No Teams vulnerability was exploited, and the activity is not attributed to a known group; the chain is a reminder that a user-approved remote session plus insecure AD CS configurations can become certificate-backed access to privileged identities.

Synology warns of C3RB3R ransomware surge targeting outdated NAS devices

Synology warned customers to update immediately amid a surge of C3RB3R (Cerber-imposter) ransomware attacks exploiting DiskStation Manager flaws patched in December 2024 — including CVE-2024-10441 (arbitrary code execution), CVE-2024-50629 (arbitrary file read) and CVE-2024-10445 (arbitrary file write) — against devices running outdated versions. Ransom notes (read-me3.txt) rename encrypted files with a .L0CK3D extension and direct victims to a dark-web portal selling a "C3rb3r Decryptor" for thousands of dollars; the first victim reports surfaced more than two weeks ago. Synology, which last issued a ransomware warning in 2019 (after the eCh0raix brute-force campaign), lists critical fixes across DSM 7.2.2 down to 7.2 and recommends enabling automatic updates and blocking internet access to the management interface. Shadowserver tracks over 380,000 internet-exposed Synology NAS devices, making the attack surface substantial — and C3RB3R has previously hit Confluence and GitLab servers via their own RCE flaws, so the operators move with the patch lag.

4

Critical Langflow flaws allow unauthenticated code execution on AI servers

IBM's bulletin for the open-source Langflow AI agent-builder flags two critical flaws: CVE-2026-93680 (CVSS 9.8, authentication bypass reachable with no account) and CVE-2026-110186 (CVSS 9.8), described as unauthenticated remote code execution exploitable through Model Context Protocol tool enumeration — Langflow can expose its flows as MCP tools to AI applications. A further high-severity issue (CVE-2026-110193, 8.8) lets a low-privileged account make the Agentic Assistant generate or submit component code that runs operating-system commands, and CVE-2026-110192 (6.5) exposes insufficiently protected credentials. Every release from 1.0.0 through 1.12.8 is affected; the only fix is 1.13.0. A public Metasploit module already cites the authentication bypass, and the default-on LANGFLOW_AUTO_LOGIN setting in 1.12.8 — which "bypasses authentication and should only be used in development environments" — makes misconfigured deployments trivially reachable. The flaws are not yet in CISA's KEV catalog, but the Metasploit coverage means organisations running Langflow should treat this as urgent rather than advisory.

5

Malicious npm cluster impersonates AI SDKs to steal credentials and cloud accounts

OpenSourceMalware's verified feed logged a fresh cluster of npm packages impersonating AI developer SDKs — char-ai-sdk and character-ai-sdk (critical/high), persona-ai-sdk (high, with OSV advisory MAL-2026-18463) and tavern-ai-sdk (high) — that execute on import and implement credential theft, cloud-account abuse and repository propagation. The cluster is the same-campaign shape as yesterday's observed lookalike wave, and the operational risk is concrete: any developer or CI pipeline pulling one of these into a build hands over credentials and cloud access at install time, and the propagation behaviour means compromised repositories can seed the packages onward. The affected version spans are effectively "all versions" of the malicious entries, so blocking by exact version is unreliable — teams should inventory their dependency trees for these names and any lookalike of a well-known SDK, rotate secrets touched by affected builds, and monitor for unexpected cloud activity. The OSV advisory for persona-ai-sdk is the authoritative record for detection and quarantine.

Google dismantles 1.3 million-device Android botnet; TV box maker sold criminal access

Google and industry partners took down the XCPE backdoor botnet, which enrolled more than 1.3 million Android set-top boxes — mostly under Shenzhen manufacturer Videostrong's own and other brands — before control infrastructure was dismantled in late October. Google says it is "highly confident" Videostrong intentionally built the x.cpe backdoor into firmware as a system app over several years and monetised it by selling access to criminal affiliates who loaded malware modules for ad fraud and routing other people's traffic through owners' home connections; it does not believe a rogue insider or mistake was to blame. Through the backdoor, operators could silence Play Protect checks, silently install apps, run commands, capture screens and keystrokes, copy account data and open reverse shells. Google took legal action against the control domains and servers, also cutting off more than a dozen malware families loaded through the backdoor and estimating the action severs access to at least 10 million more infected devices; it has banned Videostrong and its affiliates from building certified Android devices. The backdoor remains on the boxes, and owners cannot remove it themselves.

2

Second wave of suspected cyberattacks hits supermarket freezers across Europe

Supermarket and grocery-store refrigeration systems across Europe were hit again in the early hours of Monday (2 November), the second such wave in three weeks: retailers reported a "limited number" of smaller stores affected in Sweden, more than 50 in Belgium, and over a dozen across Ireland, Estonia, the Netherlands and Luxembourg, with freezers remotely set to 50 degrees Celsius to spoil stock. No government agency has identified the perpetrators — the Centre for Cybersecurity Belgium is aware and says "it may not be possible to identify them" — and neither the October French wave (around 700 victims per the Paris prosecutor's office) nor Monday's has been claimed. Technicians (including Belgium's Sabcobel, mobilised from 1 a.m.) report affected units need physical modification, not just software updates: the Estonian contractor blamed an older Danfoss system, and Dutch outlet NOS reported Copeland equipment targeted in Belgium — both vendors say the devices are discontinued and out of support, and the EU's Cyber Resilience Act will not oblige manufacturers to provide support periods until December 2027. CCB's spokesperson linked the rising incident count to "the targeted use of AI" in vulnerability discovery over the past six months, though not specifically to this attack.

1

Polymarket post-mortem pledges US$3.9 million restitution after DebugBear script hijack

Polymarket's post-mortem of last week's wallet-draining compromise commits US$3.9 million to victims — US$2.7 million covering the initial theft of positions and funds, plus US$1.2 million for losses tied to the platform's hours-long shutdown — with the company saying "no claims or tickets needed" because eligible wallets were identified from on-chain and trading data; 95 per cent of affected wallets were refunded in full as of Saturday. The incident stemmed from a compromised performance-monitoring script loaded from vendor DebugBear, which used users' active sessions to sell positions and move funds before Polymarket removed it, paused trading and reset all sessions. DebugBear's own follow-up says the compromise began at its domain registrar and targeted a specific customer's website. The restitution total is an escalation of the US$2.7 million figure reported on 31 October–2 November, and lands the same day ASIC warned Australians that offshore prediction-market products carry no Australian legal protections — a sector now worth a reported US$40 billion at Kalshi's latest valuation.

2

ASIC warns prediction markets are "highly speculative" and outside retail protection

The Australian Securities and Investments Commission told licensees it is "not currently convinced" that prediction-market contracts for retail clients are in the public interest, describing them as "highly speculative" all-or-nothing bets with "all the features of a bet" and comparing them to binary options — banned for retail clients in Australia since 2021. ASIC's warning, carried via financial-services trade press, flags market-integrity risks including misuse of confidential information, market manipulation and disputes over how outcomes are determined, and makes two compliance points: Australians who access offshore platforms directly do not have protections under Australian law, and licensed financial services providers that facilitate access to offshore providers remain subject to all their obligations under Australian law for that activity. The statement lands against a background of surging retail interest in event contracts — US platform Kalshi reported over 30 million accounts and a US$40 billion valuation on its latest raise — and follows last week's compromise of prediction-market Polymarket, reinforcing the regulator's scepticism about the product class.

VOLTA infostealer uses Claude, Gemini and Kimi to rank victims by financial value

Cisco Talos disclosed VOLTA-AUTODOXER, a Windows infostealer that offloads a novel analytical step to large language models: after harvesting Chromium passwords and cookies, Outlook and Slack data, payment cards, cryptocurrency wallet seed phrases, Discord/Telegram/Steam/Roblox sessions, Microsoft OAuth tokens, SSH and cloud keys and more, it builds a structured "evidence bundle" profiling the person behind the machine — geolocation and home address, employer and domain, inferred birth year, card last-four digits, Slack workspaces, document authorship metadata and a live Roblox account valuation — corroborating addresses by mining the victim's Gmail for receipts from Amazon, DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub, Walmart and PayPal. The bundle is serialised and sent through OpenRouter to a default of Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6, falling back to Moonshot's Kimi K2 and Google's Gemini 2.5 Flash, under a 240-word "forensic intelligence analyst" system prompt that forbids the model from using outside facts. The output ranks the victim's valuables — "WHAT THEY'RE WORTH" — by monetisation tier and assesses their operations security, giving operators automated victim prioritisation.

1

Russian and Ukrainian hackers hijack emergency apps to spread fake nuclear attack warnings

Late on Sunday (1 November), Kyiv residents received phone notifications warning of a possible radiation hazard, chemical threat and ongoing street fighting — from Air Alert, the app 6.6 million Ukrainians rely on for missile and drone warnings. Ukraine's Air Force said the app had "likely been compromised by the enemy" after a message in Russian warned of a possible Oreshnik ballistic-missile strike and carried a link to a Telegram channel of a previously unknown group calling itself Hitcore Corp. The developers, Ajax Systems, disabled push notifications, suspended the service and restored it by early Monday with extra security measures. Within hours, a Kyiv-based group calling itself DC8044 claimed it had compromised the mobile app of Russia's Emergency Situations Ministry (EMERCOM) through its API and distributed false warnings of nuclear warheads launched towards Russia; the ministry acknowledged the app hack but said the public warning system was unaffected. The reciprocal strikes echo 2023 precedents — hijacked Russian radio broadcasts and AnonGhost's fake Red Alert — and mark the first time both sides' mobile alert apps have been hit in the same cycle.

2

Iran-linked DarkBlinders compromises Israeli figure and Kurdish regional government cloud

Dream Research Labs disclosed a cyber-espionage campaign by Iran-aligned actor DarkBlinders between August and October 2026 that compromised a high-profile individual in Israel and a government cloud environment in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq. DarkBlinders, assessed active since at least September 2024 and previously linked to attacks on aviation infrastructure and telecommunications in the UAE and Iraq, relies on spear-phishing with trojanised utilities and attacker-controlled download sites; earlier chains documented by Elastic Security Labs (tracking the cluster as REF8685) and Group-IB deploy SHELBYLOADER via DLL side-loading to run the SHELBYC2 (AEROSTAT) backdoor, which uses GitHub for command-and-control. Palo Alto Networks separately described the actor (as CL-STA-1178) running "Blinder Tunnel", a recruitment-themed campaign posing as the Dubai Airports IT department with fake coding challenges. The report adds to a pattern of Iran-nexus operators extending compromise from Gulf aviation and telecoms into government cloud and high-value civilian targets, with no technical overlap to other known groups.

Connecticut Medicaid claims data exposed for 41,000 recipients after provider-account access

The Connecticut Department of Social Services reported a data incident affecting the protected health information of 41,000 individuals, involving claims and payment information related to the Connecticut Medicaid (HUSKY) programme after an unauthorised user gained access to a state Medicaid provider reimbursement account. DSS was informed by its fiscal agent and account administrator, Gainwell Technologies, on 25 June 2026, and the investigation assessed the access was "most likely financially motivated" rather than aimed at patient data, although data theft could not be ruled out. The exposed information includes names, identification numbers associated with Medicare claims or provider payment accounts, dates of service, payment information, details of services received, amounts paid and information related to non-Medicaid health insurance — but not electronic medical records, financial account numbers or Social Security numbers. The provider portal has been secured and affected individuals offered credit monitoring and identity-theft protection. It is the second HUSKY-related incident this year: an earlier March breach affected roughly 22,500 individuals via a Hartford Healthcare employee's compromised credentials.

Key to this page

Two pill families appear in the text and they answer different questions. A CVE pill colours severity — a measured CVSS band from the National Vulnerability Database. A threat-actor pill colours attribution confidence — how well-corroborated the naming is, which is a claim rather than a measurement. Both are links: a CVE opens the ATT&CK matrix or its wiki page, an actor opens its wiki page.

CVE identifiers

  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNCritical · CVSS 9.0+
  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNHigh · CVSS 7.0–8.9
  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNMedium · CVSS 4.0–6.9
  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNLow · below 4.0
  • CVE-XXXX-NNNNNo severity resolved — not the same as low

Threat actors · MITRE ATT&CK

  • APT29State attribution stated by MITRE ATT&CK
  • ShinyHuntersSelf-declared, or criminal-reporting attribution
  • Transparent TribeContested — ATT&CK hedges, or two plausible sponsors
  • ZIRCONIUMNo attribution in MITRE ATT&CK
