Executive Summary

France formally attributed last month's wave of attacks that sabotaged refrigeration systems at around 700 grocery stores — causing extensive food losses and at least 15 fires — to the Shahid Kaveh unit of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, opening a judicial investigation and pledging European sanctions. The vendor patch front was unusually heavy: Microsoft shipped its November Patch Tuesday updates, Google emergency-patched the eighth Chrome zero-day exploited in attacks this year, and SAP fixed four maximum-severity (CVSS 10.0) flaws, while CISA added four newly exploited vulnerabilities including GPUStack and IBM Aspera Faspex to its known-exploited catalogue. On the breach side, AI infrastructure vendor Fireworks confirmed two October intrusions that exposed customer billing data and AI datasets, Japanese marketing firm ARARA's breach of 77 million email addresses reached marquee names including SEGA (2.2 million subscribers), and hearing-aid giant Amplifon disclosed a breach affecting US customers. ESET exposed a new Turla toolkit, Cereal, intercepting NATO and EU correspondence on government Microsoft Exchange servers in Romania and Kazakhstan, and ransomware group BYOD claimed a serious hit on Vanderbilt University.

All three ACSC listings were checked this morning: no new alerts, advisories or news items have been published since 5 November (the open-weight AI model and post-quantum vendor guidance from that date were covered in the 6 November edition; the newest alert remains the 23 October Fortinet advisory). The day's biggest stories still land locally. France's IRGC attribution is the first official naming of the supermarket refrigeration sabotage, and the US partners' 5 November guidance on hardening industrial refrigeration and cold chain systems applies directly to Australian food-and-grocery operators, who sit inside the SOCI Act critical infrastructure framework — ANSSI also observed simultaneous targeting of Schneider Electric equipment, a brand widely deployed in Australian OT. The SAP CVSS 10.0 flaws (ICM2SHELL, PROXYGATE) hit NetWeaver and Web Dispatcher components that underpin ERP estates across Australian enterprises and government; the Microsoft and Chrome fixes should roll out under Essential Eight patching cycles. N-able's N-central outage is an MSP supply-chain moment — N-central is used by Australian managed service providers, and hosted RMM downtime is a reminder of dependency concentration. Turla's Exchange-focused toolkit reinforces the ASD/ACSC line on hardening government email; Fireworks' breach and the Mandiant "LLMJacking" analysis name APAC among affected regions. The week to 10 November saw 10 stories geolocated to Australia.

In the week 5–10 November (61 stories), breach/data-leak coverage led at 20, ahead of zero-day/vulnerability at 17 and malware at 7; regulatory/policy (5), ransomware (4) and AI security (4) followed. Geography ran US 36, Global 13, Australia 10, Europe 1, APAC 1. Four threads stand out. First, OT sabotage with physical consequences is becoming a formal attribution battleground: Iran's IRGC now stands accused of starting fires through supermarket refrigeration controllers, with a second, still-unattributed wave across 11 European countries under investigation — and the same Shahid Kaveh unit, aka CyberAv3ngers, previously hit US water utilities. Second, the disclosure-to-exploitation pipeline has never run hotter: Chrome's eighth zero-day, Barracuda's ESG chain now exploited in the wild, Xen Orchestra's actively exploited auth bypass, and N-able's 33 disclosed flaws all landed within 48 hours of a heavy Patch Tuesday. Third, the "AI economy" is a new target class: Fireworks AI's breach with Notion as a downstream victim, Mandiant's LLMJacking findings ($100,000-a-day fraudulent cloud charges), and Qubole's customer account compromise all point at AI and cloud-data supply chains. Fourth, Russian state espionage retains its focus on government email: Turla's Cereal toolkit shows Exchange servers remain the interception point of choice for FSB-linked operations targeting NATO states.

France blames Iranian IRGC hackers for sabotage of refrigeration controllers at about 700 retail sites

France's Cyber Crisis Coordination Centre — grouping ANSSI, the military cyber command and the DGSE and DGSI intelligence services — published a five-page attribution report identifying the Shahid Kaveh unit of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps as responsible for the October attacks that sabotaged refrigeration systems at around 700 French grocery stores, causing what officials described as extensive food losses and at least 15 fires. Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot confirmed the attribution at a G7 meeting, said France had summoned Iran's ambassador, and pledged European sanctions against those responsible. ANSSI also identified simultaneous targeting of Schneider Electric equipment at several small hydroelectric facilities, with moderate impact. Shahid Kaveh is the unit previously linked to CyberAv3ngers and the 2023 attacks on US water utilities, with US State Department rewards of up to US$10 million offered for its commanders — now named publicly, including IRGC units' head Hamid Reza Lashgarian and deputy commanders. The Paris Prosecutor's Office opened a judicial investigation and requested international arrest warrants. The report does not address the second, still-unattributed wave that hit refrigeration systems across 11 European countries in early November.

2

SEGA newsletter data breach exposes 2.2 million email addresses via provider ARARA

Japanese gaming giant SEGA disclosed that a breach at its third-party newsletter provider, ARARA, exposed the email addresses of 2.2 million newsletter subscribers across 11 SEGA and SEGA Fave mailings, plus 92,300 records with additional information yet to be determined. SEGA said the exposed data did not include names, dates of birth, addresses, credit card details or passwords, and that the separate SEGA Account distribution system was unaffected, with no confirmed misuse so far. ARARA itself disclosed on 2 November that a web server vulnerability had enabled a breach of its email distribution service compromising 77 million email addresses belonging to nearly 380 corporate clients, and that attackers had used the access to send more than 7 million phishing emails impersonating credit card companies in late October. Other named victims of the same incident include MOS Burger (7.5 million), Shochiku Multiplex Theatres (3.3 million) and TKC (2.3 million). The incident is separate from the still-ongoing outage of SEGA Fave's arcade network services, which was hit by its own intrusion in early November.

1

Microsoft ships November 2026 Patch Tuesday updates with HEIF, MDM remote code execution fixes

Microsoft released its November 2026 Patch Tuesday cumulative updates (KB5132061 for Windows 11 26H2/25H2/24H2, KB5131087 for 23H2), the eleventh Patch Tuesday of the year, bundling fixes for what the company describes as hundreds of vulnerabilities alongside feature changes. The update guide lists notable fixes including CVE-2026-100204, a remote code execution vulnerability in HEIF Image Extensions; CVE-2026-102219, an RCE flaw in Windows Modern Device Management; and CVE-2026-100223, an elevation-of-privilege flaw in Windows NTFS. The slate also includes a CNG Key Isolation Service elevation-of-privilege bug (CVE-2026-103571), a Win32k EoP (CVE-2026-105028), a Volume Shadow Copy EoP (CVE-2026-105017), Azure CycleCloud and Azure NFS helper privileges issues, and two Visual Studio Code fixes covering an information disclosure and a security feature bypass. No flaw in this month's batch has been flagged by Microsoft as actively exploited, and neither Windows 11 build carries special changes — the same fixes apply across supported versions.

Google patches eighth Chrome zero-day exploited in attacks this year (CVE-2026-125350)

Google released an emergency Chrome Stable channel update to patch a use-after-free vulnerability in the browser's XML component, tracked as CVE-2026-125350, confirming the flaw is being exploited in the wild and withholding technical detail until most users have updated. A use-after-free occurs when the XML parser frees an object from memory but a pointer to it is later dereferenced, allowing memory layout manipulation and code execution. The fix rolls out as Chrome 157.0.8103.50/.51 on Windows and macOS and 157.0.8103.50 on Linux, and is the eighth actively exploited Chrome zero-day Google has patched in 2026, following flaws in CSSFontFeatureValuesMap (CVE-2026-2441), Skia (CVE-2026-3909), V8 (CVE-2026-3910, CVE-2026-11645, CVE-2026-87491 and CVE-2026-85046) and Dawn/WebGPU (CVE-2026-5281). Google's advisory system of record notes the update may take days to weeks to reach all users, though the latest version was available immediately through the update channel.

N-able takes N-central cloud offline after researcher discloses 33 zero-days with PoC exploits

Security researcher Sean Bales publicly disclosed more than 30 alleged zero-day vulnerabilities in N-able's N-central remote monitoring and management platform, publishing a GitHub repository ("Able2Shell") with proof-of-concept exploits after late-Sunday (8 November) disclosure, and telling BleepingComputer the discoveries were AI-assisted and that earlier vulnerability reports to N-able had gone unaddressed. The disclosed flaw classes include authentication bypasses, credential disclosure, arbitrary file writes, directory traversal, XSS, XXE and remote code execution; the most critical chain abuses an unauthenticated XXE in the service-import function to steal a trusted session, reset a password and create a rogue Default Administrator account with command execution. N-able responded by taking its hosted N-central environments offline across the Americas, Europe and Asia-Pacific and telling on-premises customers to shut down servers until further notice, saying it has seen no evidence of active exploitation and is developing a hotfix, with updates promised every four hours. The researcher said the flaws were tested against a pre-release version, 2026.4.0.31, and likely affect earlier builds.

4

SAP patches ICM2SHELL and PROXYGATE maximum severity flaws (four CVSS 10.0)

SAP's November 2026 security updates address 35 vulnerabilities, including four rated 10/10 CVSS affecting NetWeaver, Web Dispatcher and Gateway components, which researchers at Onapsis say can be exploited by unauthenticated attackers to fully compromise enterprise systems with no user interaction. Two memory-corruption flaws — CVE-2026-76964 in the NetWeaver Application Server ABAP's Internet Communication Manager and CVE-2026-89126 in SAP Web Dispatcher — are collectively tracked as ICM2SHELL and described as turning routine web traffic into an ingress point for remote code execution against the component that faces the network by design. CVE-2026-89103, tracked as PROXYGATE, is a broken-access-control issue in SAP Gateway that lets an unauthenticated external attacker convince the gateway they are an internal application server, achieving full control over the installation and direct exposure of business data and transaction integrity. The fourth maximum-severity flaw, CVE-2026-120017, is a missing-authentication issue in NetWeaver's SAML2 Service Provider. Onapsis found and reported three of the four.

Fireworks AI discloses two October breaches exposing customer billing data and AI datasets

AI infrastructure company Fireworks confirmed two separate October intrusions, saying in an updated notice (Monday) that a well-known, financially motivated cybercriminal group breached systems initially flagged on 6 October, stealing account and billing information, secrets and "AI-specific data" affecting a limited number of customer accounts, all of whom were notified. A second incident began on 23 October when cybercriminals attacked a third-party cloud provider hosting Fireworks' R&D machines, then used that access on 24 October to reach Fireworks development systems and run an unauthorised workload exposing internal data, billing information, invoices, payment history and datasets. Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 is conducting the investigation. Digital workspace company Notion said it was notified on 2 November, disconnected from Fireworks and disabled its Insights feature after Fireworks reported on 6 November that customers' data may have been accessed. The breach tracks the "LLMJacking" pattern documented by Google's Mandiant and Threat Intelligence Group, which reports fraudulent cloud charges exceeding US$100,000 in a single day and cumulative US$1 million.

CISA adds GPUStack, FreeSwitch, OpenSSH and IBM Aspera Faspex flaws to known-exploited catalogue

CISA added four vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 9 November, citing evidence of active exploitation: CVE-2026-102895 (CVSS 9.8), an improper-authentication flaw in GPUStack — an open-source AI inference platform — allowing unauthenticated administrative access, persistent administrator API-key creation, disclosure of worker and cloud-provider secrets and code execution through the inference-backends endpoint; CVE-2026-116779 (CVSS 9.8), a code injection vulnerability in SignalWire FreeSwitch enabling configuration and credential disclosure, arbitrary call control and command execution; CVE-2026-123218 (CVSS 9.8), an incorrect-authorisation flaw in IBM Aspera Faspex letting an authenticated user escalate privileges; and CVE-2026-123158 (CVSS 8.1), the OpenSSH "SSHole" authentication-bypass issue on musl-based Linux. Security firm Previdian counted 22 exploitation attempts against SSHole from 14 unique IP addresses since 6 November. GPUStack was fixed in July, FreeSwitch in October and the OpenSSH and IBM issues in November 2026; federal agencies face binding deadlines to remediate.

Trump nominates Palantir CIO Michael LoSapio to lead CISA

President Donald Trump nominated Michael LoSapio, chief information officer of Palantir, to lead the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, the first Senate-confirmable pick for the top civilian cyber defence position since Sean Plankey withdrew earlier this year and Nick Andersen began serving in an acting capacity in February. The move lands as CISA has lost more than 1,000 personnel — a third of its workforce — and had its budget cut by billions during the second Trump administration, and a week after the agency operated largely behind the scenes ahead of the midterm elections, providing voluntary vulnerability scanning and running its Incident Coordination Group as a threat-sharing hub. Whether the Senate takes up the nomination in the jam-packed lame-duck session is uncertain; if it lapses, Trump would need to renominate LoSapio next year before an incoming Democratic majority, with oversight expected from Representative Bennie Thompson's House Homeland Security Committee. LoSapio joined Palantir in 2017, leading infrastructure and network security before becoming CIO in late 2024.

ESET uncovers Turla's Cereal toolkit intercepting NATO and EU correspondence on government Exchange servers

ESET researchers published analysis of a previously unknown toolset, Cereal, used by the Russia-linked Turla espionage group (also known as Snake, Secret Blizzard and Waterbug) to intercept email on Microsoft Exchange servers at government organisations. First found in November 2025 on a government email server in Kazakhstan, the toolkit later appeared at a Romanian government organisation compromised on 23 December 2025, where it was configured to selectively collect messages exchanged with NATO and EU institutions and those carrying .DOC, .DOCX and .PDF attachments matching sender, recipient and subject criteria. A component called CerealBox is injected into Exchange's _EdgeTransport.exe_ process to inspect email without registering a new transport agent, while CerealNeuron awaits encrypted web requests on paths resembling legitimate Exchange services and executes disposable, task-specific CerealRunner binaries entirely in memory. ESET observed no external command-and-control infrastructure: the compromised Exchange server itself serves as the operational hub, a concealment approach used by only a handful of APT groups. Roughly 70% of Turla's targets since 2022 have been former Soviet republics or European states with historical ties to Moscow.

Hearing aid giant Amplifon discloses breach exposing US customers' Social Security numbers and medical information

Milan-based hearing aid company Amplifon, which operates across 28 countries, began notifying US state regulators and customers of a cyberattack first detected on 11 February, with investigators concluding hackers had access to company files between 18 January and 8 February. Notifications to Texas officials name 85,804 affected individuals and Vermont a further 1,027, and the company filed a breach notice in California; several class-action law firms have claimed the true toll is 1,332,826 people, a figure Amplifon has not confirmed. Stolen data includes names, driver's licence and state ID numbers, medical information, health insurance data and financial information. No hacking group has claimed responsibility for the incident as of this week, and the healthcare-industry pattern it exemplifies — device and wearable makers repeatedly breached through 2026, including iRhythm's 8.18 million-person incident last month — continues unabated. Amplifon was founded in 1950 for World War II veterans and expanded to cover the US, Asia and Africa.

Sutherland Healthcare Solutions and Hoag Memorial Hospital Presbyterian confirm cyber incidents

Two US healthcare entities confirmed separate incidents this week. Sutherland Healthcare Solutions, a revenue cycle management and medical coding business associate servicing payers, providers and MedTech companies, identified an incident spanning 29 July 2024 to 10 July 2026, in which an employee engaged an unauthorised individual to conduct work on their behalf, accessing names, addresses, dates of birth, health plan information, Social Security numbers, claims data and plan members' stated gender. Sutherland said the access did not appear malicious and no misuse has been identified, terminated the access and began mailing notification letters, with affected-headcount numbers yet to be published and the incident not yet on the HHS Office for Civil Rights breach portal. Hoag Memorial Hospital Presbyterian, a nonprofit hospital network serving Orange County, California, detected suspicious network activity on 8 October and, after engaging external forensics, confirmed unauthorised access with potential theft of patient information. Both organisations are offering affected individuals identity protection services.

Vanderbilt University hit by BYOD ransomware attack with theft of student and research data claimed

Vanderbilt University confirmed Monday it is responding to an active cybersecurity incident affecting "university devices, systems, and cloud-based services", instructing students, faculty and staff to shut down university devices, disconnect from university Wi-Fi and watch for signs of compromise. The BYOD ransomware gang — a group that launched in 2026 and currently lists five other organisations on its leak site, having previously claimed the University of New Mexico Health Sciences Center — claimed the attack, alleging it encrypted Vanderbilt's databases and stole sensitive data including what it claims are more than 1 million Social Security numbers, full names, email addresses, phone numbers, student and employee records, research data and domain controller access. BleepingComputer received a photograph of an alleged Vanderbilt computer screen showing a BYOD ransom note ("README_BYOD_31.txt") and video of an infected device playing an audio threat, and confirmed the note's links point to the BYOD-operated site — but the university itself has not confirmed any data exfiltration or encryption. The claim is treated as probable pending Vanderbilt's own disclosure.

York University breach exposed personal information from online forms dating to 2007

Toronto's York University confirmed that a threat actor breached its systems and accessed personal information submitted through online forms on 14 university websites at various times between 2007 and 2026, with the unauthorised access discovered on 25 October. The university's notification lists the exposed data by category: email addresses, phone numbers, dates of birth, York identification numbers, government ID information, third-party contact information, health information, academic and employment history, financial status, criminal background, demographic details and submitted photos — though most stolen records contained basic contact information and York ID numbers, with the mix varying per individual. York said the forms are now secure, will directly notify individuals whose government ID or financial account information was exposed, and is offering two years of credit monitoring and identity theft protection. The university, which serves more than 55,700 students with 7,000 staff, has not specified how many people are affected and no ransomware group has claimed the attack. Contact information exposed alongside sensitive data raises the risk of targeted phishing and social engineering.

Binance warns of iOS sandbox escape that could expose crypto wallet secrets

Crypto exchange Binance warned that a vulnerability it dubs "worse_query" affecting iOS 26.7.1, 27.0.1 and 27.2 allows malicious apps to read local files stored by other applications, putting cryptocurrency seed phrases, private keys and login credentials at risk across the entire app ecosystem, not just Binance's own app. The technique is a publicly documented iOS sandbox escape; a proof-of-concept tool called WQ Files published yesterday uses it to browse, export, edit and delete other apps' data without a jailbreak, and could be distributed as a sideloaded app bypassing the App Store. Binance said no cryptocurrency losses have been confirmed but urges users to delete unnecessary apps, update iOS immediately, and — where compromise is suspected — transfer funds to a new wallet created on a clean device and reset all active login sessions. Apple's 9 November security updates (iOS 27.2.1, 26.7.3, 18.7.12) address nine flaws including CVE-2026-113963, which allows an app to access sensitive user data, though it is unclear whether that fix covers the same issue Binance is warning about.

1

Medical transcription firm PJ&A to pay US$10 million to resolve HIPAA and state-law violations

A US$10 million settlement resolves a multi-state investigation into Perry Johnson & Associates (PJ&A) and four related companies over violations of the HIPAA Security Rule and state laws following the medical transcription provider's March 2023 breach of approximately 13.3 million individuals' electronic protected health information. Under the agreement led by New York Attorney General Letitia James with Connecticut, New Jersey and Michigan, PJ&A must pay US$300,000 now, with the remaining US$9.7 million suspended unless the companies fail to meet the settlement's data-security obligations, which require them to adopt and maintain robust security policies and practices. Investigators found the attacker first accessed PJ&A's network on 27 March 2023 using a compromised employee credential, exfiltrated database files containing names, addresses, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, diagnoses, health insurance information and transcription contents (including lab results and medications) between 7 and 19 April, and leaked the data after a ransom demand went unpaid. The incident was detected on 2 May 2023 when hackers began contacting employees directly.

1

Default 'admin:admin' password lets researchers take over two automakers' car-controlling apps

Security researchers Shubham Shah and Sam Curry demonstrated that the login systems behind two car brands' mobile apps could be completely taken over — including accessing any customer's account and, for owners of connected cars, unlocking doors and starting the engine — because both brands' ForgeRock identity platforms (now part of Ping Identity) ran the OpenIDM component with its built-in administrator console still protected by the documented default credentials: username "admin", password "admin". The console was blocked from the open internet, but the researchers found an edge/server disparity in how the web address was parsed — encoded slashes and extra characters let the edge route the request as a harmless static file while the backend treated it as the path to the console. From the console they used a built-in scheduler to run scripts on the identity server, reaching the scheduler that executes code via the admin tier. The pair tested the full attack only on their own and consenting owners' cars in the United States in February 2026, and both systems were fixed about two hours after disclosure.

Both are links: a CVE opens the ATT&CK matrix or its wiki page, an actor opens its wiki page.
