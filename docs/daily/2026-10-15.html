🛡️ Cyber Digest — 2026-10-15 (Daily)

A daily roundup of key cybersecurity developments across sectors

📋 Executive Summary

Paragraph 1 — Top Stories: The defining story of the day is the chained exploitation of the IVanti/Connect Secure zero-day wave. Volexity documents an attacker chaining an authentication bypass with a remote code execution flaw and a still-unpatched privilege escalation to reach root on fully patched Ivanti Connect Secure VPN appliances, then harvesting Active Directory credentials, LAPS passwords and BitLocker recovery keys before stripping its own traces from Ivanti and Linux audit logs. CISA added CVE-2026-104935 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 13 October, with the relevant disclosure being Volexity's own post-exploitation write-up; only two of the three chained flaws have fixes, and the privilege escalation remains unpatched — making it the top patch-first priority across Australian perimeter estates. Alongside it, the largest Patch Tuesday on record: Microsoft fixed 1,413 flaws including three zero-days, one an actively exploited Windows Shell RCE flaw added to CISA's KEV. Docker's "GhostPAT" Hub authentication flaw (CVE-2026-105445) let a reassigned personal access token authenticate as the target user to pull, push or delete container images; Ledger asked resellers across Asia to halt hardware wallet sales over a suspected supply-chain attack; US authorities shut down 40+ "laptop farms" and charged fourteen in the North Korean remote-IT-worker fraud scheme (FAMOUS CHOLLIMA); Microsoft disrupted phishing-kit operators behind ~1 billion phishing messages; and a ~24-package RubyGems crypto/web3 supply-chain cluster was verified as malicious alongside a fresh tea.xyz-linked npm autopublish wave. Verification: Verified

Paragraph 2 — Australian & New Zealand Context: ACSC's 14 October alert "Protecting operational technology and edge devices" is the anchor for Australia, materially relevant because the IVanti Connect Secure chain is widely deployed on Australian government and enterprise perimeter estates, remediation is incomplete (the privilege escalation is unpatched), and CISA's KEV addition makes patch-first action urgent under ASD guidance. No fresh ACSC advisory outranks it in the 48-hour window; the combined 9 October ACSC/ASD advisory on Chinese government-linked actors combining automated and hands-on hacking to steal sensitive data remains the primary guidance. The Docker "GhostPAT" PAT-reassignment design flaw, the npm Trusted Publishing consent gap and the curl HTTP/2 RCE are all supply-chain items with direct Australian developer relevance. For New Zealand, no fresh Privacy Commissioner notification surfaced in the 48-hour window (the newest listing remains an overseas-parented organisation notification), NZ Police cybercrime feeds were quiet, and CERT NZ remains unreachable as documented; emergent NZ risk is limited given the quiet regional window. Verification: Verified

Sector Reports

🌐 Global (Macro)

1. Volexity tracks chained exploitation of Ivanti Connect Secure zero-days to reach root, steal AD credentials — privilege escalation still unpatched - Summary: An attacker chained an authentication bypass with remote code execution (CVE-2026-104935, patched in the 13 October release) and a still-unpatched privilege escalation to reach root on fully patched Ivanti Connect Secure appliances, then stole Active Directory credentials, LAPS passwords and BitLocker recovery keys while stripping its own traces from Ivanti and Linux audit logs. CISA added CVE-2026-104935 to its KEV catalogue on 13 October; because only two of the three chained flaws are fixed, organisations running Connect Secure should treat appliances as compromised if lateral movement is suspected and patch immediately. Verification: Verified - Source: Volexity and The Hacker News - Reliability: Tier 1/4 — Very High - Date: 2026-10-13

2. Microsoft's largest Patch Tuesday on record: 1,413 flaws, three zero-days, actively exploited Windows Shell RCE added to KEV - Summary: Microsoft's record October Patch Tuesday addressed 1,413 vulnerabilities including three zero-days, one an actively exploited Windows Shell remote code execution flaw added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue. Administrators should treat the shell flaw as actively targeted and apply the update ahead of routine patch cadence; the scale makes ASCII patch-priority guidance the primary take-away for Australian estates. Verification: Verified - Source: The Hacker News and BleepingComputer - Reliability: Tier 2/4 — High - Date: 2026-10-13

3. Docker Hub 'GhostPAT' flaw lets reassigned PATs impersonate users and tamper with images (CVE-2026-105445) - Summary: Docker disclosed a critical Docker Hub authentication flaw (CVE-2026-105445) that let an attacker create a personal access token, convert an account to an organisation, name the target user as owner, and reassign the PAT to a new identity without changing the secret — authenticating as the victim to pull, push or delete container images in the target's organisations. Docker rotated affected PATs and recommends rotating any token tied to account-to-organisation transfers. A registry PAT-identity and consent design gap with Australian container-orchestration relevance. Verification: Verified - Source: Docker Blog and BleepingComputer - Reliability: Tier 1/4 — Very High - Date: 2026-10-13

4. Ledger asks resellers across Asia to halt hardware-wallet sales after suspected supply-chain attack - Summary: Ledger asked resellers across Asia to pause hardware wallet sales after a suspected supply-chain attack on some in-transit units, urging customers to purchase only through official stores and verify authenticity at first use. The suspicion matches the week's broader PAT-reassignment and registry-identity thread and carries crypto-hardware supply-chain relevance for AU/NZ consumers. Verification: Reported - Source: BleepingComputer - Reliability: Tier 2/4 — High - Date: 2026-10-14

5. US DOJ shuts down 40+ 'laptop farms' and charges 14 in North Korean remote-IT worker fraud scheme (FAMOUS CHOLLIMA) - Summary: The US Department of Justice, with CrowdStrike, charged 14 individuals and dismantled more than 40 'laptop farms' that enabled North Korean remote-IT worker fraud, in which DPRK-linked actors posing as US-based IT contractors infiltrated companies to steal data and generate revenue for the regime. The takedown follows the CrowdStrike/FBI FAMOUS CHOLLIMA disruption pattern. Australian enterprises should revalidate remote-hiring identity processes. Verification: Verified - Source: The Hacker News and CrowdStrike - Reliability: Tier 2/4 — High - Date: 2026-10-14

6. Microsoft disrupts Chinese-language phishing-kit ecosystem behind ~1 billion phishing messages; ~24-package RubyGems crypto/web3 supply-chain cluster verified malicious - Summary: Microsoft took down a network of Chinese-language phishing-kit operators estimated to have generated around one billion phishing messages, one of the largest takedowns of the year. Separately, the Open Source Malware project verified a cluster of ~24 malicious RubyGems packages (seed-phrase validator and wallet-recovery) that steal cryptocurrency seed phrases on install — part of the tea.xyz-linked npm autopublish supply-chain wave. Verification: Verified - Source: The Record and Open Source Malware - Reliability: Tier 2/4 — High - Date: 2026-10-14

1. Bank of America fined $1.2bn for 'egregious' AML deficiencies; UK sanctions Cryptomus for Russian money laundering - Summary: US regulators finalised a roughly $1.2 billion penalty against Bank of America over long-running AML and sanctions-compliance failures tied to its transaction-monitoring programme, in a settlement that includes remediation commitments and external compliance testing. Separately, the UK sanctioned crypto exchange Cryptomus for laundering funds tied to Russian sanctions evasion. The pair reinforce that financial institutions' compliance programmes — not just perimeter security — are in the enforcement crosshairs, alongside the Docker PAT and npm Trusted Publishing supply-chain pressure on token rotation across outsourced financial estates. BoE and IMF warn cyber disruption to systemically important banks is a financial-stability risk - Summary: The Bank of England and the IMF jointly warned that an unresolved cyber attack on a systemically important financial institution or core market infrastructure could cascade into financial stability risk, urging banks to plan for severe prolonged disruption rather than treat cyber as a resilience add-on. The warning stems from reliance on a small number of cloud, payments and market-data third parties — concentration Australian and NZ banks share. Verification: Verified - Source: Finextra - Reliability: Tier 2/4 — High - Date: 2026-10-14

3. 'Hirudo' Android banking trojan replaces banking apps with trojanised clones to defeat integrity checks - Summary: Researchers documented 'Hirudo', a new Android banking trojan distributed outside official app stores that installs modified clones of legitimate banking applications in place of the originals, defeating platform anti-fraud and application-integrity checks. By sideloading lookalike apps that pass Play Protect checks, the trojan captures credentials and one-time codes as victims log in. For AU/NZ, banks' device-attestation and app-integrity layers are active evasion targets. Verification: Reported - Source: BleepingComputer - Reliability: Tier 2/4 — High - Date: 2026-10-14
